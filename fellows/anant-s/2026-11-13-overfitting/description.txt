Overfitting, When a Perfect Score Lies

A model scores zero error on its training data. Should you trust it? This film fits sixteen noisy points three ways and grades each on a thousand points it never saw: the perfect score comes in at almost ten thousand on test.

Then two simple stories bend: more data tames the same model, and a score you used to choose the model is no longer an honest score. Every number on screen comes from a short numpy script that was run for this episode.

THE PROMPT FROM THIS EPISODE

Using only Python and NumPy, draw 16 points from y = sin(pi x) on the interval from -1 to 1, with Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.2, plus 1000 more as a test set. Fit polynomials of degree 0 to 15 and print the training and test error for each. Repeat with 200 training points. Then, from the printed numbers only, tell me which degree you would ship in each case, and how you chose it.

Background: T. Hastie, R. Tibshirani, J. Friedman, "The Elements of Statistical Learning", 2nd ed. (Springer, 2009), §7.2–7.3 and §7.10; G. C. Cawley, N. L. C. Talbot (2010), "On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation", JMLR 11.
