Bayes' Theorem: The Positive That's Usually Wrong

A test catches 99% of the people who have a disease, and yours comes back positive. If the disease is rare, the chance you are sick is about 1 in 6, not 99%.

This film counts it out on 10,000 people, builds Bayes' theorem from the counts, and shows how much the answer depends on one number: the base rate.

THE PROMPT FROM THIS EPISODE

A test catches 99% of people who have a disease and wrongly flags 5% of healthy people. One person in 100 has the disease. Someone tests positive, then takes the same test again and tests positive a second time. Before calculating, guess the chance they are sick. Then count it out for 1,000,000 people, show the counts after each test, and say what you had to assume about the two tests.

Background: T. Bayes and R. Price, "An Essay towards Solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances" (1763); G. Gigerenzer and U. Hoffrage, "How to improve Bayesian reasoning without instruction: Frequency formats" (Psychological Review, 1995).
