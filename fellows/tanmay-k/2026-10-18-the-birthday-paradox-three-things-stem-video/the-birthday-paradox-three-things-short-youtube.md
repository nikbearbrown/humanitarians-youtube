Take Away Days: why 23 people is a coin flip

Put 23 people in a room and the odds that two share a birthday are about even. Keep the group at 23, take
away days, and watch the odds jump: 95% with 90 days to choose from, almost certain with 30. It isn't the
people and it isn't uneven birthdays. It's how many days there are, and the square root of that number is
about how many people it takes. A year needs 23. A month needs 7.

What this Short claims, and what it does not:

- With 23 people and every day equally likely, the chance that at least two share a birthday is 50.7%.
- For 23 people born within the same 90 days it is 95.4%; within the same 30 days, above 99.99%. The windows
  are a calculation, not a claim about any real group, and the probabilities are for random groups of 23,
  not for the dots shown (the dots are an illustration, not data).
- Real, uneven US birthdays move the 50.7% by at most about a tenth of a point.
- The rule, people for even odds is about round(1.2 times the square root of the days plus a half), matches the
  exact answer for 365 days (23 people) and for 30 days (7 people), and is never more than one person off for
  any number of equally likely days from 2 to 2,000. It assumes every day is equally likely.
