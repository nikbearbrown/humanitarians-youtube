Which of Gardner's Eight Intelligences Can a Machine Do?

A Humanitarians AI essay says Howard Gardner never had to ask which of his intelligences a
machine might take, because in 1983 technology "was not yet a serious competitor to any of them."
So I went and read what Gardner wrote after 1983. He did ask. And in a 2024 paper, he and two
colleagues answered, one intelligence at a time.

Think of his theory as a house with eight rooms. This video walks through it room by room, and a
light only comes on when Gardner's own writing says a machine does what that room is for. By the
end you'll know the count, why the last two rooms are the ones worth arguing about, and why the
count itself came from a different inspection than the one the house was built with.

What this video claims, and what it does not:

- Multiple-intelligences theory is Gardner's floor plan, not settled science. His own team's
  handbook chapter says it would seem to be at odds with a single general ability ("g"), and notes
  how few studies were designed to test it as a whole. The video says so before the first room.
- The six lights are the 2024 paper's own verdicts, in its own words. The paper does not score the
  six symptoms one by one for machines; it says computational systems "exhibit a reasonable
  sample." Reading that as a performance test rather than the 1983 criteria is my reading, and the
  video says so.
- Room seven's door "ajar" is the video's framing: the paper lists evidence, then says "one should be
  more cautious," and doesn't say why. Room eight is dark because, on the authors' reading, the
  question doesn't apply ("category error"), not because a machine failed it.
- One correction to the essay: Gardner's 2024 paper says programs at the 1956 Dartmouth meeting
  already "carried out mathematical and logical operations which, if carried out by human beings,
  might have been considered intelligent." So the logic light may have been on before the house was
  built. What was "by no means clear" then was conversation and games of strategy.
