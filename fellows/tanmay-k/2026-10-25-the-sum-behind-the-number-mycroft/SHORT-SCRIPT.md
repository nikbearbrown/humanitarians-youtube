# SHORT SCRIPT: The Sum Behind the Number, as a trailer (9:16), draft 2

**Working title:** *Minus Seventy-Eight* (the long film is *The Sum Behind the Number: Taking Apart a Stress Test*)
**Supersedes:** draft 1 (`SHORT-SCRIPT-v1.md`). Draft 2 answers the note that the Short must carry its context and not feel
rushed: 8 lines instead of 6, 235 words instead of 171, and the arithmetic spread over three lines instead of two.
**Role:** a trailer that stands on its own. A viewer who never sees the long film gets what a stress test is, what Mycroft is,
the list of shocks, the sum, the check, the question, and a closed loop.
**Written fresh for the Short.** None of the long film's beats are reused or cut down.
**Voice:** Kokoro `am_onyx`, unhurried. The number is the first thing said; the name rides in the answer (S02); the title is
spoken once, at the end. Numbers in words. No sentence under five words. Positive framing throughout.
**Content rules:** no political content, no real holdings, no third-party media. Every portfolio is invented; the tickers
are the workflow's own; the numbers are scenario arithmetic, not predictions; not investment advice (said once, S02).

## The shape: ONE BAR THAT BUILDS

One scene, never cut: a single stack of blocks. It starts as the headline number alone, defines a scenario as a list, builds
the first holding's bar block by block, builds the other two beside it, weighs them, and returns to the headline number with
its parts standing underneath. The loop closes on the opening object, now explained.

## Lines

### S01 · THE NUMBER · ~9 s (tone: dry, direct)
> A stress test says an invented portfolio of three chip stocks would lose seventy-eight percent in a tech crash. Where does a number like that come from?

*On screen:* from frame one, the headline "Where does −78% come from?" and the number "−78%" at 260 px; the three holdings as faint chips; an empty stack outline pulses beside the number; the first block drifts in.
*Source:* "Invented portfolio · scenario arithmetic, not a prediction."

### S02 · WHAT THIS IS · ~12 s (tone: amused, warm)
> I'm Tanmay Kulkarni, for Humanitarians AI. This week I contributed to Mycroft, an open repository of finance pipelines. The stress test is one of them, and the portfolio is invented, not investment advice.

*On screen:* headline "It's a sum"; the number shrinks to the top; a name line fades in ("Tanmay Kulkarni · Humanitarians AI"); a label "Mycroft · open repository of finance pipelines"; then "invented portfolio · not investment advice".
*Source:* "Mycroft · Scenario Stress Testing Agent · PR #74."

### S03 · A SCENARIO IS A LIST · ~15 s (tone: curious, concrete)
> A scenario is a short list of named shocks, each one a percentage hit to part of the market. This tech crash names four price shocks: the whole market, minus fifteen percent; semiconductors, minus thirty-five; AI stocks, minus forty; and technology, minus thirty.

*On screen:* headline "A scenario is a list"; a card of named shocks appears, one row per phrase: market −15%, semiconductors −35%, AI stocks −40%, technology −30%; a caption "the workflow's own template".
*Source:* "The workflow's own template (Tech Sector Crash)."

### S04 · ONE HOLDING · ~13 s (tone: steady, building)
> The engine's rule is to add up the shocks that belong to each holding. The first chip stock belongs to the market, to semiconductors, and to the AI list. Fifteen plus thirty-five plus forty comes to minus ninety.

*On screen:* headline "One holding, three blocks"; a bar outline for the first holding; blocks land one per phrase (market −15, semiconductors −35, AI list −40) with the running total −15, −50, −90; a badge "the README prints this sum".
*Source:* "c01 · stress-results.json; the workflow's README worked example."

### S05 · THE OTHER TWO · ~9 s (tone: quiet, clear)
> A second chip stock gets the same ninety. The third is off the AI list, so it gets fifty, and that is a different sum.

*On screen:* headline "Same crash, different sums"; the second bar builds to −90; the third bar builds to −50 and a label "off the AI list" marks it.
*Source:* "c01 · stress-results.json (all_positions)."

### S06 · THE WHOLE BOOK · ~8 s (tone: patient, plain)
> Now give each its share of the book: forty percent, thirty, and thirty. Thirty-six plus twenty-seven plus fifteen makes seventy-eight.

*On screen:* headline "Add the shares"; shares 40 / 30 / 30 appear under the bars; the arithmetic types in, one term per phrase: 36, then + 27, then + 15, then = 78; the number from the first frame is lit.
*Source:* "c01 · stress-results.json."

### S07 · THE CHECK · ~7 s (tone: quietly pleased)
> I wrote a Python copy of the adder and ran it beside the original code. On twenty-four requests, every number matched.

*On screen:* headline "Run beside the original"; two columns of results appear and align row by row; the counter climbs to "24 of 24".
*Source:* "parity-results.json · 24 of 24 identical."

### S08 · ASK WHAT WAS ADDED · ~10 s (tone: brisk, handing over)
> So next time a stress test gives you one number, ask what was added. The full film is "The Sum Behind the Number: Taking Apart a Stress Test".

*On screen:* headline "Ask what was added"; the number returns to full size with the three bars standing under it (the opening state, explained); the closing card (the exact film title and "Humanitarians AI") overlays it for about 3 s inside the safe area.
*Source:* "Invented portfolios throughout."
