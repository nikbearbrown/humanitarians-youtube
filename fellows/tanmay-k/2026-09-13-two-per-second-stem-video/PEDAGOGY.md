## Six questions only the read can answer

1. **B02's three rules.** Do they land as a rule you could actually reach for, or as three sentences that blur into each other? This is the beat that earns the reusable-rubric claim; if it blurs, the claim is false.

2. **The unit hazard.** Across B03 to B08 the same physical rate is spoken three ways — two hertz, a hundred and twenty beats per minute, a hundred and twenty steps a minute. Do they stay distinguishable by ear, or did you lose track of which frame you were in?

3. **B07's admission.** "I am citing that older figure through the newer paper, not from the original." Does that sound like honesty, or like hedging? If it sounds like hedging it undercuts the film instead of strengthening it.

4. **B11's silence.** Two seconds after "Find it before I say it." Does it read as an invitation, or as a mistake in the edit? Time it with a clock, not by feel.

5. **B12's turn.** "In English. Two words." Does that land on its own, or does the retelling underneath need to be read aloud too for the absence to register?

6. **B15's verdict.** Ninety words in one run. Does it hold, or does it sag before "two words at a time"?
