## What the film argues

Ask people to tap at whatever rate feels natural and the curve peaks near 120 BPM. Measure a
stranger's head as they walk and you find a tuned resonance at two steps per second. The
stressed syllables of an English sentence arrive at about the same rate. Three fields that do
not cite each other, landing on the same number.

The film asks the same three questions of each study — what exactly was measured, how many
people, and what qualifier sits in the original sentence that falls off when the finding gets
retold. Two of the three hold. The third was never the universal claim: the source literature
says the 2 Hz stress beat is a fact about **English**, and that qualifier disappears in the
retelling.

Two claims were cut rather than repaired. A widely repeated figure about speech
intelligibility below 2 Hz is not in the paper it is usually credited to. A lifespan range
for normal walking cadence could not be traced to any primary source. Neither appears in the
film, and both cuts are named in the YouTube description rather than dropped quietly.

The 1.95 Hz laboratory figure from Murray, Drought & Kory (1964) is cited **through**
MacDougall & Moore (2005), not read first-hand — the 1964 paper has no abstract in any index.
