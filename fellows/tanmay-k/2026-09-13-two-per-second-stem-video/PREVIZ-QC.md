Frames in [`frames/`](frames/), one PNG per beat, 1920×1080, generated by
[`render_previz.py`](render_previz.py) from the brand palette and bundled fonts in
`brutalist.art/`.

**These are previz, not masters.** The master render is manim/remotion at **3840×2160**.
This pass exists so the legibility plan in `beat_sheet.json` can be judged on the page
before anything is built — every frame draws the artifact its beat promises, with the

### Third pass — the corner card

B02 promised a three-question card that persists and lights up. Seventeen frames were
rendered without it; three carried a footer stage direction describing it instead. Built in
this pass via `corner_card()`, present B03–B15, lit at B04, B08, B11, B12, B14.

**The lit chip is INK, not SPARK.** Terracotta there would have meant two accent moments on
every cross-examination beat. The card is chrome and is treated as chrome.

### Second pass — review feedback

| Frame | Defect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **B03** | Both annotations were drawn inside the plot box, so the resonance curve ran through `peak ~120 BPM = 2 Hz` and `one tap every 500 ms` | Plot lowered; annotations moved above it, with the terracotta rule extended up to meet them |
| **B04** | Same cause, plus the full-height mean rule **cut straight through the scatter it was annotating** | Rule replaced with a callout box (`mean 104.6` / `n = 23, range 54–170`) and a short arrow that stops on the curve, above the dots. A small terracotta tick below the axis keeps the exact position marked without entering the data area |

**Rule for the manim build:** a marker line may not cross the data it annotates. Where a
full-height rule would pass through points, annotate from outside the plot area with a
pointer and an axis tick. B03 keeps its rule deliberately — it passes through the curve at
the apex, which is the thing being marked, and there are no data points to obstruct.

The B10 band error is worth naming plainly: a frame in a film about claims drifting from
their sources was itself drawing numbers that did not match its own labels. It was invisible
in the beat sheet and obvious in the picture. That is the argument for previz.

## Fourth pass — ported to manim at 3840×2160

All 17 beats now exist as 4K slates in [`manim/`](manim/), rendered by
[`scenes.py`](scenes.py) via [`render_slates.py`](render_slates.py). `render_previz.py`
stays the layout reference; `px()`/`py()` map the 1080-tall previz space onto manim units
so the two cannot drift, and font sizes use a measured constant (previz px × 0.60643).

**A slate is not a filled beat.** It is a 4K still for the Gate P read — no motion, no
measured audio. `beat_sheet.json` now reads build state off disk rather than asserting it:
`status: SLATE`, `filled: 0 of 17`, and `gate_p: NOT RUN`.

Two defects hit during the port, both fixed:

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| B09 and B12 failed to render | `Text.get_part_by_text()` does not exist in manim 0.18 — it falls through `__getattr__` and raises a misleading `TypeError` about positional arguments | `glyphs()` helper slicing by character index, with spaces stripped to match how manim indexes Text |
| The terracotta wash behind "in English" / "speculate" was invisible | `set_z_index(-1)` pushed the wash behind the filled white card it sits on | z-index dropped; draw order (card → wash → text) does the work |

**Still outstanding at the slate stage**, and all of it is downstream of the read:

- Motion. The three legibility notes describing motion — card return, scatter return, the
  2 Hz rule redrawn with the speech leg narrowed — are still unimplemented and unverified.
- Kokoro narration, which cannot begin until Gate P is signed.
- Inter renders slightly looser through Pango than through PIL. Cosmetic at 4K, worth one
  look during the read.

## Fifth pass — letter spacing

**Defect:** gaps opening *inside* words on the manim slates — `Tud or-Locke`, `(2 019)`,
`CADE NCE`, `ad ults`, `no t a stroll` — while the real word-spaces squeezed shut. Directly
noticeable, and worst on the citation lines, which is where it does the most damage: a
source line that looks broken reads as a source you didn't check.

**Cause — size-dependent, not a font problem.** manim quantises glyph advances at small
font sizes. Diagnosed by rendering the same string at several sizes: at `font_size=28` Inter
is clean; at `font_size≈16` (where every citation and chrome label sits, FS(26) ≈ 15.8) it
breaks. Ruled out along the way: ligatures (`disable_ligatures` changed nothing),
`MarkupText` (same result), and the typeface itself (Lato and Montserrat break identically
at small sizes).

**Fix.** `label()` now builds every Text at `TEXT_BASE = 200` and scales it down to the
target size, so advances are computed at high precision before scaling. Verified
side by side: direct at 15.8 breaks, base-200-scaled-to-15.8 is clean. All 17 slates
re-rendered; no layout shifted, no new overlaps.

The previz frames never had this — PIL lays out text directly. It appeared only in the
manim port, which is a reason to keep both renderers rather than retiring previz.

Note on B16: **this overlap did not exist in previz.** PIL renders that serif line narrower
than manim does, so the collision only appeared after the port. Coordinates are now
identical in both renderers, but the lesson stands — matching coordinates do not guarantee
matching layout when the text engines differ, so both must be looked at.

One manim trap recorded while fixing B13: passing `""` as an axis label builds an empty
`Text`, which raises `IndexError: too many indices for array` from deep inside the renderer
with no hint at the cause. `axes()` now skips empty labels.

## Seventh pass — the chrome itself

Every previz and manim frame this week carried a beat id and act label in the top-left,
drawn by `chrome()`. Those exist to tell a builder which beat they are looking at. They have
no place in a shipped master, and Week 21's cuts do not have them.

This QC record is where it should have been caught. Six passes here checked collisions,
clipping, band positions, safe areas and accent law — all questions about whether the frame's
content is correct. None asked **which of these elements is content at all.**

Add that question to this file's standing checklist: for each element on the frame, who is it
for? If the answer is "whoever is building this", it does not ship.
