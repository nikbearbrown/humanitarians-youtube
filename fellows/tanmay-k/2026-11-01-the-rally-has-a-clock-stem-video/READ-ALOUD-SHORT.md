**S01 · THE BET** (20 words)

In my tiny Pong, a perfect defender loses on the sixth shot, and nobody makes a mistake. Here is why.

**S02 · THE CONTEXT** (41 words)

Pong is two paddles and one ball. An essay I read says it has no perfect defense, and I wanted to know when that is true. So I built the smallest Pong I could. I'm Tanmay Kulkarni, in for Humanitarians AI.

**S03 · THE COUNT** (21 words)

Watch the rally. Shot one. Shot two. Shot three. Shot four. Shot five. All defended. Shot six. The ball goes past.

**S04 · THE TIME** (24 words)

The defender can see where the ball is going. What it lacks is time, because the ball gets five percent faster on every shot.

**S05 · THE TWO BARS** (41 words)

Two bars explain it. The first is how far the paddle can travel while the ball crosses. The second is how far the worst shot makes it go, about half the field. If the first is longer, a perfect defense exists.

**S06 · THE CLOCK** (46 words)

But the crossing keeps shrinking, so the first bar sinks. On the sixth shot it drops below the line, by about eight pixels. The arithmetic says shot six, and a search that tries every aim agrees. Nobody made a mistake. The rally ran out of time.

**S07 · THE LIMIT** (28 words)

It is only a model, with no spin and no real machine. But it shows that no perfect defense is true here only because the ball speeds up.

**S08 · THE FILM** (18 words)

The full film is called The Rally Has a Clock: When Is No Perfect Defense True in Pong?
