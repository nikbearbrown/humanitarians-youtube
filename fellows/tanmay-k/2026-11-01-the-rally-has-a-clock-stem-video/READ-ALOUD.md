**B03 · WHAT THE DEFENDER KNOWS** (~21 s, 74 words)

The moment the ball leaves a paddle, its direction is fixed. The defender can see exactly where it is going. What the defender does not have is time. The ball has to cross the whole field, and that crossing is all the time there is. Keep an eye on the two bars in the corner. One is how far the paddle can travel while the ball crosses. The other is how far it must.

**B04 · THE WORST SHOT** (~17 s, 61 words)

Now put yourself in the attacker's place. You can aim anywhere. Every aim gives a different landing spot, and together they cover the whole height of the field. The defender cannot know the aim in advance, so the best place to wait is the middle. Then the worst shot is the one that lands at an edge, half the field away.

**B05 · THE LINE** (~21 s, 75 words)

So the whole question shrinks to one comparison. In the time the ball takes to cross, can the paddle cover about half the field, minus the paddle's own reach? If it can, a perfect defense exists. If it can't, none does. Here, the paddle needs a little over a quarter of the ball's sideways speed. Keep the form, because it works on any field: the defender's reach in one flight, against half the free height.
