A battery can look full and still reset your device #Shorts

A device resets, and its coin cell can still be most of the way full. If the charge did not run out, what did? Inside a coin cell is a three volt source and a small resistor, near ten ohms when fresh. The bigger the burst of current the device asks for, the more voltage is lost through that resistor. Give the device a reset line, a voltage it cannot go under (I chose two volts), and the cell has a limit: the biggest burst it can pass, about a hundred milliamps when fresh. Then use the cell, and the resistor grows, from ten ohms to about sixty-five in Energizer's plot, so the limit falls faster than the charge does. Two asks on the same cell: eight milliamps never meets the line and uses the whole cell; sixty milliamps meets it with the tank nearly half full, and the device resets with 48 percent left. A cell can die rich, and how rich depends on what you ask of it.

What this Short claims, and what it does not:
- It is a model fitted to graphs I read off two data sheets (Energizer's coin handbook and Duracell's CR2032 datasheet), and I tested no real cell. The numbers are "about".
- The two-volt reset line is my own choice, so the shape is the claim, not the exact percentages. Cold and a capacitor across the cell are not modelled.
