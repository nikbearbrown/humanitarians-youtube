**S00 · THE CRASH** (~10 s)

A device resets, and its battery can still be most of the way full. If the charge did not run out, then what did?

**S01 · WHAT IS INSIDE** (~23 s)

Here is what is inside. A coin cell is a three volt source with a small resistor hidden in it. Energizer says a fresh one is near ten ohms. When the device asks for a burst of current, some voltage is lost pushing it through that resistor. The bigger the burst, the more you lose.

**S02 · SETUP** (~17 s)

I'm Tanmay Kulkarni, in for Humanitarians AI. I did not open a real battery. I built a model of a coin cell from two manufacturers' published graphs, and I ask it one thing: how much is left when the device resets?

**S03 · THE LINE** (~21 s)

Every device has a reset line, a voltage it cannot go under, and I chose two volts. That gives the cell a limit, the biggest burst it can pass. Fresh, that is about a hundred milliamps. Remember this picture: the tank is the charge left, and the line is that limit.

**S04 · THE LINE MOVES** (~21 s)

Now use the cell, and the resistor grows. Energizer's plot shows it going from ten ohms to about sixty-five. So the line comes down: a hundred milliamps at the start, about forty-three once nearly three quarters is spent, about eight and a half at the end. The tank empties slowly, but the line falls faster.

**S05 · TWO ASKS** (~22 s)

Now let's make two asks. Eight milliamps never meets the line, so the device uses the whole cell. Sixty milliamps meets it with the tank still nearly half full, and the device resets with forty-eight percent left. The same cell, and the same tank. What changed is only how big a burst the device asked for.

**S06 · THE LIMIT** (~22 s)

Let me be plain about what this is. It is a model fitted to graphs I read, and I tested no real cell. Cold would make it worse, and I did not model a capacitor. And my reset line is my own choice, so the shape is the claim, not the exact numbers.

**S07 · CLOSE** (~23 s)

So when something resets with a battery that still looks fine, ask how big its burst is, not how much is left. A cell can die rich, and how rich depends on what you ask. The long film adds a third ask, and the sum you can do yourself. It is called The Cell Dies Rich.
