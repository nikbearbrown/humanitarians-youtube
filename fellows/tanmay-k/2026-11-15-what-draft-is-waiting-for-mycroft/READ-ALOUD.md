# Read-aloud: What Draft Is Waiting For: Reading a Status as a Claim

Long film, 16:9. 787 words, about 4.0 minutes at 3.3 words a second. Starts with Tanmay's introduction and ends with the sign-off. Numbers are in words; the name is spelled the way it will be said. Tone is shown after each beat title; it is not read aloud. SIGNED OFF (Gate P) by Tanmay, 2026-11-17.

**B00 Cold open** (*curious, a little puzzled*)

Three of my last four films said, near the end, that the recipe was a draft. I said it, I moved on, and I never said what it was waiting for. This week I went to find out.

**B01 Introduction** (*warm, plain*)

Hi, this is Tanmay Kulkarni, for Humanitarians AI. This week I contributed to Mycroft, a public repository in which each piece of work is a recipe: something an agent runs and a person reads. The pull request I opened has been merged, and it adds two steps to one recipe. But what I want to look at is smaller: the one word above everything else in every recipe, and what it would take to change it.

**B02 The ladder** (*clear, steady*)

That word is a status, and the statuses form a ladder with five rungs. Draft, specified, runnable on sample data, runnable live, verified. The repository's rule is short. A status is a claim, and a claim needs evidence written down. Change the word without the evidence and you have not climbed. You have only typed.

**B03 The first rung** (*explaining, concrete*)

So what does each rung ask? From draft to specified: no open to-do items, and every to-do item that was closed carries its proof. My stress-test recipe has six steps. Each has a closing line, with a date, saying the script exists and has been run. That leaves zero open to-do items. By that test, the next rung looks within reach. I have not climbed it, and I will come back to why.

**B04 The rung that needs a person** (*surprised, then slower*)

The rung after that is where I expected more code. It asks for a full run on sample data, checks that pass, and audits that were generated and read. Read. By whom? Not a script. A person. It is the single piece of evidence a machine cannot hand you. My contradiction detector climbed to this rung on the fourth of October. Its proof is a folder of six gate records. A gate is a checkpoint where a person has to say yes or no, and each record names who did.

**B05 What the record says** (*careful, pulling back*)

Now hold on. Here is what that rung does not mean. Open one of those records. It says what the decision covers: sample mode, over invented data, and nothing else. It lists the risk that is left over. And it says what voids it: any edit to the recipe or its scripts. A status that can expire is a status that says something.

**B06 It does expire** (*matter-of-fact, a little wry*)

And it does. Those records name version zero point two zero. The recipe has had one small edit since, and sits at zero point two one. By its own rule, those records are due for another look. Yet the word on the recipe has not changed. The record can age while the status stays put. That is where this ladder strains, and it is why the version is written down. A person has to look again. It is on my list.

**B07 Where my five stand** (*proud but measured*)

Here is where my five recipes stand. The contradiction detector is on the third rung. The stress-test recipe has six of six steps built. The price fetcher has five of six, after this week. The what-if recipe has three, and the dashboard has one. None of those numbers is a feeling. Each is a count of scripts that exist and can be run.

**B08 This week's evidence** (*disciplined*)

This week's climb is the price fetcher. The new steps check a set of prices as a set, and write the report a person reads. The evidence is a self-test of twenty-four rows. Each row says what I ran, what I saw, and what I expected. And at the bottom sits the section the repository insists on: what I did not test. No live call, no real market data, and no claim that my three thresholds suit a real portfolio. And step two, which does not exist yet.

**B09 What I left alone** (*calm, matter-of-fact*)

Notice what I left alone: the recipe itself. The rule this week was to add files and edit none. So its status still says draft, and its to-do markers still stand. The stress-test recipe is the same: changing its word takes a record, and I have not written one. The repository's own check even lists the ten rows the recipe will need next. That list is not a worry. It is the next edit, already written out.

**B10 Your turn** (*direct, to the viewer*)

What I take from all this is simple. The next time someone says it works, ask three things. Which rung is that? What is the evidence for that rung? And what did you not test, and what would void it? Try it on your own project's last update: write the rung, the evidence, and one thing you did not test. If you can fill in all three, you have learned something real. If you cannot, you have still learned something.
