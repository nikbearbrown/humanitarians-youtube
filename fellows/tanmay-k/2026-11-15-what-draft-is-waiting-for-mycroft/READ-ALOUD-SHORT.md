# Read-aloud: What Draft Is Waiting For: Reading a Status as a Claim (Short)

Short, 9:16, its own script, one continuous arc. 406 words, about 2.3 minutes at 2.9 words a second (slower than the long film on purpose: the context comes first and each idea gets its own line). Starts with the hook and ends on the long film's exact title. Numbers are in words; the name is spelled the way it will be said. Tone is shown after each line title; it is not read aloud. SIGNED OFF (Gate P) by Tanmay, 2026-11-17.

**S00 Hook** (*curious, unhurried*)

Someone tells you their AI tool works. Fine. But works how, tested on what, checked by whom? In an open repository I contribute to, every recipe carries one word that has to answer those questions: draft, or something higher. What would you have to show to change that word?

**S01 What the repository is** (*plain, slower*)

Mycroft is an open collection of recipes. A recipe is a piece of work written out, step by step, so an agent can carry it out and anyone can check it. The split matters: the agent does the doing, and a person does the deciding.

**S02 Who I am** (*warm*)

I'm Tanmay Kulkarni, for Humanitarians AI. This week I added two steps to one of those recipes. It made me ask a question I had skipped. What is the word above every recipe actually waiting for?

**S03 The ladder** (*clear, steady*)

That word is a status, and it sits on a ladder with five rungs: draft, specified, runnable on sample data, runnable live, verified. Sample data means invented data, so nothing real can go wrong. The rule is that each rung is a claim, and a claim needs evidence written down.

**S04 What each rung asks** (*steady, a small lift on the discovery*)

The first rung asks for no open to-do items, and every one that was closed has its proof. The next asks for a full run on sample data, checks that pass, and audits that were read. Read, by a person. No script can supply that.

**S05 The record** (*careful*)

One of my recipes has climbed to that rung. Its proof is six decision records, one for each checkpoint, and each names the person who decided. Open one and it says what it covers: sample data only. It lists the risk that is left. And it says what cancels it: any edit to the recipe.

**S06 It expires** (*matter-of-fact*)

That recipe has had one small edit since. So by its own rule, those records are due for another look, even though the word on the recipe has not changed. A status is only as fresh as someone's last look, and that is why the version is written down.

**S07 What I did not test** (*disciplined*)

My own evidence has a section the repository insists on: what I did not test. No live call, no real market data, and one step still unbuilt. A list that is empty is only a longer way of saying it works.

**S08 Your turn** (*direct, then the title*)

So the next time someone says it works, ask three things: which rung, what evidence, and what did they not test? The full film is What Draft Is Waiting For: Reading a Status as a Claim.
