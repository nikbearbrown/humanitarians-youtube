The Sky Switch: why a fast tune gets a yellow sky

Quick, bright music or slow, low music: which one gets the yellow sky? Most listeners say the quick one, in two different countries. A sky doesn't sound like anything, so what are they matching? Feeling. In a Berkeley study, people rated music and colors as happy or sad, strong or weak, and the two sets of ratings lined up very closely. Swap the pairing and it feels wrong, because the two feelings no longer agree.

What this Short claims, and what it does not:

- In that study, 97 listeners (48 in the United States, 49 in Mexico) picked colors for 18 orchestral selections by Bach, Mozart and Brahms. Quick, major music drew lighter, yellower colors; slow, minor music drew darker, bluer ones.
- The emotion ratings of the music and of the chosen colors correlated from 0.89 to 0.99. That is a correlation, not proof that emotion causes the match; the authors say a direct link is not ruled out.
- The Bach and Brahms examples are the paper's own illustration of its hypothesis, not measured picks.
- The test used two countries and Western classical music. The sky, colors and tunes on screen are illustrations made for this Short, not data.
