## 3. The angle

> **Colour never hears the music. Feeling is the go-between.**

The essay's title says colour *controls* emotion. The measurements say something more interesting about how:
a listener matches colour to music **through emotion**, not through any direct link between wavelength and sound.
Happy music and a yellow swatch both read as "happy"; slow minor music and a blue-grey swatch both read as
"sad". Change the feeling and the matched colour changes with it. The 4:1–12:1 scene ratio works on the same
channel: an editor is setting a rhythm of arousal, and colour rides along with it.

**The turn:** the folder's own ratio is a *time* measure, and Lillard & Peterson tested *pacing*, not colour.
The film says so plainly and builds on it: the dial is arousal, and colour is one of several things that
move it.

## 4. Structure: *the film re-colours itself to its own music*

The form is the argument. The film's palette is driven by its own score and narration. A fast major passage
brightens and warms the frame; a slow minor passage cools and darkens it. The viewer is then asked to match,
and the same frame comes back in the opposite mood at the end.

1. **Cold open.** Two eight-second clips of the same cartoon-flat scene: one fast and major, one slow and minor.
   Which colour goes with which? (The viewer picks before being told.)
2. **The bridge.** Palmer 2013: the pattern holds in two countries; emotion ratings carry it.
3. **The infant's side.** Categorise at 6 months, name at 18: the machinery that makes a colour a *category*
   exists before the word does.
4. **The cultural limit.** Green and white flip across regions. The match is learned in part, so it travels
   only so far; this is where the Mexico/US agreement stops being a law and becomes a tendency.
5. **The editor's dial.** The 4:1–12:1 ratio, shown as a strip of scene lengths. Lillard & Peterson, with the
   boundary stated: pacing, immediate, four-year-olds.
6. **Return.** The cold-open pair again, this time with the sound swapped: fast music on the blue-grey frame.

## 5. Boundaries (deliberate)

  harm from any show, and names no specific programme as harmful.
- It does not claim colour causes emotion. It claims listeners match through emotion.
