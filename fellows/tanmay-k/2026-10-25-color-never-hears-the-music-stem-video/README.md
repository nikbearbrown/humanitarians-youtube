## The topic

**Chosen topic: `claude-for-design/the-chromatic-manipulation`** in the `humanitarians-youtube` repository: a short
beat sheet on how color and emotion connect. The film began with an essay we read, and goes to the research on the
music–color link.

- **Selection:** a randomized, unseeded draw over the library's singleton topics (read-only; nothing in the shared
  repo was checked out, pushed or modified). The draw, the rejected draws and the uniqueness checks are in
  `TOPIC-DECISION.md`.
- **Angle:** *color never hears the music; feeling is the go-between.* People match a color to a piece of music
  through emotion, not through any direct link between wavelength and sound (Palmer et al. 2013). The angle and its
  boundaries are in `ANGLE.md`.
- **Method the film teaches:** rate the sound on two feelings (happy–sad, strong–weak), rate each color on the same
  two, and see whether they agree. The two scales are the paper's own emotion dimensions; the "rate, rate, compare"
  routine is our teaching device.

## What the films claim, and what they do not

The full list is in each `*-youtube.md`. In short: the findings are the paper's (97 listeners in the United States and
Mexico, 18 orchestral selections, emotion-rating correlations of 0.89 to 0.99) and are a **correlation**, tested on
two countries and Western classical music. The positions shown for fast + minor music are approximate, read off the
paper's Figure 2. The skies, swatches, tunes and diagrams are illustrations, not data. Three details (the infant
color timeline, color symbolism across regions, and the calm/busy scene-length rule of thumb) are attributed on screen
to the essay we read; `FACTCHECK.md` records which claims have a separate source and which do not.
