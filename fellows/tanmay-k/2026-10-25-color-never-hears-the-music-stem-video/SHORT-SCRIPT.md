# SHORT SCRIPT: Color Never Hears the Music, as a trailer (9:16), draft 3

**Working title:** *The Sky Switch* (the long film is *Color Never Hears the Music: Why a Fast Tune Gets a Yellow Sky*)
**Supersedes:** draft 2 (`SHORT-SCRIPT-v2-12of12.md`). Draft 3 answers the note that the Short must not feel rushed or short of context: 8 lines instead of 6, about 92 s instead of 56, with the missing context added back.
**Role:** a trailer that stands on its own. A viewer who never sees the long film gets the question, the answer, what the study did, why it works, a concrete example, a limit, and a closed loop.
**Written fresh for the Short.** None of the long film's beats are reused or cut down.
**Voice:** Kokoro, same as the long, at an unhurried pace. The first words are the question; the name rides in the answer (S02); the title is spoken once, at the end. Numbers and years in words. No sentence under five words.
**Content rules:** no political content, no real living individuals, no third-party media. Tunes and colors are our own, labelled as illustrations.

## The shape: ONE SKY THAT CHANGES WITH THE TUNE

One scene, never cut: a single landscape with a sky and, from S02 on, a small strip (HAPPY–SAD across, STRONG–WEAK up). A quick bright tune warms the sky to yellow; a slow low tune cools it to blue-grey. The viewer hears both tunes before the question, the study gives the answer, a music dot and a color dot land on the same spot, the paper's own example puts names on it, a swap clashes, and the loop closes on the opening yellow.

## Layout and phone contract (Shorts rubric: legibility)

Canvas 1080×1920 logical (rendered at 2160×3840). Every text element sits inside:
- **vertical:** y 230 to 1440 (12% to 75% of the height). Nothing above or below.
- **horizontal:** x 60 to 900. Nothing at x > 920 in the lower half (button rail).
- **type floor:** every source line **34 px**, every strip label 36 px, key captions 44 px, the headline 56 px. No text below 34 px anywhere.
- **sources:** one line each, in the lowest band of the safe area (y 1340 to 1440), at least 2 s on screen.
- **headline** (y 270) carries the argument in a few words so the Short reads with the sound off; it changes on every line.
- **label stacks** (S03, S07): one line at a time, each at least 1.5 s before the next arrives; never more than three lines.
- **closing:** no end card. The closing text overlays the restored opening scene for about 3 s.

## Pace contract (Shorts rubric: pace)

- **No silence of 1 s or more:** 0.3 s lead at the start, 0.4 s between lines; the tune continues under every gap.
- **A visible change at least every 2.5 s, no static stretch over 3 s.** The schedule below ties each change to a word in the line; line starts are set by the measured audio and the changes are re-checked at 540×960 on the render.
- **Calm, not slow:** about three words a second at Kokoro's natural pace. If the measured read lands above 3.3 words a second, a line is cut, not sped up.

## Lines

### S01 · THE QUESTION · ~10 s (tone: dry, unhurried)
> Two skies, one yellow and one blue-grey. Two bits of music, one quick and bright, one slow and low. Which sky goes with which?

*On screen:* headline "Which sky goes with which?" from frame one over a grey sky. Clouds drift fast under the quick tune (label "A: quick, bright"), then slowly under the slow tune (label "B: slow, low"). Then two swatches appear at the lower edge of the sky, "1 yellow" and "2 blue-grey", in an order that does not match the order played.
*Source:* "Illustration: our tunes, our skies. Not data."

### S02 · MOST PEOPLE AGREE · ~9 s (tone: amused, warm)
> I'm Tanmay Kulkarni, for Humanitarians AI. If you put yellow on the quick one, you chose the way most listeners do.

*On screen:* headline "Most listeners pick yellow for quick". The sky warms to yellow while the quick tune returns; a small ring marks the matched pair. A name line fades in inside the safe area ("Tanmay Kulkarni · Humanitarians AI").
*Source:* "Palmer et al., PNAS 2013: US and Mexico."

### S03 · THE STUDY · ~16 s (tone: curious, concrete)
> Researchers at Berkeley tested this with real music by Bach, Mozart and Brahms. About ninety seven people, in the United States and in Mexico, picked colors for each piece. Quick, major music drew lighter, yellower colors. Slow, minor music drew darker, bluer ones.

*On screen:* headline "A real test, two countries". Labels appear in order: "Bach · Mozart · Brahms", "97 listeners · US + Mexico", "picked colors for each piece". Then "quick + major → lighter, yellower" while the sky is yellow; the tune slows and the sky cools to blue-grey as "slow + minor → darker, bluer" appears.
*Source:* "Palmer, Schloss, Xu & Prado-León, PNAS 110(22), 2013."

### S04 · THE GO-BETWEEN · ~15 s (tone: leaning in, patient)
> But a sky doesn't sound like anything, so they must be matching feeling. Listeners rated the music, and the colors, as happy or sad, strong or weak, and the two sets of ratings lined up very closely.

*On screen:* headline "They match through feeling". The strip comes up (HAPPY–SAD across, STRONG–WEAK up). A music dot and a color dot slide in one after the other and land on the same spot. A small line reads "ratings of music vs. colors: up to 0.99".
*Source:* "Emotion ratings, music vs. chosen colors: 0.89–0.99 (Palmer et al. 2013). Correlation, not proof."

### S05 · THEIR OWN EXAMPLE · ~12 s (tone: steady, concrete)
> The paper gives its own example. Bach's fast dance feels happy and energetic, and fits a saturated yellow. Brahms's slow Adagio feels sad, and fits a dark grey.

*On screen:* headline "Their own example". The dots move to the HAPPY end: label "Bach · fast dance" and a saturated-yellow swatch lands beside the dots; the sky is yellow. Then the dots move to the SAD end: label "Brahms · slow Adagio", a dark-grey swatch lands; the sky cools.
*Source:* "The paper's own example of its idea (Palmer et al. 2013)."

### S06 · THE SWAP · ~10 s (tone: quiet, a little wry)
> Now swap them, and a quick tune under a blue-grey sky feels wrong, because the two feelings no longer agree.

*On screen:* headline "Swap them and it clashes". The quick tune plays over the blue-grey sky; the two dots split apart and the gap is labelled "disagree", pulsing once.
*Source:* "Illustration. Not data."

### S07 · THE LIMIT · ~8 s (tone: honest, plain)
> One limit is that this is a correlation, and the test used only two countries and Western classical music.

*On screen:* headline "Strong, not a law". A three-line label stack appears one line at a time: "correlation", "2 countries", "Western classical only". The sky drifts slowly warmer.
*Source:* "Palmer et al. 2013: unclear for non-Western scales."

### S08 · TRY IT · ~12 s (tone: brisk, handing over)
> To test it yourself, try the quick, minor case. It's in the full film: "Color Never Hears the Music: Why a Fast Tune Gets a Yellow Sky".

*On screen:* headline "Test your own". The sky returns to yellow with the quick tune (the opening state). The closing card (the exact film title and "Humanitarians AI", inside the safe area) comes in on the spoken word "Color" and stays about 4.4 s; in the final 1.0 s it fades out as the sky eases back to the opening grey (as built and measured; the first plan said about 3 s and 0.7 s).
*Source:* "Palmer et al. tested US and Mexico only."
