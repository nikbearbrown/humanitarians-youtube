# READ-ALOUD-SHORT — Reading a Twentieth of a Pixel (Short, 9:16), draft 2

Its own script, one continuous arc on one scene (the pixel row); not a cut of the long film. Numbers in words. Draft 2 slows draft 1 and adds the context it skipped: what a Doppler shift is and how color becomes speed now come before any photon is counted (S01), and the hook is stated as a condition, not a claim about what astronomers achieve. Draft 1: `READ-ALOUD-SHORT-v1.md`. Same sources as the long film (`FACTCHECK.md`).

**S00 · HOOK** (~19 s)

Say you want a galaxy's speed to within one kilometer per second, using only its light. You have to find a line of color to a twentieth of a pixel. The pixel that catches that light is more than twenty times wider. So how can a ruler read a mark that fine?

**S01 · HOW LIGHT GIVES A SPEED** (~18 s)

Here is how light gives you a speed. Hydrogen gives off light at one exact color, six hundred fifty-six point two eight nanometers. If a galaxy moves away from us, that color stretches a little. If it moves toward us, it squeezes. The size of the change is the speed.

**S02 · SETUP** (~15 s)

I'm Tanmay Kulkarni, in for Humanitarians AI. I didn't use a telescope. I built the measurement: a line of hydrogen light, and a row of pixels. Photons fall in one at a time, and all I see is which pixel caught each one.

**S03 · TEN** (~12 s)

Ten photons. I guess where the center of the line is, and I am off by about fifteen kilometers per second. That is most of a pixel. With ten photons, I know almost nothing.

**S04 · THE TURN** (~14 s)

A hundred photons, and my guess steadies. A thousand, and I am off by only one point four, about six percent of a pixel. Notice what did not happen. The pixel never got smaller. The guess got finer.

**S05 · WHY** (~15 s)

Why? Every photon is a small vote for where the center is, and votes average out. The error is the width of the line, divided by the square root of the photons. So a hundred times the light buys ten times the precision.

**S06 · THE LIMIT** (~17 s)

I want to be honest about what this is. It is a simulated line. Add ten times as much sky glow as light from the line, and my error more than doubles. I did not test blended neighbors or instrument error, and I measured nothing on a telescope.

**S07 · CLOSE** (~12 s)

So the next time you see a speed quoted that finely, ask how many photons paid for it. The long film works through the numbers. It is called Reading a Twentieth of a Pixel.
