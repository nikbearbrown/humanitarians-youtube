Why a Coin Flip Beats a Learning Algorithm #Shorts

A puzzle from Sutton and Barto's reinforcement learning textbook: four squares, one that swaps your
buttons, and you can't see where you are. A fair coin beats a classic learning algorithm by more than
three and a half times, and the best coin isn't even fair: 2 − √2, about 58.6%. So why random? Give the
player more information. If the randomness vanishes, it was covering for what the player couldn't see.
If it survives, someone's watching for a pattern.

Try it on a goalkeeper at a penalty, and tell me in the comments: vanish, or survive?
