Deciding What the Channel Is For

I stopped making videos and started organizing the place they live: unlisting the clutter, and sketching what the Medhavy channel should actually be. The interesting question turned out to be: how do you log the beginning of a plan without pretending it’s the finished one?

Heet K. · OPT Contract, Humanitarians.ai · September 21 – 27, 2026

For months this journal has been about individual things: this intro, that explainer, the outro in three formats. This week I zoomed out and worked on the thing that holds all of them — the Medhavy YouTube channel itself.

Two parts. First, housekeeping: I went through the channel and unlisted or removed videos that didn’t belong there anymore — the clutter, the leftovers, the things that shouldn’t be on a public channel we’re trying to build deliberately. Second, and harder, I started figuring out how the channel should actually be structured going forward: what types of videos it should hold, how they fit together, what the shape of the thing is. That second part is barely begun. It’s Phase 1 of getting started, and I want to be careful about that word.

Housekeeping is easy to log; a plan is not

The reorganization is the simple half to report, because it’s a finished action. I unlisted the videos that needed unlisting. That either happened or it didn’t, and it did — done, verifiable, over. Cleaning up is satisfying precisely because it has a clear end state.

The structure work is the half that tempts a dishonest label. When you spend hours thinking about how something should be organized, you end the week with a head full of ideas and the feeling of having figured something out — and it’s easy to write that up as “designed the channel structure,” which sounds like a deliverable. But I didn’t design the channel structure. I started to. This is a first pass, an early sketch, the opening move of a plan that will take several more passes to actually become one. “Phase 1” isn’t a humble-brag or a hedge; it’s the literal status. I’m at the beginning, and the honest report says beginning, not done.

The word “done” doesn’t apply to thinking yet

There’s a specific trap with planning work: thinking feels like progress, and it is, but it doesn’t produce the same kind of artifact that building does. A plan in my head — even a good one — isn’t a plan the project can use until it’s actually worked out, written down, and ideally checked by someone. This week I have the start of one. I don’t have the thing itself.

And this week there’s a second honesty note: nobody reviewed any of this. It was self-directed work, part of my own workflow — I decided the channel needed tidying and structuring and I did it. That’s fine, but it means there’s no external confirmation to point at, and I’m not going to manufacture one. The reorganization is done because I did it and it’s visible. The structure is Phase 1 because that’s genuinely where it is. Neither has anyone else’s stamp on it, and the log says so.

The honest footnote

The usual discipline, applied to organizing rather than making. The channel is reorganized — unnecessary videos unlisted and removed, a completed and verifiable action. The channel structure is Phase 1: a first pass at what the channel should hold, not a finished plan, not reviewed by anyone, explicitly early. Both were done independently as my own workflow.

Seventeen weeks in, I notice the discipline stretches to cover work that isn’t a video at all. The temptation this week wasn’t to overclaim a rendered artifact — it was to let a week of thinking sound more finished than thinking is. So: I tidied the channel, and I started figuring out its shape. The first is done. The second is Phase 1. That’s the whole of it, said at its real size.
