# Frictional log — first videos on the confirmed branding

## 2026-10-18 — the first content the tooling was for

> Written 2026-10-17, within the week it describes, from the Week 20 report.
> The production detail below is reconstructed; no slugs or renders were captured.

- **Evidence:** the [four Shorts](README.md#the-videos), listed in the entry below
- **Branding used:** [professor-confirmed intro/outro](../2026-10-01-intro-outro-brutalist-pass/) (confirmed 11 Oct)
- **This week's other thread:** [collaborative video plan](../2026-10-15-collaborative-video-plan/)
- **Hours:** Week 20 total 22.0 — 10.0 on this thread — [HOURS.md](../HOURS.md)

**What I was working on.** Making a few actual videos for the channel, using the
Brutalist tool and the confirmed intro/outro.

**What I tried, and what I expected.** I expected this to be the easy week. The
branding was confirmed, the skill worked, the content direction was taking
shape — on paper this is just assembly. I expected the bookends to be a solved
problem I could stop thinking about.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**

- **A confirmed sting is a constraint, not just an asset.** Once the intro and
  outro are fixed and approved, the body has to fit between them — their pacing
  sets an expectation the content either meets or fights. I had been treating
  the branding as decoration to be appended. It is closer to a frame that
  dictates the shape of what goes inside.
- **"A few" is doing a lot of work in this report, and I should not let it.**
  I cannot now say with confidence how many or which ones. That is a recording
  failure, not a memory failure — the information existed at the time.
- **Shared, not confirmed.** Same discipline as the plan: these went to the team
  and the professor for input and nothing has come back. I am not reading the
  branding's confirmation as covering the content that wears it.
- **The evidence problem compounded.** Week 18 left the skill unnamed and
  uncommitted. Week 19 added a professor-confirmed deliverable on top of that
  gap. This week adds actual videos on top of both. Each layer is real work
  resting on a foundation nobody outside can inspect.

**What Claude contributed, and what I accepted, changed or rejected.**

Claude proposed the intro and outro designs. I did not take them as given — I
sent back changes and iterated, and what shipped is the result of those rounds
rather than a first suggestion accepted. The shape of the collaboration was
consistent throughout: Claude generated candidates, I directed the revisions,
and the professor's confirmation is what closed it.

What that means for credit: the design ideas started on Claude's side, the
direction and the judgement calls were mine, and neither half produced the final
on its own.

**What I understand now, and what I still do not.**

I understand that finished branding changes how you write the thing it wraps —
the bookends are a timing contract, not a sticker. I had assumed the tooling
weeks were upstream of the content weeks; they are actually continuous with them.

I do not know whether these videos are any good, because nobody has responded.
And I cannot point at them. Two months of branding and planning produced a first
batch of content, and the repository's record of it is the sentence "a few new
videos." That is the single largest gap in this folder, and it is the newest.

**What changed.** The videos now have titles and links in [README.md](README.md).
Three days ago this folder described "a few new videos" and nothing else.

Two of the four are what this week actually produced — *What Really Stops A
Sliding Puck?* and *How Does Anything Ever Move?*. The other two are earlier
Shorts that carry the same intro/outro, kept here as evidence of the branding in
use rather than claimed as this week's output.

**What I notice looking at them together.** They are all classical mechanics —
falling bodies, mass versus weight, friction, inertia. I did not set out to make
a themed set, or at least I did not record that I had. Seeing the four titles in
one table makes the coherence obvious in a way that making them one at a time
did not. The channel-direction question that took three weeks to not-answer is
partly answered here by what I actually built.

**What is still open.** No beat sheets, no Drive folder, and the skill that
produced them remains unnamed and uncommitted. A reader can now watch the output
and still cannot rebuild it — which is the half of the evidence rule this
repository cares most about.
