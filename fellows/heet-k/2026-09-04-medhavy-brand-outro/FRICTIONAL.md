## 2026-09-06 — intros closed, outros opened

**What I was working on.** Making the outro counterpart to the now-final intros,
and sending it into the same review loop.

**What I tried, and what I expected.** I expected the outro to be close to free.
The intros had just been confirmed, the palette was settled, the wordmark
treatment was settled, the build path was known — an outro is the same brand
elements in reverse order. I expected to reuse rather than design.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**

- **An outro is not an intro backwards.** An intro's job is to establish and hand
  off; an outro's job is to close and point somewhere — the URL, the next video.
  Reversing the intro's arc produced something that felt like it had stopped
  rather than ended. I made several outros instead of one, because I could not
  tell in advance which ending read as deliberate.
- **I could not self-confirm it.** The intros had just taught me that my own
  assessment is not what makes brand work final. So I sent a set rather than
  picking a favourite and presenting it as the answer.
- **The hardest part of the week was not building anything.** It was keeping three
  statuses apart in the report: intros *confirmed*, outros *sent and pending*,
  explainer *ongoing*. The intros' confirmation was the week's good news and it
  would have been easy to let it colour the other two. I wrote them separately
  and said explicitly that nothing spilled over.

**What Claude contributed, and what I accepted, changed or rejected.**

- Accepted: reusing the intro's brand constants verbatim — palette, tracking,
  mark treatment — so the outro is recognisably the same identity rather than a
  second opinion about it.
- Rejected: shipping a single outro as *the* outro. Sent a set for review instead.

**What I understand now, and what I still do not.**

I understand that "sent" and "approved" are different states and that collapsing
them is the most tempting small dishonesty in a weekly report. The intros being
final made me want to write the outros as nearly final. They weren't.

I do not know which outro the professor would pick, or why — that came the
following week. And I did not keep the outro source anywhere I can now point at,
which means this entry describes work whose inputs a reader cannot open. That is
a real gap and I am recording it rather than smoothing over it.
