# Frictional log — catalogue segregation, five videos, and a plan

## 2026-11-22 — giving 200+ videos a shape, and getting it signed off

> Written 2026-11-22, the day the week closed, from the Week 25 report.
> Reconstructed rather than captured as it happened.

- **Confirmed:** the segregation and the five videos, by the professor
- **Planned only:** the automation skill — prompt to video
- **Hours:** Week 25 total 22.0 — 8.0 segregation, 8.0 videos, 3.0 planning — [HOURS.md](../HOURS.md)

**What I was working on.** Sorting the 200+ video catalogue into sections,
making five new videos, and deciding what skill to build next.

**What I tried, and what I expected.** I expected the segregation to be the
chore and the videos to be the week. It was the other way round — the
segregation is what got confirmed alongside them, and it is the thing that
changes what the channel looks like to someone arriving at it.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**

- **The fourth pass at the channel question is the one that worked, and the
  difference is what I was sorting.** Week 17 removed things. Week 20 curated.
  Week 22 read the corpus to find a gap. All three were about *what should
  exist*. This week was about *what already exists and how it is arranged* —
  a smaller question with a reachable answer. Eight hours and it was done,
  against roughly thirty spent on the harder version of it across three earlier
  weeks.
- **Sections are a claim about the channel, not just a tidy-up.** Deciding what
  the sections *are* is the content-direction question in disguise — you cannot
  group 200 videos without asserting what kinds there are. I had been treating
  organisation and direction as separate problems; sorting the catalogue turned
  out to answer part of the direction question as a side effect, the same way
  [Week 17's unlisting](../2026-09-24-channel-reorganization/) did and I only
  noticed a month later.
- **I sent it, and it came back confirmed.** The last three weeks all ended with
  work held — the [QC&I videos](../2026-11-12-qci-videos-refined/) and the skill
  are still unshared. This week I sent, and both the segregation and the videos
  were confirmed. The pattern I named in Week 24 — holding becoming a habit — is
  worth re-reading against this week, because the difference in outcome is
  stark and the only variable was sending.
- **I planned the automation skill rather than starting it.** Three hours
  deciding what to build. Given that the QC&I skill from Weeks 23–24 is still in
  progress and unmentioned this week, scoping a second, more ambitious skill
  before finishing the first is a choice I should be able to defend and have not
  written down a defence for.

**What Claude contributed, and what I accepted, changed or rejected.**

The five videos were produced through the Brutalist tool and the skill-based
pipeline, as in previous weeks. The segregation — which sections exist and which
video belongs in which — is mine. The automation plan is mine.

**What I understand now, and what I still do not.**

I understand that a question I could not answer in the abstract for three weeks
became answerable when I attacked it from the catalogue end instead of the
intention end. That is the same lesson as
[Week 22](../2026-10-29-video-repo-analysis/) — work from the corpus, not from
the plan — arriving a second time, which suggests I have not fully learned it.

I also understand, more sharply than before, what holding costs. Three weeks of
held work has produced no feedback; one week of sent work produced two
confirmations.

What I do not know is whether the sections are the right sections. They are
confirmed, which means someone agreed with them — not that they are what a
viewer needs. And I do not know what happens to the QC&I skill now that a second
skill has been scoped ahead of it.
