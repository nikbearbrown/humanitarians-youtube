## Why the behavioral round matters (framing line)
"When two candidates are technically even, the behavioral round often decides the offer." Sourced to `blueprint-trends-notes.md` in the book (`[^trends9]`) — a process/trend claim, not a technical one.

## What's tested (the book's own framing box)
Collaboration, ownership, handling ambiguity, and influence without authority. Panels want **specific, evidenced stories**, not adjectives about yourself.

## The STAR method
Anchored to Wikipedia, "Situation, task, action, result" (accessed 2026-07-15, `[^star]`). The book states: "The STAR method evaluates candidates by how they answer behavioral and situational questions." Structure:
- **Situation** — the scenario (brief).
- **Task** — your objective.
- **Action** — what *you* did, and why (not "we").
- **Result** — the outcome, quantified where possible, and what you learned.

STAR fits questions on conflict, time management, problem-solving, and interpersonal skills (book's own scope statement).

## The failure mode (required "when it breaks" content)
"The common failure: all Situation, no Result. Candidates set up the scene and never say what happened. The Result is the point." This is the load-bearing diagnostic line for the video's Pragmatist-register beat — used verbatim in spirit at B03.

## Story bank (delivery infrastructure)
Don't script per question — prepare 5-6 real stories mapped to competencies:

| Story | Covers |
|---|---|
| A project you owned | ownership, initiative |
| A disagreement | conflict, influence |
| A failure | resilience, learning |
| An ambiguous task | judgment under uncertainty |
| A deadline crunch | prioritization |

"One good story often answers several question archetypes."

## Delivery notes
Keep it short and specific, be honest, quantify results where possible ("cut the report time from 3 hours to 20 minutes"), and answer what the question is *really* scoring — not its literal wording.

## The worked example (used as the video's spine, B04-B06)
**Q:** "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate."

**Weak answer (quoted from the book):** "We disagreed about a chart once but it worked out fine." — vague; no task, action, or result.

**STAR rewrite (quoted from the book):**
- **S** — "On my internship, a teammate wanted to report signups including test accounts."
- **T** — "I needed the number to be accurate for the leadership deck."
- **A** — "I pulled both figures, showed the ~12% inflation from test data, and proposed a filter we both agreed on."
- **R** — "We shipped the corrected metric; it became the team's standard definition."

Competency scored (book's own annotation): influence + data judgment.
