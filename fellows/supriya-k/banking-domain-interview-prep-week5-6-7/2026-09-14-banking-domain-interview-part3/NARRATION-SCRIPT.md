## OPEN

**B00.** Hi, I am Supriya and this video is about what a banking panel listens for in a STAR answer. Same method; a different half of the story.

## ACT I — The STAR Framework

**B01.** Part one: the framework.

**B02.** Situation, Task, Action, Result: the scene, your responsibility, what you did, and what changed because of it.

**B03.** The generic failure: all Situation, no Result.

## ACT II — What a Banking Panel Listens For

**B04.** Part two: what a banking panel listens for.

**B05.** This is the book's own framing, not a published rubric: on top of the usual competencies, a bank panel listens for risk awareness, control judgment, and comfort under audit.

**B06.** Prepare a story for each of three situations: flagging a data-quality issue, an audit or examiner request, and a stakeholder pushing against a control.

**B07.** The banking failure is subtler. "The numbers looked off" isn't a Result. Flagging it to your lead and catching the error before quarter-end close is.

**B08.** And name the control: "we fixed it" is weaker than "we added a validation check that catches that error."

## ACT III — Weak Answer, STAR Rewrite

**B09.** Part three: a weak answer, rewritten.

**B10.** The weak answer ends: "I mentioned it and we fixed it before it went out. It worked out fine."

**B11.** The rewrite. Situation: a teammate's dashboard, two days before the VP sees it. Task: you built its data model. Action: one region doubled; you trace a double-counting join, flag your teammate and lead that day, and hold the publish. Result: the VP never sees it, and a dbt range check now fails the build.

**B12.** What's scored: noticing the anomaly, escalating it right away, and naming the control it produced.

## ACT IV — Daniel's Full-Loop Mock

**B13.** Part four: Daniel's mock loop.

**B14.** A full banking loop can run five stages, though not every bank runs all five in a day. Daniel, three and a half years in, practices them back to back.

**B15.** "Tell me about a time you found an error in your own or a colleague's work."

**B16.** His answer: a mis-joined table inflated a retention metric, and then, "I fixed it and let my manager know." No new check. No outcome.

**B17.** He scores that stage a one: STAR shape, but no named escalation or outcome. His other four stages score twos.

**B18.** So behavioral gets most of his remaining hours, and his note to himself fits every stage: finish the thought out loud.
