# Research Summary — "The STAR Method Banking Panels Actually Score"

## Act I — The STAR Framework

The universal STAR method, quoted directly from the chapter:

> "Structure every answer as: **Situation** — the scenario, briefly. **Task** — your
> responsibility or goal. **Action** — what *you* specifically did. **Result** — the outcome, and
> what changed because of it." (`chapters/12-behavioral-round.md`, "The STAR method, and the
> failure specific to banking")

> "The generic STAR failure is all Situation, no Result — candidates set the scene and never say
> what happened." (same section)

## Act II — What a Banking Panel Listens For

The book is explicit that this is its own pedagogical synthesis, not a published rubric — this
video keeps that framing:

> "No single published source names these as a formal scored rubric — this is this book's own
> synthesis, built on two things that *are* well-documented: the general STAR framework, and the
> well-known regulatory reality that banks operate under heavy supervisory examination." (Ch. 12,
> "What a banking panel weights beyond the generic")

The three competencies (quoted): risk awareness, regulatory/control judgment, comfort under
audit/examiner scrutiny (Ch. 12, same section).

The banking-specific failure and its accepted version, quoted directly:

> "'I noticed the numbers looked off' is not a Result a risk-aware panel accepts. 'I noticed the
> numbers looked off, so I flagged it to my lead before it went into the report, and we caught a
> mapping error before it reached the quarter-end close' is." (Ch. 12, "The STAR method, and the
> failure specific to banking")

The three archetypes (quoted, condensed): flagging a data-quality issue affecting a regulatory or
reported number; working through an internal-audit or examiner-style request; handling conflicting
pressure from a business stakeholder versus a control function (Ch. 12, "Banking behavioral
archetypes").

The delivery rule, quoted directly:

> "'We fixed it' is weaker than 'we added a validation check that catches that specific error
> before it reaches the report.' The second version proves you understood *why* the problem
> mattered, not just that you solved it once." (Ch. 12, "Building your story bank and delivering
> it")

## Act III — Weak Answer, STAR Rewrite

Both the weak answer and the STAR rewrite are quoted verbatim from the chapter's worked example
("Tell me about a time you found an error in your own or a colleague's work"), including the exact
dollar-free, illustrative dashboard/dbt-test-suite scenario. The "what's scored" framing (risk
awareness, escalation, outcome/control) is the chapter's own closing analysis of that worked
example, quoted directly:

> "Competency scored: risk awareness... escalation... and outcome/control — naming the specific
> control... which is exactly the 'control or escalation action' the weak version omitted
> entirely." (Ch. 12, worked example closing note)
