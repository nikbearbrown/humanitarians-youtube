Project Brief: Detecting Inauthentic Instagram Engagement
Project: Madison — Intelligence Agents layer
Team: Asheesh Nellutla, Karishma Suresh Kumar
(Names are in the source file. They are not spoken on the cut.)

What we're working on:
Building a machine learning capability that detects inauthentic social media engagement on
Instagram - bot accounts, fake followers, and fake likes or comments. The system takes an
account's profile and engagement data and returns an explainable risk assessment: how likely the
account itself is fake, and separately, how likely the engagement on a given post is fake. This is
being built as a new capability for Madison's Intelligence Agents layer, alongside the existing
scraping infrastructure already in place for social media research.

Problem we are solving:
Right now, a brand or creator deciding whether to invest in an influencer partnership has no easy
way to verify whether that influencer's audience is real. Large companies can afford proprietary
vetting tools for this; smaller brands, independent creators, and nonprofits — the groups
Humanitarians AI exists to support — can't. Without a way to check, marketing spend can go
toward partnerships built on inflated, fake engagement, with no way to catch it beforehand.

Objective:
Give brands and creators an accessible, explainable trust signal — not a black-box verdict, but a
risk score with visible reasoning behind it — so they can check an account's authenticity before
committing resources to a partnership. The goal is to make this kind of verification available to
the people Madison is meant to serve, not just those who can afford enterprise tools.

Our approach and how it differs from existing tools:
Existing influencer-vetting platforms (HypeAuditor, Modash, CreatorIQ, and others reviewed in
our competitive analysis) check fraud at the account level only - producing a single score based on
account-wide statistics like follower ratios and growth patterns, typically using rule-based
thresholds (for example, flagging an account once its fake-follower percentage crosses a set
number) rather than a trained, validated machine learning model.

Our approach differs in two concrete ways:
A trained and validated classifier, not a rule-based score. We train our model on TwiBot-20, a large,
labeled benchmark dataset with known ground truth (real vs. bot) — letting us measure and report
actual performance, rather than relying on hand-set thresholds.
Engagement-level evaluation, not just account-level. We separately assess whether the specific
likes and comments on a post look authentic, independent of whether the account posting looks
authentic. None of the competitors we reviewed appear to offer this second layer.
