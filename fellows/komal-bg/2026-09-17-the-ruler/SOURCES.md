## Jung vs the twelve-brand wheel

- **Carl Jung** named archetypes as recurring patterns in the collective unconscious. He did **not** publish a twelve-seat marketing wheel.
- Branding later maps twelve characters onto a wheel; the Ruler seat is control against chaos. This cut names Jung and “branding,” not a specific book.

## The Ruler (psyche used in B03)

- Job: take control, especially when situations seem out of hand; make life as predictable and stable as possible.
- Desire: control. Goal: create a prosperous, successful family, company, or community. Strategy: exert leadership. Fear: chaos, being overthrown. Trap: being bossy, authoritarian. Gift: responsibility, leadership.

## Luxury mapping (interpretation, labeled as such)

The reel’s Rank / Access / Standard frame is a Teardown reading (hierarchy, impressive surroundings, status, prestige, closed order) applied to luxury houses. It is not a quote from Jung.

Falsifiability: other seats exist. Lover expects quality for intimacy, not status; Magician is a different mastery promise. The reel does not claim every luxury house is a Ruler.

## American Express as worked example

- Public card hierarchy (Green / Gold / Platinum) is a **simplified redraw** of a widely known membership ladder, used to show rank-as-product.
- “Luxury personas” / the high-achieving executive attracted to Ruler products: people with Ruler tendencies care about image, status, and prestige because those things enhance power.

## What this reel does not claim

- No Jung quote that luxury “is” the Ruler.
- No follower counts, card-member totals, or current Amex campaign slogans as facts.
- No claim that Mercedes, Rolex, or Louis Vuitton are Ruler brands.
