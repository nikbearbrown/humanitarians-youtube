Film claims only. Research notes live in SOURCES.md.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| B00–B02 | Jung named shared patterns; branding uses twelve characters on a wheel | SUPPORTED | Jung: archetypes / collective unconscious. Twelve-seat brand wheels are a later marketing application, not Jung’s diagram | Film does not name a branding-book author |
| B02 | Ruler’s job is control / keep chaos out | SUPPORTED | Standard Ruler-seat reading: take control; fear of chaos; make life predictable | None |
| B02 | Outlaw as shadow of Ruler | QUALIFY | Outlaw is a **contrast seat** on the twelve-wheel (smashes order), used here as the wheel’s “shadow archetype” slot, not a clinical Shadow box | Narration says “Shadow: the Outlaw, who smashes the same order” |
| B03 | Desire control; fear chaos / overthrown; gift responsibility; trap tyrant | SUPPORTED | Standard Ruler-seat psyche (control / chaos / responsibility / tyrant) | None |
| B04 | Luxury maps via rank, access, standard | QUALIFY | Interpretation of Ruler (hierarchy, status, impressive order) applied to luxury. Not a cited table | Labeled as “why luxury lands here” — Teardown |
| B04 | Not every luxury house is Ruler (Lover / Magician) | SUPPORTED as framework logic | Twelve seats exist; Lover quality-for-intimacy vs Ruler status; Magician is a different mastery promise | None |
| B05 | American Express plays the Ruler; membership as rank | QUALIFY | Teaching example: public Green / Gold / Platinum ladder as visible hierarchy. Not a market-share claim | Caption: “Hierarchy redrawn (simplified).” |
| B05 | Luxury personas choose Ruler houses to be noticed | QUALIFY | Interpretation: Ruler-leaning buyers care about image/status/prestige because look enhances power | Soft attribution in narration (“that is why”) |
