## Beat 4 — THE PROBLEM

**On screen:** can't compute -> don't pretend

**Narration:** To check that a balance sheet balances you need assets, liabilities, and equity. If one is missing, you can't run the check. A two-answer system must pick. Pass is a quiet lie, you didn't verify anything. Fail cries wolf on data that might be fine. Neither tells the truth.

## Beat 5 — THE MECHANISM

**On screen:** PASS · FAIL · UNKNOWN

**Narration:** So every rule returns one of three verdicts. Pass, when the data is present and the check holds. Fail, when it's violated, an impossible margin, a sheet that doesn't balance. And unknown, when the inputs aren't there. Unknown is the system refusing to pretend, surfacing exactly where you can't yet trust the data.

## Beat 7 — The verdict

**On screen:** Calibrated doubt beats confident guessing

**Narration:** Ask whether something that validates data for you can say I don't know, or whether it's forced to pretend. Does it catch the truly impossible? When it can't decide, does it tell you? A checker that's always certain is not trustworthy. One that's honest about its limits is. Calibrated doubt beats confident guessing.
