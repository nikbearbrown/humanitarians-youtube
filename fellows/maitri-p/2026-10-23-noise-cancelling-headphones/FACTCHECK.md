# FACTCHECK — noise-cancelling-headphones

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sound reaches the ear as changes in air pressure | TRUE — sound is a pressure wave in air |
| 2 | A microphone on the headphones picks up outside noise | TRUE — feed-forward active noise control |
| 3 | The headphones play a sound with opposite pressure changes | TRUE — anti-phase signal (destructive interference) |
| 4 | Cancellation happens at the ear, not in the room | TRUE — the anti-noise is played inside the ear cup |
| 5 | Works best on steady low sounds such as engine hum | TRUE — predictable, low-frequency noise is easiest to match |
| 6 | Voices and sudden sounds may still be heard | TRUE — fast-changing, higher-frequency sound is harder to cancel |
| 7 | Ear cushions block some sound physically | TRUE — passive isolation |
| 8 | Never perfect silence | TRUE — on screen the computed sum keeps a residual |
| 9 | Wave animation residual | COMPUTED: opposite wave at 0.88 strength, 0.22 rad timing error → peak residual 0.24 (24%) |
