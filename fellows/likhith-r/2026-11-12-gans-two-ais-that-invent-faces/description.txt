This person never existed. No camera took the picture: two AIs competing against each other made it. This explainer covers GANs, and why convincing fakes call for caution.

What's covered:
- In 2014, Ian Goodfellow and colleagues proposed GANs: a generator (the forger) and a discriminator (the detective)
- The forger makes fakes from noise and the detective calls real or fake; each improves the other
- Training is done when the detective is right only half the time, a coin flip
- StyleGAN (NVIDIA, 2019) made faces realistic enough for a new fake on every refresh
- The danger: scam profiles, forged videos, non-consensual images, and a 2024 Hong Kong case where about US$25 million was sent after a deepfake video call
- Cautions: confirm surprising requests on a channel you trust, check content credentials, don't treat detectors as proof, and never share someone's likeness without consent

Sources: Goodfellow et al. (2014), Karras et al. on StyleGAN (2019), Hong Kong police reporting via CNN (2024), Arup's confirmation, and the C2PA standard. The faces shown are drawn stand-ins, and the accuracy curve comes from a labelled toy run.
