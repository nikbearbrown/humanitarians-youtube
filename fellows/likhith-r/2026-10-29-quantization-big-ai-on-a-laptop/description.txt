A 70-billion-weight AI model needs about 140 GB just for its weights, yet people run big models on laptops. This explainer covers quantization.

What's covered:
- Every weight is a number, two bytes each at 16 bits
- Quantization rounds each weight onto a small grid and stores it in 8 or 4 bits
- 8 bits gives 256 levels, 4 bits just 16, with a scale per group to map them back
- 14 GB becomes 7, then about 3.5, and often runs faster because memory is the bottleneck
- GPTQ corrects rounding errors and AWQ guards the key weights; below 4 bits quality drops

Sources: the GPTQ and AWQ papers, the llama.cpp and Ollama docs, and published low-bit evaluations. Every number shown comes from a labelled toy run.
