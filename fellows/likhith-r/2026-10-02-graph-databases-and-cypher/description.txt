Some data is mostly relationships: who knows whom, who paid whom. This explainer covers graph databases and Cypher.

What's covered:
- A graph database stores nodes (things) and edges (relationships) directly
- In SQL, a three-hop question needs three joins; a graph follows the stored edges
- Cypher draws the pattern: (a:Person)-[:FRIENDS_WITH]->(b:Person)
- *3 asks for exactly three hops, and both engines return the same answer
- Graphs fit fraud rings, recommendations and knowledge graphs; for plain rows and totals, tables still win

Sources: Neo4j Cypher Manual, Neo4j's graph database guide, openCypher. Every query and result shown comes from a real run in Kuzu and SQLite.
