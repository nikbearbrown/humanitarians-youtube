At NVIDIA's GTC Berlin keynote, four robot ducks were told to sit, stand and take a nap, and three of them fell over. This explainer covers Microduck and sim-to-real training.

What's covered:
- Microduck: a 25 cm, $399 open-source biped from Pollen Robotics and Hugging Face, with 15 motors
- Sim-to-real: the robot learns in a physics simulation, not on fragile hardware
- Thousands of simulated copies practise at once on one GPU, and a fall is just a reset
- The trained policy is exported as one file and runs on the real robot's motors
- The transfer is imperfect, but simulate-then-transfer is becoming physical AI's standard

Sources: Pollen Robotics' Microduck pages, Tech Times and RuntimeWire on the Isaac Lab training path, and Reuters/KSL and Yahoo News coverage of the keynote. The fall counts shown come from a labelled toy run.
