# SOURCES — How Facial Recognition Actually Works (And When It Shouldn't)

Primary source for the central factual claim (B04):

- **NISTIR 8280 — Face Recognition Vendor Test (FRVT) Part 3: Demographic Effects**
  (NIST, December 19, 2019). https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2019/nist.ir.8280.pdf
  Evaluated **189 algorithms from 99 developers** on **18.27 million images of 8.49
  million people**, drawn from operational State Department, DHS, and FBI
  databases. Finding: most algorithms show higher false-positive rates for
  women, African Americans, and especially African American women; some of
  the most accurate one-to-many algorithms showed similar (much smaller) gaps
  across those groups.
- **NIST news release**: "NIST Study Evaluates Effects of Race, Age, Sex on
  Face Recognition Software" (Dec 19, 2019).
  https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2019/12/nist-study-evaluates-effects-race-age-sex-face-recognition-software
- **Industry-aligned counter-perspective** (cited in B04 as "some industry
  voices argue the disparity is overstated"): Security Industry Association,
  "What NIST Data Shows About Facial Recognition and Demographics" (Feb 14,
  2020). https://www.securityindustry.org/2020/02/14/what-nist-data-shows-about-facial-recognition-and-demographics/

## What this reel does NOT claim

- Does not cite a specific named algorithm/vendor's individual score.
- Does not claim the demographic gap is closed industry-wide — only that it
  narrows substantially for the top-performing algorithms in NIST's own test.
- Does not take a policy position on whether facial recognition should be
  regulated, banned, or expanded — the narration is deliberately framed as
  descriptive (B06's closing thesis is about proportional scrutiny, not a
  verdict on the technology).
