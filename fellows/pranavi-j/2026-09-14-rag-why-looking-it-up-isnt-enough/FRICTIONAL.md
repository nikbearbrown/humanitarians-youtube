# Frictional log — RAG: Why "Looking It Up" Doesn't Guarantee It's True

*Reconstructed 2026-10-20 from this folder's BUILD-LOG.md and FACTCHECK.md.*

## What was tried and expected

This general-AI-topic explainer teaches a reusable 3-question rubric — Relevant / Current / Grounded — for deciding whether a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system's answer is actually grounded in what it retrieved, rather than just sounding like it is. The plan was a 9-beat explainer: the rubric shown in full before any example, a worked example (a support bot confidently answering from a retrieved document that's eight months stale), a falsifiability case where retrieval genuinely is the right fix, and a closing audit task — both worked scenarios generic and hypothetical by design. This was also built under the toolkit's new submission spec: separate landscape and vertical deliverables, with the vertical companion a true full-length 4K native-portrait rebuild rather than a cropped Shorts cut.

## Where it resisted

- GATE A caught two genuine "shapes never change" defects (beats whose only non-text shape was a static, unchanging divider line) — fixed by adding real moving or state-changing shapes that also improved the actual explanation, not just checker appeasement.
- GATE B caught a real off-frame text placement where reveal tags positioned relative to a wide, centered element pushed their own width past the frame edge — fixed by anchoring to measured coordinates instead of a directional offset.
- The first full render came back with real edge-bleed and underfill/contrast defects (panels sized past the safe half-width, and the same `teal`-on-`ink` palette contrast bug already documented in a sibling reel). The underfill fixes were initially solved with an "invisible oversized frame" trick that technically satisfied the automated canvas-fill metric — but direct manual frame extraction caught that this had hidden the real problem: several beats had genuinely empty lower halves of the frame for much of their runtime, content clustered in the upper half behind the fake border. This required a second, real fix pass — redesigning the actual content to use the full frame height — rather than trusting the metric.
- The vertical rebuild hit its own round of real bugs: an early draft accumulated chained relative positioning into an actual out-of-frame coordinate; another beat's independently-guessed positions produced a real text/highlight-box overlap; and after a "clean by the numbers" export, manual frame extraction again caught two defects the automated metric couldn't see — a title visibly touching the text below it, and a rubric beat wrapping text by raw character count instead of word boundaries, splitting real words mid-letter ("keyword overl/ap").
- The most serious issue surfaced after both masters had already shipped to `deliverables/`: a frame pulled from the shipped vertical master showed nonsense mid-word gaps in the exec-summary card's text, even though the actual scene source file on disk was already correct plain English. The root cause was a stale-render-cache bug — the render pipeline's skip-if-already-filled logic doesn't detect source-code changes, so most of both the landscape and vertical masters had been silently assembled from Manim clips that predated the latest source edits. This required deleting every cached clip in both aspect ratios and fully re-rendering and re-verifying both masters from scratch, including re-confirming file hashes to prove no staleness remained.
- A separate, more minor texture was investigated during that same cleanup — a fine inter-character spacing artifact visible only under heavy pixel-level zoom — and was confirmed via pixel-diffing to be a pre-existing font-rendering characteristic of the environment, not something introduced by the stale-cache bug or fixed by the rebuild; left alone as out of scope since it doesn't affect normal-viewing legibility.
