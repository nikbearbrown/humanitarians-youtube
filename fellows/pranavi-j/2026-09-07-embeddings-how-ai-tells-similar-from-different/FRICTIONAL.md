# Frictional log — Embeddings: How AI Tells Similar From Different (When Keyword Matching Can't)

*Reconstructed 2026-10-20 from this folder's BUILD-LOG.md and FACTCHECK.md.*

## What was tried and expected

This general-AI-topic explainer teaches a reusable 3-question rubric — Wording Varies / Context Flips Meaning / Exactness Is The Point — for deciding whether a text-matching rule should key off exact keywords or semantic similarity (embeddings). The plan was a 9-beat, ~2.5-minute `ai-explainer`: a keyword-rule hook that misses a document tagged with an industry acronym instead of the exact phrase it expected, the framework shown in full before any example, a hand-built 2D "meaning space" scatter diagram, a falsifiability case (two similar-looking but must-stay-distinct rule numbers) stress-testing the same idea, and a closing audit task. Both worked examples were planned as fully generic and fictional from the start, confirmed by fact-check before narration lock — one borderline realistic rule-number format was even swapped for a fully fictional placeholder to remove any risk of being mistaken for a real citation.

## Where it resisted

- Several real bugs were found and fixed before the first render: a coordinate placed well outside the hard frame because a render-free static-checker stub's position estimate diverged from real Manim metrics (fixed by switching to a fixed explicit anchor computed from measured bounds rather than a relative `next_to`); a genuine overlap between two elements caught only by constructing the real Manim objects and measuring their bounds directly; and diagram gridlines that struck through almost every plotted label, triggering the post-render layout checker's real "text struck by a stroke" check (removed entirely rather than patched).
- A known latent palette contrast bug from a sibling reel (`teal` measuring a broken 1.56:1 contrast on the dark `ink` background) was proactively found and fixed here before the first render, rather than being rediscovered later.
- The first full 4K render's GATE V pass came back with 165 MAJOR findings, overwhelmingly underfill — this was not resolved until a dedicated 2026-09-09 patch redesigned larger diagrams, typography, and spacing across all 9 beats in both aspect ratios, after which the real count still included one genuine BLOCKER: a document card's fade-in entrance animation briefly slid past the safe-area edge, caught by extracting and inspecting the actual flagged frame rather than trusting the report number.
- Rebuilding the 9:16 Short surfaced a second, separate stale-cache bug: the portrait scenes had been redesigned, but the render pipeline's "skip if already filled" logic silently reused the old pre-fix clips, producing a short master that was byte-identical to the stale build — only caught by comparing file hashes, which forced deleting the stale clips and re-rendering from scratch.
- A further 2026-09-10 patch found a defect GATE V's automated bounding-box check is structurally blind to: two "meaning space" scatter diagrams had large empty regions for most of their steady-state runtime even though the automated MAJOR count never moved, because the checker measures a panel's own bounding box as "content" rather than the distribution of meaningful content within it — only caught by extracting real frames spread across each beat's full runtime and looking at them directly. The fix (reordering when elements appear, pulling scattered points closer together, adding a connecting visual element) required re-discovering the same stale-cache bug again mid-patch, this time on individual beats rather than the whole short.
- Two separate fellow-reported visual problems arrived after the fact via screenshots: text sitting too close to the true frame edge (inside the zone typical video players cover with on-hover controls, even though technically inside the raw frame), and a diagram label rendered essentially invisible against its background (measured contrast of 1.16:1, far below even the already-fixed teal issue). Both required new palette variants and careful re-verification that the fix for one didn't reintroduce the other (enlarging bottom margins for frame-edge safety initially ate into the clearance needed from a panel's own border, caught by the post-render layout checker as a new regression before being resolved by shrinking the panel itself).
- A mid-session upstream pull of the shared toolkit introduced two new build-blocking behaviors not present when this project started: silent beats now required an explicit opt-in declaration that this reel's title card had never carried, and the automatic final-export step had a rounding bug that refused to export at all because of an ~27ms gap between the container's reported duration and the last decodable frame — worked around by manually reassembling the final master from the same per-beat clips the automated step would have used, rather than editing the shared toolkit.

## Still open / understood now

- The GATE V MAJOR count (15 on 16:9, 26 on 9:16) is understood to include findings the tool cannot meaningfully resolve further — staged-reveal transients, ink-background contrast dilution from a whole-frame luminance heuristic, and deliberately compact title/brand cards — the same accepted category documented across this fellow's other reels.
- The 2026-09-10 patch explicitly documents that GATE V's bounding-box-based check cannot detect content-distribution defects (sparse content within an otherwise "filled" panel) — real verification for that class of issue required manual frame extraction, not the automated gate, and that distinction is now understood as a genuine limitation of the tooling.
- The toolkit's automatic final-export rounding bug and its new short-pipeline planning-step requirement were worked around locally without editing the shared `brutalist/` repo, since this task was scoped to read-only toolkit use.
- Publishing was not authorized for either the long-form or Shorts cut as of the last BUILD-LOG entry; nothing in this project was uploaded or pushed externally.
