# Sources — Lost in the Middle: Why More Context Doesn't Guarantee a Better Answer

General AI/STEM topic explainer. The "lost in the middle" effect — reduced reliability for
information placed in the middle of a long context versus the start/end — is a well-known,
widely-discussed class of behavior in long-context language model research. This video describes
the general, settled shape of the effect qualitatively; it deliberately does not cite a specific
paper's numeric results or claim the effect's current severity in any specific real model
(providers have worked to mitigate it over time, and its exact shape varies by model and
context-handling architecture).

No specific real model or vendor is named or benchmarked. The worked example (a long onboarding
document with a buried fact) is fully generic and illustrative, matching the pattern used in this
fellow's prior STEM videos (e.g. the "strawberry" tokenization example, the fabricated-citation
hallucination example).
