**First monthly baseline: 2026-11-18**

## Executive judgment

There is substantive work here, but the collection mixes **working software, research prototypes, negative experimental results, educational films, operational work and proposed projects**. Its strongest public story is not “our AI solves everything.” It is **people building useful AI, measuring its failures, and keeping human judgment in charge**.

My first outreach slate would be:

1. **Gavia: offline AI that helps people review wildlife photographs and recordings.** Strong concrete demonstration and conservation mission. Field effectiveness still needs validation.
2. **Medhavy and cancer education: a textbook tutor, reviewed evidence, and a reading roadmap.** Show an actual student workflow, then show how experts catch mistakes. Do not claim clinical or learning efficacy.
3. **Lyrical Literacy: AI-assisted learning media, including the Homes of Hope bilingual-song prototype, with human review and children's privacy.** Excellent human story, but pre-pilot, not a proven literacy intervention.
4. **“We tried reinforcement learning; the simple rule held up.”** Laksh's autoscaling research is an unusually defensible responsible-AI story, with public implementation and recorded evidence.
5. **“More data can make medical AI worse.”** Dhruvi's stale-ECG study is a compelling research lead, contingent on accessible manuscript/results and expert review.

This is a **ranked editorial shortlist**, not authorization to publish films, a certification of products, or an assessment of individual worth. Each story has a release gate below. No external outreach, repository deletion, or alteration of fellows' work was performed for this report.

## Ranked outreach list

### How this ranking works

This is an editorial ordering using five dimensions: **human/public benefit, concrete AI contribution, evidence strength, clarity of the story, and readiness after proportionate review**. Documented failures can rank above flashy but weakly supported success claims. No numerical score is presented because the evidence is heterogeneous and no defensible common outcome metric exists. Clinical, child-related and financial stories have higher release requirements. Closely related work is grouped, preserving contribution distinctions in the detailed reviews.

**Evidence language:** “source-backed” means inspectable code/data or production artifacts support that part of the account; “report-backed” means the claim appears in project records but has not been reproduced here; “prototype” does not mean deployed; “plan” does not mean built. All ranks below concern story potential, not proof of beneficiary outcomes.

## What is substantive, and what is not established

### Strong substance in the records

- **Concrete software and inspection trails:** Gavia's public implementation references; RL autoscaler's public training code; Medhavy's captured workflows/code-pinned claim ledgers; multiple Mycroft failure reports with commits and test references.
- **Actual research effort rather than only aspirational topics:** CaNCURE ablations and prerequisite diagnostics; stale-ECG and NeuroVEP reports; SurveyMind statistical framework; RAMAN review corrections; calibrated fine-tuning and agent-memory experiments.
- **Substantive educational production:** inspectable scene scripts, beat sheets, source/claim records, demos and review logs across the portfolio. This deserves credit even when it is not a new AI product.
- **Necessary supporting work:** deployment fixes, secure LMS integration, nonprofit site repairs, games integration, brand/rights management and publication QA. These should be credited as engineering/design/operations, not relabelled “AI research.”

### Things this review does not establish

- Independent clinical effectiveness, better learner outcomes, improved employment outcomes, conservation population benefit, financial returns or organizational cost savings.
- That every film is final, published, accurate or usable. Many explicitly remain under review, source-only, private/local or blocked.
- That more files or videos means a more valuable contributor. Revisions, generated artifacts, shorts, templates and support files inflate counts.
- That README-only folders identify inactive people. Work may live elsewhere or be inadequately reported.

## Practical first-month plan

### Prepare three demonstration stories

- **Gavia:** one representative success and one failure, human review, offline use and current licensing limitations. Verify installation without recommending bypass of security warnings as a substitute for publisher verification.
- **Medhavy:** a learner asks a real question, inspects retrieved context, sees a limitation, and a reviewer explains a corrected example. Pair the product demo with the CaNCURE prototype only if the integration is real and clearly shown.
- **Lyrical Literacy / Homes of Hope:** a staff-approved prototype song and explanation of language/cultural review. No children need to appear for the story to work.

### Prepare two evidence-led research stories

- **RL autoscaling:** reproduce a small benchmark slice, show the simple baseline and why “no win” is a valuable result.
- **Clinical-data freshness or NeuroVEP:** choose the project with accessible aggregate evidence and expert review first. Do not rush a clinical headline because its mission sounds strong.

### Keep a publication register

Track manuscript title, contributor folder IDs, project attribution, draft/submitted/accepted/preprint/published status, venue, date and verified public identifier. Missing evidence remains missing. Workshop, journal, preprint and article are distinct categories.

## Monthly refresh protocol

Use `fellows/status`, not `updates`. Preserve this baseline and add a new dated report monthly.

1. Record the exact repository commit and enumerate new/changed/deleted paths since the prior snapshot.
2. Reconcile every folder, including new submissions and previously README-only records. Recheck aliases without merging them on guesswork.
3. Review changed project records and the linked implementation/results, not just the video narrative. Distinguish new artifacts from re-renders and duplicated variants.
4. Update categories, maturity, verified publications and the outreach ranking. Explain promotions/demotions by evidence, not output volume.
5. Verify the current top stories' playable/shared links, evidence, permission/rights and human-review gates before calling them outreach-ready.
6. Report new builds, substantive changes, blockers and the best next stories to Nik. Do not contact fellows, publish media, or delete repositories/folders automatically.
7. Retain every dated report and its source commit so progress can be compared. Reuse the same evidence vocabulary; never turn a past “reported” result into “verified” merely through repetition.

The accompanying status README defines the durable report structure. The monthly schedule itself is maintained separately; this file does not claim that a scheduler was installed by a repository edit.
