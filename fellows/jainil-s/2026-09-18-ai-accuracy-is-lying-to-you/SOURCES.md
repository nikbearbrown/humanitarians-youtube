## Derived, not cited — the spine of the reel

Worked example, fully checkable on screen:

- Condition affects **1 in 1,000**. Test has a **5% false-positive rate** and
  catches every true case.
- Of 1,000 people: **1** true positive. Of the 999 healthy, 5% ≈ **50** false
  positives.
- A positive result is therefore right about **1 in 51 ≈ 2%** of the time.
- A model that simply answers "negative" every time is **99.9% accurate** and
  catches nothing.

Every number above is arithmetic from the two stated premises. The premises are
chosen for the illustration, not claimed as any real-world prevalence.
