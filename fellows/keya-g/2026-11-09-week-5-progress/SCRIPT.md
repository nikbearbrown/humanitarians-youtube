## 2. This week's video
This week's explainer is How Duolingo Decides What You Practice Next. I've been learning Japanese on Duolingo for over a year, so I researched the forgetting curve, spaced repetition, Leitner flashcard boxes, Duolingo's blog posts, and the research paper behind its model.

## 3. Reading the paper
The biggest lesson came from the paper itself. A summary said the model improved engagement by twelve percent. But the results table showed that number compared two versions of the same model. Against the older method, the results were mixed. So I took the number out. Now I check what a number is compared against, not just the headline.

## 4. Landscape and vertical
I also learned how different a vertical video is from a landscape one. The narration is the same, but a squeezed landscape layout doesn't work on a phone. Each vertical scene needs its own design: one idea at a time, stacked top to bottom, with bigger text.

## 5. Measure, don't assume
The build also taught me to ask for measurements. Claude Code first gave me a wrong figure for the vertical text size, so I asked it to measure the real frames. Every scene's text was above my seventy-two pixel rule.

## 6. Watching and listening
Some things only a person catches. I picked how to say shimasu, mimasu and Leitner by ear, from test clips. And while watching the previews, I noticed the typing and the voice were out of sync in one scene, and got it fixed.

## 7. Watching Brutalist videos

## 8. Takeaway
