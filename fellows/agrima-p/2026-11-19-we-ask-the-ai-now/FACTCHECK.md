# FACTCHECK — we-ask-the-ai-now

Source: the user-supplied article "We Ask the AI Now" (pasted in the build request)
and its three references: Bain & Company (2026, March 20), *Why consumers choose an AI
chatbot over a search engine*; Caswell (2025, September 3), *Tom's Guide*, reporting a
survey by the publisher Future; Crestodina (2026, March 18), *Orbit Media Studios*,
*The AI-search adoption survey*. Every figure below is stated in that article with the
attribution shown; none was added or changed. I did not re-verify the figures against
the original surveys; they are reported as the article reports them and labeled that
way. **Orbit Media is a web design and SEO company and ran its own survey; the Future
survey is from a media publisher and from 2025; Bain's survey was conducted in
September 2025; the surveys measure different things (default tool, use for certain
tasks, frequency of use), so their numbers are not compared or merged** — said in
narration (B02, B03, B09), on each card (source chip) and on the sources card.

No real brand or company logo is used: the search bar, magnifier and chat bubble are
generic icons, and names such as Google and Bain appear only as plain text.

1. **The opening scene** (B01) — planning a weekend trip: a few years ago, "best
   things to do in Lisbon" and ten tabs; today the same question to an AI chat plus "I
   have two kids and a small budget" and one answer in seconds. **An illustration, not
   a real case, but it matches something real** — narration and an on-screen label say
   so.

2. **Search split** (B02) — Bain (1,500 US consumers): 56% mostly or always use search
   engines as their default tool; 16% say the same about AI chatbots. Orbit Media
   (March 2026, 1,110 US adults): Google use has not declined; the share who "usually
   use search" for research grew from 22% to 29% between 2025 and 2026; the share who
   "usually use AI chat" fell from 35% to 28%. The article's explanation (Google is the
   default on most phones and browsers) is not narrated. The Bain and Orbit cards are on
   opposite sides of a divider and never combined.

3. **What we hand over** (B03) — Bain: among chatbot users, the top uses are making
   complicated topics easier to understand (53%), step-by-step instructions (47%),
   brainstorming (45%) and quick factual questions (45%). Future (via Caswell, 2025):
   55% of US respondents and 62% in the UK use AI instead of search engines for some
   tasks; the six example tasks (planning vacations, fitness plans, gift ideas, tech
   problems, comparing products, emails) are shown on screen, not narrated; the card says
   "survey from 2025". Orbit: only 24% preferred AI chat for local and business
   searches, so search keeps the edge there.

4. **The answer at the top** (B04) — Orbit: Google's AI Overviews appear in 76% of
   search results pages, **based on a study it cites** (Search Engine Land, named in the
   article, not on screen); in Orbit's survey 70% use AI summaries at least
   occasionally, about 23% ignore them and 7% have turned them off. Bain: 46% of its
   respondents use AI overviews from search engines. "Search engines now summarize, and
   AI tools now search" is the article's own line. The results page is a generic mock.

5. **Younger people** (B05) — Bain: 76% of baby boomers and 55% of Gen X mostly or
   always use search engines, versus 42% for both millennials and Gen Z; "if habits
   follow age, the mix will keep shifting" is the article's own line.

6. **Trust** (B06) — Orbit: 49% agreed they trust Google's results more than AI chat;
   36% are unlikely to ever trust AI chat tools; 44% said AI has changed how they look
   for information online. The three gauges are one per figure, not a combined score.

7. **Being found** (B07) — the author's own observation (labeled "my own observation"):
   when the answer comes first and the websites second, being clear and easy to find
   matters more, for a hotel, a gallery, a shelter or a small shop. Orbit's author's list
   of what won't change (informative websites that help visitors, original research and
   genuine points of view, word of mouth, live events and communities, email,
   influencer marketing), then "the tools change; the goal of helping people who need
   you stays". The four icons are generic.

8. **The closing idea** (B08) — we now choose a tool depending on the question
   (directions to a coffee shop: search; a complicated idea in plain words or a trip
   planned around the family: ask the AI); typing keywords and scanning ten blue links is
   becoming one of several ways we look for answers. All the article's own.

9. **Sources note** (B09) — Orbit Media is a web design and SEO company and ran its own
   survey (the article adds: with QuestionPro, not narrated); the Future survey is from a
   media publisher; Bain's survey was conducted in September 2025; the surveys measure
   different things so their numbers should not be compared directly; the opening scene is
   an illustration.
