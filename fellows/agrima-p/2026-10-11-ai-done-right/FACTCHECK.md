# FACTCHECK — ai-done-right

Every claim traces to the user-supplied article, "AI Done Right: What
Nonprofits Built Around AI Can Teach Us About Trust, People, and Purpose,"
which cites three sources: Fast Forward (2026, Sept 15), Poynter / Nonprofit
Quarterly (2026), and The Bridgespan Group (2026, Sept 10). Per DOUBLE-CHECK
LAW, every percentage below is **self-reported by nonprofits that already use
AI** and is narrated with the article's own soft attribution ("seems to,"
"about half," "about one in four") and a plain statement of limits in B08.

1. **Most nonprofit AI stories are about saving time; a 2026 survey of 917
   nonprofit staff and leaders found 98% use AI in some way** (B01) — per the
   article, citing Poynter (2026) / Nonprofit Quarterly. The 98% is of
   *survey respondents*, not all nonprofits; the on-screen label says "of 917
   nonprofit staff and leaders ... (2026 survey)." The "faster emails,
   quicker grant drafts, less paperwork" framing is the article's own.

2. **AI-powered nonprofits build their whole service around AI; examples: a
   tool that helps courts record what is said, and a lesson planner inside
   WhatsApp that gives teachers time back** (B02) — per the article, citing
   Fast Forward (2026). The chat on screen is an illustrative mock-up, labeled
   "illustrative chat," not a real transcript.

3. **Adalat AI: slow paperwork was clogging courtrooms in India; a simple
   stenography tool now runs in 5,500 courtrooms across 11 states** (B03) —
   per the article, citing Fast Forward (2026). The courtroom-with-paperwork
   graphic is an illustration, not a depiction of a specific courtroom.
   "Start with the real problem, not the impressive technology" is the
   article author's own takeaway, presented as such.

4. **A September 2026 Fast Forward report surveyed 119 AI-powered nonprofits
   in 20 countries and interviewed 20 leaders** (B04) — per the article,
   citing Fast Forward (2026, Sept 15).

5. **92% said AI made their services more efficient; none said the risks
   outweigh the benefits** (B05) — per the article, citing Fast Forward
   (2026). Narrated "it seems to help," not as proof of effect: this is
   self-report from organizations that chose AI.

6. **88% worry about sharing wrong information; 84% about misusing personal
   data** (B05) — per the article, citing Fast Forward (2026).

7. **76% keep asking for feedback from the people they help; about half (51%)
   invite community members to help shape the tools; 85% say they are very
   open about using AI** (B06) — per the article, citing Fast Forward (2026).
   "Designing for you vs. designing with you" is the article author's own
   framing.

8. **90% have a plan to grow; only about 24% have what it takes; most say
   steady, flexible funding would help more than short project grants**
   (B07) — per the article, citing Fast Forward (2026). The "gap" arrow
   simply shows the difference between the two reported figures.

9. **Limits: answers are from nonprofits that already use AI, so they don't
   describe every nonprofit; the report was supported by Google.org;
   Bridgespan notes that skipping AI can be the right choice for some
   organizations** (B08) — per the article, citing Fast Forward (2026) and
   The Bridgespan Group (2026, Sept 10). "Self-reported" is the same limit
   stated plainly (the article's "answers from nonprofits that already use
   AI").

10. **Closing view: groups that seem to be doing it well start with a real
    problem, keep the people they serve involved, and are open about how they
    use AI; none of that needs a big budget, it mostly needs attention**
    (B09) — the article author's own conclusion, presented as such ("here's
    what I take from all of this"), not as a finding. The closing question
    ("Who is at the table with us?") is the host's reframing, not a claim.

## What's NOT claimed

No claim that AI "works" for nonprofits in general, that these percentages
describe the sector, or that any nonprofit should adopt AI. No named product
is endorsed. No outcomes beyond what the article states.
