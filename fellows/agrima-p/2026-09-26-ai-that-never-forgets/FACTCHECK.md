# FACTCHECK — ai-that-never-forgets

Every claim below traces to the user-supplied article, "The AI That Never
Forgets: How Marketing Is Learning to Read Your Mind, One Conversation at a
Time," which itself cites four sources. Per DOUBLE-CHECK LAW, claims sourced
to industry-analysis blogs (rather than independent research) are narrated
with soft attribution ("one analysis found...") rather than presented as
settled fact.

1. **"Cookies and retargeting are the old system"** (B01) — general,
   well-established mechanic of online advertising; not attributed to any of
   the article's four sources, doesn't need one.

2. **"Google's Gemini added ongoing memory into its assistant"** (B02) — per
   the article, citing Contrary Research (2026): "Google added this kind of
   ongoing personal memory into Gemini across Workspace and Android earlier
   this year." The video drops the "earlier this year" qualifier (dates the
   video) but keeps the claim itself.

3. **"ChatGPT automatically pulls facts from conversations and stores them"**
   (B02) — per the article, citing AI Magicx (2026): "ChatGPT now
   automatically pulls facts out of conversations and stores them in a memory
   bank it can draw on later." Kept close to verbatim mechanism, no invented
   detail.

4. **"~$5B market now, projected to nearly quadruple by 2030"** (B02) — per
   the article, citing Vellum (2026): market "valued at close to $5 billion
   this year," "projected to nearly quadruple by 2030." Vellum is an
   AI-tooling company publishing its own market analysis — a single-source,
   industry-analysis figure, not independently verified. Narrated as a
   market-size figure attributed to the analysis, not stated as an
   independently confirmed fact.

5. **"~47 minutes/day saved with persistent memory"** (B03) — per the
   article, citing AI Magicx (2026): "professionals who set up this kind of
   persistent memory saved close to 47 minutes a day compared with people
   starting from scratch in every conversation." Same publisher as claim 3 —
   an AI-tooling blog with a commercial interest in the technology it's
   measuring. Narrated explicitly as "one analysis found," not as a general
   claim about all AI memory users.

6. **"Privacy researchers describe this as an identity model, not
   personalization"** (B04) — per the article, citing Contrary Research
   (2026): memory "isn't only reacting to past behavior, it's building an
   ongoing picture of who someone is," and is "harder to audit, correct, or
   remove than a browsing history." Contrary Research is an independent
   research firm (not selling AI-memory tools), so this claim carries more
   weight than claims 4–5; still presented as a described framing
   ("researchers describe...") rather than an established fact.

7. **"Responsible tools give real view/edit/clear control"** (B05) — per the
   article, citing MemoryLake (2026), an AI-assistant review site. Presented
   as a description of what the more careful tools do, not a claim that every
   AI product currently offers this.

## What's NOT claimed

No specific company is named as doing memory retention badly; no claim that
any named product mishandles user data. The video does not take a side on
whether AI memory is net-good or net-bad — per the user's explicit request,
it presents both the upside and the tradeoff and stops short of a verdict.
