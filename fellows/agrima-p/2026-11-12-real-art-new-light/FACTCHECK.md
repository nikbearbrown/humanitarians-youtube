3. **The Night Watch** (B03) — Kemp (2021): in 1715 Rembrandt's painting was cut down
   on all four sides to fit a wall in Amsterdam's town hall, and the strips were never
   found; the Rijksmuseum used AI to imagine the edges, with a smaller 17th-century copy
   as a guide and three neural networks trained to match Rembrandt's style; the new
   sections were printed on canvas, varnished and mounted on metal frames around the
   painting for three months, as part of a restoration project launched in 2019; the
   original was not repainted. **From a wire report** — narration ("This comes from a
   wire report."), a source chip and the sources card say so. Narration says "mounted

   narrated. The director's "stepping back in time 300 years" remark is not used. The
   frame on screen is generic and labeled "(generic frame -- not the real painting)".

5. **MoMA** (B05) — Chan (2023): Refik Anadol's Unsupervised filled a 24-foot by
   24-foot lobby wall with moving imagery generated from 320,000 visual inputs from the
   museum's collection; sensors picked up light, visitors' movement and the weather; a
   team of 15 people shaped the results; Anadol describes it as a collaboration between
   people and machines. Alemdar (n.d.): it became the first generative AI artwork MoMA
   acquired — **TRT World shows no visible date** (said on the sources card). The
   article's own line "the real artworks were the raw material" closes the beat. The
   wall on screen is generated abstract colour, labeled "(generated abstract colour --
   not the real work)".

7. **Ideas anyone could borrow** (B07) — the author's own suggestions, not rules, not
