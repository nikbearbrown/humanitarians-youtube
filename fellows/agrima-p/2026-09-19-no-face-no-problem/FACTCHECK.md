# FACTCHECK — no-face-no-problem

Sourced from the user-supplied article "No Face, No Problem: Inside the
Rise of AI Accounts Nobody's Ever Seen — and Why We Might Trust Them
Anyway," which itself cites seven AI-content-tooling vendor blogs (listed
at the bottom). The article hedges several of its own claims, and this
build preserves those hedges rather than presenting anything as settled
fact.

1. **"Faceless accounts now make up a big share of new creator ventures
   launched this year"** (B02) — per the article's own framing, attributed
   to "trackers following creator monetization." **No independent data
   is cited** — the article's own reference list is entirely vendor blogs
   selling faceless-content tools (EasyViral, AutoFaceless, etc.), who have
   a direct commercial interest in this narrative being true. Narrated with
   the article's own hedge: "take that number with a grain of salt."

2. **"Blind listening tests suggest most people can't reliably tell an AI
   voice from a human one"** (B03) — per the article's own claim; no
   specific study, sample size, or methodology is named in the source
   article itself, so this is presented as a reported claim, not a cited
   statistic with a number attached.

3. **"Some platforms reportedly downrank AI voices that sound too cheap or
   robotic"** (B03) — the article's own word is "apparently" — carried
   into the narration as "reportedly," not stated as confirmed platform
   policy.

4. **Niche list and "some of the platforms' highest ad rates"** (B04) — per
   the article's own claim, unattributed to a specific study.

5. **"A majority of people say faceless content feels more trustworthy, not
   less"** (B05) — **the single claim this build treats with the most
   explicit on-screen skepticism.** The article itself flags this as
   coming from "sources tracking viewer behavior" that are "mostly...
   companies selling AI content tools, who obviously have a reason to want
   that conclusion to be true." This build carries that skepticism into
   BOTH the narration ("I'd take that stat with real skepticism") AND the
   visual — B05's Manim scene displays an explicit on-screen flag reading
   "sourced from AI-tool vendors — take with skepticism," not just a
   spoken caveat.

## What's NOT claimed as fact

This video does not present the "big share of new ventures" stat or the
"majority find it more trustworthy" stat as verified data — both are
carried with the article's own hedging intact. No specific percentages are
invented; the article gives none, so neither does this build.
