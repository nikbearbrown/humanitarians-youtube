# FACTCHECK — rescue-reinvented

Every statistic in this reel is sourced from the user-supplied article
"Rescue, Reinvented: How Artificial Intelligence Is Reshaping Rescue Work"
(pasted directly in the build request), which itself cites three named
sources. Per the DOUBLE-CHECK LAW, this toolkit did not independently
re-verify those underlying sources — every number below is attributed to
the article's own citation, not asserted as independently confirmed fact.

## Claims and their source (as cited in the article)

1. **"Recovery rates have gone up by as much as 25% in areas with active
   shelter participation."** (Finding Rover, facial-recognition matching)
   — DigitalDefynd (n.d.), *How is AI used to help animals?*
2. **"AI turning a volunteer's rushed notes into an adoption post, a
   social caption, and a donor update, all at once."** — Who Will Let the
   Dogs Out (n.d.), *How animal shelters can use AI to increase adoptions,
   awareness, fundraising, and community engagement*.
3. **Doobert coordinating foster placements, volunteer schedules, and
   transport relays.** — mentioned directly in the article's own text
   (no separate footnote in the source article beyond the general framing);
   presented as the article's own description of the tool, not independently
   verified feature-by-feature.
4. **"About 4.2 million shelter animals were adopted in 2025, and outcomes
   have meaningfully improved since 2019."** — ASPCA (n.d.), *U.S. animal
   shelter statistics*.
5. **"Shelter capacity remains stretched thin nationally, and a significant
   number of animals still don't find the outcome everyone would want for
   them."** — ASPCA (n.d.), same source.
6. **Recommendation for a "human fact-check" on AI-generated content.**
   — Who Will Let the Dogs Out (n.d.), same source as claim 2.

## What is NOT claimed

- No specific shelter names, individual outcomes, or per-shelter figures
  are asserted — only the aggregate/national statistics the article itself
  cites.
- The article's own hedge language is preserved rather than sharpened into
  false precision — "as much as 25%" (a ceiling, not an average) and "a
  significant number" (not a specific count) are both carried through
  narration exactly as the article frames them.
- No claim in this reel asserts AI "solved" shelter capacity problems —
  B05 explicitly states the honest, ongoing gap alongside the wins,
  matching the article's own explicit framing ("I don't think that reality
  should be hidden behind the good news... but I also don't think it should
  overshadow it").
