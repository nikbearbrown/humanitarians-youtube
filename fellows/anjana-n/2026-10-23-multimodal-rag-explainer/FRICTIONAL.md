## 1. Focus
- Explaining to a general audience why AI search struggles with tables, charts, and code, and how that can be fixed.

## 2. What was tried, and what was expected
- **Example document:** the original brief described it separately in each section where it appears.
- **Expectation:** the brief's colors and script could be used as written.
- **Length:** each section was expected to stay within the target length.

## 3. Points of resistance, and next steps taken

### Keeping the example consistent
- **The problem:** the same example document appears in four sections in a row.
- **The fix:** the example was defined once and reused everywhere, so every section shows the same figures.

### Showing two different kinds of failure
- **The problem:** text-only systems fail in two ways.
  - A table is still found but arrives as unreadable text.
  - A chart is lost entirely.
- **The decision:** the video keeps these as separate failures. The first one is harder to notice, which is why it matters.

### Making clear that nothing is thrown away
- **The problem:** converting tables, charts, and code into text could look like losing the original content.
- **The fix:** the video shows that the original is kept. The text description is only used to find it.

### Acknowledging other approaches
- **The problem:** the video presents one common approach, but another is also widely used.
- **The decision:** the video doesn't claim its approach is the only one, and the alternative is noted in the production records.

### Sections ran long, and the ending
- **Length:** all five main sections went over the target word count. The script was kept as written, since everything it lists also appears on screen.
- **Ending:** the brief ended with a fade to black, which would have left a blank screen. The video now ends on the title card.
