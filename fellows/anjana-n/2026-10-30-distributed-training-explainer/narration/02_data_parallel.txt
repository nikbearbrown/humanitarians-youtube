Data parallelism is the most common strategy. Every GPU holds a complete copy of the model. The training dataset is split into different mini-batches — each GPU processes its own batch independently. After the forward and backward passes, each GPU has computed gradients from its own slice of data. The gradients are then averaged across all GPUs so every copy of the model updates identically. The result: each GPU sees different data, but they all stay synchronized. Training time scales nearly linearly with the number of GPUs.
