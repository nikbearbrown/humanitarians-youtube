# Beat 4 — Two-Level Grading

**Visual type:** Remotion
**Duration:** ~12 seconds

## What the viewer sees

A two-tier diagram stacked vertically, building from top to bottom.

**Level 1 (0-5s):**
Top tier appears first.

Left node: "Prediction" card in gold ("raised, 0.73")
Right node: "Actual Extraction" card in blue ("raised")
Arrow connects them left to right.
Green checkmark between them.

Label above the tier: "Level 1: Did we predict the guidance?"

**Level 2 (5-9s):**
Bottom tier appears below.

Left node: "Extraction" card in blue ("raised")
Right node: "Market Outcome" card in white ("+3.2% excess return")
Arrow connects them left to right.
Green checkmark between them.

Label above the tier: "Level 2: Did the guidance predict the stock?"

A vertical connecting line links Level 1's extraction card down to Level 2's extraction card. The chain is visible: Prediction → Guidance → Market.

**The divergence case (9-12s):**
The two tiers briefly flash to a different example:

Level 1: Predicted raised, actual raised. Green checkmark. Correct.
Level 2: Raised guidance, stock returned -1.4%. Red X. Wrong.

Green on top, red on bottom. The two levels are independent. A prediction can be right about the words and wrong about the money.

Label: "Two questions. Two scorecards. Both honest."

## Technical notes

- The two-tier vertical layout is the signature visual of this beat
- Gold (prediction) at the top, blue (extraction) in the middle, white (market) at the bottom
- The chain Prediction → Guidance → Market should be visually clear as a vertical flow
- The divergence case is brief (3 seconds) but critical for the insight
- Keep it clean, no clutter around the tiers
