Once the inputs are selected, they flow through gated residual networks — the TFT's core processing block. Each GRN applies a nonlinear transformation, then passes the result through a gating layer. The gate is a learned sigmoid that controls how much of the transformation to keep. If the transformation is useful, the gate opens. If it isn't, the gate closes and the original input passes through on a skip connection, unchanged. This means the network can add complexity where it helps and stay simple where it doesn't. The skip connection guarantees that at worst, the block does nothing — it can never make the signal worse than what came in.
