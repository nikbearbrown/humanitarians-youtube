This is why the TFT outperforms LSTMs on longer horizons. An LSTM compresses the entire history into a fixed-size hidden state. That works for one or two steps ahead, but five or ten steps out, too much information is lost to compression. The TFT avoids this in three ways. Variable selection keeps irrelevant inputs from entering. Gated residual networks prevent noise from propagating through the layers. And attention lets the model skip directly to the historical time steps that matter, no matter how far back they are. The result is a probabilistic forecast — not just a point estimate, but a full distribution at every horizon — that holds up where recurrent models fall apart.
