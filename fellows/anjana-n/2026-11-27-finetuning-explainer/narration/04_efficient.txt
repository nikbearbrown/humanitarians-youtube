Full fine-tuning updates every parameter. For a seven-billion-parameter model, that's expensive — you need to store gradients and optimizer states for all of them. Parameter-efficient methods skip that. LoRA freezes the original weights entirely and inserts small trainable adapter matrices beside a few key layers. Instead of updating seven billion parameters, you train maybe ten million — a fraction of a percent. The adapters learn a small adjustment on top of the frozen weights. The result is nearly as good as full fine-tuning at a fraction of the memory and compute cost. QLoRA goes further by quantizing the frozen weights to four bits, shrinking the memory footprint even more.
