## Friction protected

**KEPT — the blueprint is the beat the series exists for.** B10 is the only beat
in three parts that tells the viewer what to *do* rather than what something *is*.
It could have been cut as "just a checklist"; instead it gets the longest body
beat and the terracotta accent lands on step three — naming the cost/latency/
accuracy trade-off out loud — because that is the step that reads as senior and
the one candidates skip. The reel earns this beat by having spent twenty patterns
establishing that every choice costs something.

**KEPT — the thesis inverts the series.** Parts 1 and 2 taught patterns that add
capability. This one opens by saying these seven *take capability away on
purpose*, and B01's correction performs it: "features" is typed, then replaced
with "governors". A viewer who has watched two parts expecting more power gets
told the opposite, which is uncomfortable and correct.

**KEPT — "unusable for anything interactive" (B05).** Tree of Thoughts is the
most impressive pattern in the set and the least shippable. Saying so in the same
breath as the chess analogy is the honest version; omitting it is how people put
a reasoning search behind a chat box and wonder why it times out.

**KEPT — B11, arguing against the reel for the third time.** "A priority queue
holding three tasks is a list" is deliberately blunt. The series has now closed
every part by naming when its own content does not apply.

**CUT — the priority formula.** The source gives
`Score = (Value · TimeSensitivity) / (Effort · Risk)`. B08 teaches the four
*inputs* instead. Same call as Part 2's retrieval-score and σ-threshold cuts: at
~21s a beat, an equation consumes the beat and teaches less than the sentence.
Consistency across the series matters more than completeness in one beat.

**CUT — BFS/DFS search algorithms (§P16).** Extraneous at this length; the
branch-score-prune idea lands without naming traversal orders. Incidentally this
avoids propagating the source's typo ("Breaded-First Search").

**CUT — named model families.** The source names "o1/o3-class" for the expensive
tier; on screen it is "Reasoning · expensive". The tier is the lesson, not the
vendor, and model names date a video within months.

**CUT — the airport-security and corporate-email analogies.** Both are fine in
prose and redundant on screen: the perimeter diagram and the topology fan already
show what the analogy would explain. An analogy that duplicates the visual is
narration spent twice.

**SOFTENED — "over 50% cost reduction" → "can halve the bill."** The source
asserts it flatly with no checkable citation. Stated conditionally instead.
