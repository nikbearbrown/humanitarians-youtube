State Is The Hard Part — Agentic Design Patterns, Part 2

Part 1 gave you six patterns for a single agent. Now add a second agent, a
memory that has to persist, and an API call that fails at 3am.

Almost none of the hard problems are about the agents. They're about the state
between them — who holds it, how it decays, who may write to it, and what breaks
when it fails. Seven patterns (7–13 of 20): orchestration, memory, and
fail-safes.

Concepts from "Agentic Design Patterns: A Hands-On Guide to Building
Intelligent Systems" by Antonio Gulli.

THE PROMPT FROM THIS EPISODE
Take the multi-agent or long-running AI system I am building. For each of these
seven patterns — multi-agent collaboration, memory, learning, goal monitoring,
exception handling, human-in-the-loop, RAG — tell me whether I already have it,
whether I need it yet, and what specific failure would show up first if I skip
it. Then name the one piece of state most likely to corrupt, and how you would
protect it.

That last question is the one worth running — it turns a checklist into a risk
assessment of your own architecture.
