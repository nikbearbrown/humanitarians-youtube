**SCENE 2 — THE FRAMEWORK**
**VISUAL** — A Brutalist 3D axis grid snaps in.
`X = ANIMAL | Y = DOMESTIC | Z = SIZE`
**VO:**
This mapping is called a Vector Embedding. Instead of storing a word as a string of letters, we store it as a list of numbers representing its location in space. In this space, distance equals meaning. The coordinate for "dog" is mathematically grouped near the coordinate for "wolf," but extremely far away from the coordinate for "car."

Because meaning is now spatial, we can do math on vocabulary. If you take the numerical vector for "King," subtract the numerical vector for "Man," and add the vector for "Woman," the resulting coordinate lands exactly on the word "Queen." The AI isn't thinking; it is just calculating the geometric distance between concepts.

**SCENE 4 — FALSIFIABILITY**
**VISUAL** — The axis grid returns. The vectors blur and overlap.
**ON SCREEN:** `DIMENSIONAL COLLAPSE`
**VO:**
Where do embeddings fail? Nuance and dimensionality limits. If your vector space only has a few hundred dimensions, words with multiple meanings—like "bank" as a river and "bank" as a financial institution—collapse into the exact same coordinate. The system loses the context because the mathematical space isn't dense enough to hold it.
