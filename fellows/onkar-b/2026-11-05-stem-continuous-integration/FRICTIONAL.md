# Frictional log — Continuous Integration

**What I was working on.** A STEM explainer defining Continuous Integration and its specific application to AI engineering.

**What I tried, and what I expected.** 
- I initially wrote the script focusing purely on DevOps tools like Jenkins, Travis CI, and GitHub Actions, explaining the differences between them.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.** 
- Listing DevOps tools turned the script into a boring software catalog. It didn't explain the *why*, and it completely lacked a connection to AI.
- I pivoted to focus on "Integration Hell"—the historical problem CI was invented to solve. Then, I tailored Scene 4 specifically to AI, framing CI as the ultimate defense against model drift and hallucinations. 

**What Claude contributed, and what I did with it.** 
- Mine: The focus on the automated gauntlet (linting, tests, security).
- Claude's: Reframing Scene 4 to focus on the non-deterministic nature of AI. Pointing out that AI requires CI precisely because its outputs shift unpredictably was a much stronger thesis.

**What I understand now, and what I still do not.**
- Understood: When explaining DevOps concepts, focusing on the pain they alleviate (broken servers, lost weekends, unpredictable AI) is always more engaging than explaining the syntax of the tools themselves.
