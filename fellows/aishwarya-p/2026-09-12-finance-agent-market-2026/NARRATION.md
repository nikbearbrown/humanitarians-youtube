## B00 — Cold open
Eighty-seven percent of CFOs say AI is essential to finance operations this year. Most of them haven't moved past experimenting with it.

## B01 — Two real kinds of agent
Method: the market splits into two real categories. Assistive agents surface insights and suggest next steps, but wait for a human to approve before acting. Autonomous agents complete a workflow on their own, inside guardrails — expense approvals, vendor payments, compliance checks — without someone in the loop for every step.

## B02 — What's actually shipping
Prophix released a second wave of agents this spring: an Architect Agent that turns customer data into a validated financial model in hours instead of weeks, and a Consolidation Agent that answers questions about audit trail data in plain language — built to only answer when the underlying report data actually supports it. Sana's agents handle FP&A support, vendor management, and compliance documentation with real audit trails. Experian launched an Agent Operating System this year for financial services broadly, built around a real, global survey of over eight hundred senior decision-makers.

## B03 — The honest line in the middle of it
Prophix's own CEO put it plainly: ninety-nine percent accuracy is zero percent trust. The real differentiator these vendors are all converging on isn't raw output quality — it's whether the agent can show its work, and whether it knows the limits of what it can actually answer.

## B04 — The real, still-open gap
Deloitte's number again: eighty-seven percent say it's essential, most haven't moved past experimenting. The tools exist. The gap between having them and trusting them enough to hand over real decisions hasn't closed yet.

## B05 — Handoff
Your turn. Before adopting any finance agent, ask it the same question twice on data it shouldn't have enough evidence to answer confidently — see whether it says so, or guesses anyway.
