**Result A — Built the research foundation through a 6-week literature review.** I reviewed
six papers on uncertainty in environmental assessment and AI. I started with uncertainty
frameworks and typologies (Bevan 2022; Skinner et al. 2014), then identifying and
prioritizing uncertainty (Skinner et al. 2017, UnISERA). Next came epistemic uncertainty and
Bayesian networks (Sahlin et al. 2021) and how uncertainty carries through an assessment
(Reichert 2020), and I finished with uncertainty quantification in deep learning (Abdar et al.
2021). Every week records what I did and where I got stuck. Three findings shape the research
direction: uncertainty can't be reduced to a single confidence score; confidence in a
conclusion is not the same as how reliable the evidence is; and confidence generated by AI
doesn't necessarily reflect how good the environmental evidence is.

## 6. Next steps (plan, deliverables, target dates)

1. **Uncertainty framework for AI-driven ESG analysis — by mid-Dec 2026.** Turn the
   six-paper review into a framework that sorts environmental ESG uncertainty into several
   types: data, model, language, extrapolation and decision uncertainty.
2. **Evidence-reliability criteria — by late Dec 2026.** Define how to judge whether an
   uncertainty actually changes an ESG conclusion, keeping "confidence in the conclusion"
   separate from "reliability of the evidence."
3. **Prototype — by mid-Jan 2027.** An AI analysis of an environmental ESG report that gives
   a confidence level together with how reliable the evidence behind it is, instead of a
   single score.
4. **Evaluation and write-up — by 31 Jan 2027.** Check whether the prototype's confidence
   lines up with the quality of the underlying evidence, and write up the results.
5. **Ongoing.** Each week: a research-progress video, a STEM explainer (16:9 + 9:16), 4 fellow
   video reviews, and an update to the frictional log.
