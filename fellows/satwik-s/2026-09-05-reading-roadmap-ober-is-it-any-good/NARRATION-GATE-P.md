| **B02** | what OBER is | The study is OBER. Deployed, randomized — about five thousand seven hundred learners, two weeks, three ways to recommend the same lessons: a fixed expert-ordered path, a "people-like-you" engagement recommender, and a knowledge-based one. Same learners, same content — three orders. |
| **B03** | job one · evidence | Job one, evidence. The engagement recommender won engagement — the most sessions. But the fixed, structured path won mastery — the highest test scores, zero-point-four-six to zero-point-three-nine. And click-through? Flat — about zero-point-three for all three. The metric everyone optimizes couldn't even tell them apart. Clicking wasn't learning — and structure, not engagement, moved it. That's the direction our bet rides on. |
| **B04** | job two · blueprint (+ positioning) | Job two, the blueprint. Structure first — then personalize inside it. And here's the gift: OBER's winning order was fixed, one-size-for-all, and both personalized versions lost. Its authors name the open problem themselves — marry expert structure with personalization. That hybrid is exactly the roadmap: keep the dependency graph that drove mastery, add the project-level ordering OBER's arms never had. Keep what it rewards, avoid what it warns against, fill what it leaves open. |
| **B05** | job three · instrument | Job three, the instrument — the part to actually take. OBER measures learning without a separate study: put outcomes and quiz items into the data, then read mastery straight from the logs. So our three arms — chapter order, plain search, and the roadmap — get scored on the same signal. With one fix OBER missed: a delayed check days later, so we're measuring learning that lasts, not just the moment. |
| **B06** | the job it can't do · not proof (falsifiability) | And the job it can't do — proof. This is one preprint, on a prayer app, with no significance tests, and its "mastery" is in-app quizzes, not lasting learning. So it's safe to say the engagement winner wasn't the learning winner — it is not safe to say it proves our system works. It doesn't. We've run no learning study of our own. Yet. |
| **B07** | verdict | So — where it stands. One study, used honestly, does four jobs: it points our direction, hands us a blueprint and the hybrid to fill, gives us a way to measure, and places us against the field. What it never does is prove us right. That's the next study — and now we know how to run it. |
