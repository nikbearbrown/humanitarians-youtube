## The series arc, stated plainly

5: gates that could not fail. 6: a status not earned. 7: a checker not opening
the files. 8: a denominator counting what could never fail. 9: a record naming
no artifacts. 10: a count mostly not of defects. 11: documentation that was
executed. **12: a map every row of which was true.**

This is the purest form the series has found. In each earlier episode something
was wrong — a gate, a status, a skip list, a denominator. Here **nothing was
wrong.** Every row accurate, every description correct, and the document still
sent a reader past a thousand files. The defect was entirely in what was absent,
and absence leaves no line to audit.

## The risk, and what answers it

An episode whose finding is "the documentation was incomplete" sounds minor. Two
things give it weight:

- **The consequence is named precisely.** An agent treating that file as its
  instructions would never load ten subsystems, and the repository's own verify
  command has never walked them. That is measurable, and B04 and B05 measure it.
- **B07 refuses the easy close.** The commit could have marked everything
  reviewed and looked finished. It recorded `reviewed: false` instead, and the
  beat argues that this is the harder and better choice.
