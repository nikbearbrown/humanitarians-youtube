# PROMPTS — Likely False, Likely True

No image or video generation was used. Manim + Remotion only, so there are no
model prompts to record. GATE F requires this file; its honest content is that
nothing was generated.

## Stills

None. No archival fetch, no `pantry/`, no Ken Burns beats. Every frame is drawn.
The funnel in B07 is a drawn schematic of publication selection, not a chart of
data, and is not presented as one.

## Higgsfield

Not used, not installed, not offered. Fellow Tier build, entirely free: Kokoro
for narration, Manim and Remotion for picture.

## The on-screen prompts that ARE in the reel

Written, not generated, and shown to the viewer as content inside the composer:

**B00** — the topic question

    claude "can you trust a published trading signal? two papers, opposite
            conclusions - why?"

**B09** — the scaffolded task handed to the viewer

    claude "for this result: how many variants were tried, what counts as a
            failure here, and at what threshold would it stop being
            interesting?"

The B09 prompt asks three things, and the first is the one that usually has no
answer written down anywhere — which is the episode's finding, not a rhetorical
flourish. The GOOD/BAD discriminator sets the bar deliberately low: an
approximate trial count is worth more than a precise t-statistic with no
denominator behind it.

## What this reel does not do

It gives no trading instruction and names no instrument. Both prompts are about
reading a claim, not about acting on one.
