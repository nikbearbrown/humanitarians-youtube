## How the category was established

The commissioned phrase was treated as a question rather than a premise. Two
searches were run. The first, for energy recycling in photonic computing,
returned almost entirely papers about **reducing phase-shifter and data-movement
power** - real work, but not about reclaiming light. The second, narrowed to
optical power recovery and recirculation, surfaced the amplifier, the
photon-recycling accelerator, and recirculating mesh architectures.

That the first search missed the actual results is itself reported, in B06: the
phrase attaches most readily to work that is not about recycling light at all.

## Sources that were available and refused

- **Stanford's news page** for this work returned HTTP 403 and was never read.
- **Aggregator and news restatements** were available and not used. They
  disagree with the abstract in a way that matters: one gives the amplification
  as "about 100 times", where the abstract says "more than 17 dB" - roughly
  50-fold in power. Rather than reconcile two numbers from a secondary source,
  the reel quotes the abstract and uses dB only.

## Why B07 carries no numbers

The photon-recycling accelerator's abstract contains no numeric efficiency,
gradient or pass count - only qualitative claims - and it is a proposal rather
than a fabricated device. The recirculating mesh paper's figures were not
verified from a primary read. Both are named so the category is complete; neither
supports the verdict, and the beat says so on screen.
