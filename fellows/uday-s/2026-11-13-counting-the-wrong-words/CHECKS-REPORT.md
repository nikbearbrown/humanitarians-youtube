## Scope decision, recorded before anything was built

**Not investment advice.** No instrument, no strategy, no performance figure,
and no claim that sentiment predicts prices. B07 is the beat that enforces this
inside the argument rather than only in the paperwork: it states plainly that a
better lexicon settles measurement and says nothing about prediction.

## How the commissioned topic was handled

The brief asked how to do market sentiment analysis **for predicting stocks**.
That is two requests. The reel answers the first properly and declines the
second on the evidence:

- The **method** is four steps, three of which are engineering. B01 says so and
  moves on, because a tutorial would be filler.
- The **measurement decision** inside it is where a large, published, fixable
  failure lives — and that is the body of the reel.
- The **prediction claim** is separated out in B07 and not made.

That is a more useful answer than a tutorial, and it is the one the sources
support.

## The risk, and what answers it

- **Sounding like a debunk.** B06 is the counterweight: the fix is real, it
  worked, and it cost domain attention rather than compute. The excitement line
  in B08 is specific about that.
- **Sounding like a tutorial that promises prediction.** B07 prevents it, and
  the verdict's citation strip carries the disclaimer on screen rather than
  leaving it to this file.
