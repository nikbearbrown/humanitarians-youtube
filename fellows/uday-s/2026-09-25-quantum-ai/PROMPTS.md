# PROMPTS — The Arrow Points The Other Way

## Open slots: none

## The ask that saved this one from being wrong

The reel was commissioned from a concept summary that read well and contained a
claim the field does not support. The ask that caught it:

```
claude "treat this brief as a claim set, not a script. For each sentence:
is it supported, overstated, or backwards? Cite what you check it against."
```

Two claims were rewritten, one was reversed in direction, and one survived. The
surviving one — drug discovery and materials science — is now the beat with the
strongest reasoning in the reel, because checking it turned up *why* it
survives: the simulated system is itself quantum, which is the one case that
was never dequantized.

**Never build a video from a brief without checking the brief.**

## The ask that found the story

```
claude "for this field, what is the most cited RESULT, as opposed to the most
cited claim? And which direction does that result actually run?"
```

The most cited claim is quantum accelerating AI. The most cited *result* is
AlphaQubit — AI accelerating quantum. The inversion is the whole video, and it
came from asking for a result rather than a promise.

## Handling the field's most misread number

The 5-minutes-vs-10²⁵-years benchmark is true and routinely deceptive: it
measures random circuit sampling, chosen because classical machines find it
hard, not because anyone wants the output. It appears on screen inside a caveat
card. **When a number is famous, check what it measures before you use it — and
if it needs a caveat, put the caveat on screen, not in your narration only.**

scaffolded task. Every number carries its source on screen."
