# FACTCHECK — Twenty Percent, Or Five

Commissioned topic: **"AI for learning Music"**, retitled under the standing
permission to make the title professional and correct.

Single primary source: Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014), *Psychological
Science*. Read locally as a PDF; every figure below was located in the paper's
own text, not in coverage of it.

## The claim the reel refuses to make

The circulating version of this result is **"practice explains 21%, so talent is
the other 79%"**. The paper does not say that. It says the remaining variance is
*unexplained*.

B07 states this on screen, with the word "talent" crossed and the word
"unexplained" left standing. This is the same discipline as the earlier reel that
caught a *word counts* result being retold as *distinct words*: where the popular
reading is weaker than the paper, the paper's reading goes on screen.

---

## The limit that cuts against the reel's own argument

B07 leads with it, before the other three:

> This is variance **between people**. It is not a statement about what your next
> hour of practice buys you. The within-person question is a different question
> and this design cannot answer it.

The reel's spine — "the 5% row is your app's row" — is a claim about how the
app's data would behave as a *measurement instrument in this kind of study*. It
is not a prediction about an individual learner, and B06 and B07 both say so.

Also stated in B07: the design is correlational, and expert samples carry
restriction of range, which pushes correlations down.

---

## The link to the tool, and its limits

No product is named, tested or rated. The connection made is structural and
narrow: a practice app that timestamps every minute is, as a measurement method,
the **log method** rather than a retrospective interview or questionnaire. That
is the only bridge the reel builds, and B06 states it as a classification, not as
a finding about any app.

The reel does **not** claim:

- that AI practice tools are ineffective (the paper's conclusion is that
  deliberate practice is important);
- that logged practice *causes* smaller gains — the moderator is about how
  practice was **measured**, not about how it was done;
- anything about AI feedback quality, pedagogy, or outcomes versus a teacher.

---

## Scope decision, recorded before anything was built

The brief was "AI for learning Music". A tutorial on practice apps would be
filler and would date in a month. The reel instead answers the question underneath
it — what does the evidence say about the quantity these tools measure — and names
the measurement result that applies directly to them. That is the more useful
answer and it is the one with a primary source behind it.
