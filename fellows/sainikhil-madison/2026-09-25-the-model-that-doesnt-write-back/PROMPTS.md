# PROMPTS — The Model That Doesn't Write Back

**There are no open generation slots.** Eight beats are registered Remotion
compositions; three are photo plates of real, freely licensed photographs. No
image model, no stock purchase, no Higgsfield clip, no Jev API call — Jev needs
an account and spends money, and this is a Fellow Tier build end to end.

## B00 — the on-screen typed ask (typed, not spoken)

> Everyone is talking about Jev. I've used ChatGPT, but I've never heard of TypeSafe. Explain Jev to me gently: what it does, what it doesn't do, and why developers are excited.

## B09 — the handoff prompt (typed AND discussed in narration)

> Here's one decision my code makes today, with an if-statement or a chatbot call. Help me split it into Jev-sized questions — yes/no, choice or score — tell me which parts should stay plain code, and how I'd check that its probabilities match reality on my own data.

## The executed scripts

| Script | Produces | Deps |
|---|---|---|
| `evidence/cost.py` | B05's last row ($21.00) and the Doom-demo consistency check | stdlib |
| `evidence/calibration.py` | B04's "82 of 100" — a constructed, seeded illustration of the definition, not a Jev measurement | stdlib |
| `evidence/commons_search.py`, `commons_fetch.py` | the ten photographs and their license sidecars | stdlib + network |

## Why there is no generated image anywhere

Every picture is a real photograph or painting with a known author and license,
credited on screen. A generated picture of "an AI deciding" would be the one
image in the film that shows nothing that exists.
