# Frictional log — Madison: scoping update

2026-09-16 — making the Madison scoping progress update

* Video / Drive: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1fJAA92dC6Rvwrlc5mhOTIM0Pl8g6m9sp?usp=drive_link

What I was working on. A ~190-second progress update on Madison, the Jungian
brand-archetype detector, at the point where the project had a scope and nothing
else. What the twelve archetypes are, why most archetype tools are shallow, the
three things this one does differently, and the Ruler-first decision for luxury
branding. Fourteen beats on the Brutalist pipeline, reusing the components built
for the function-calling video.

What I tried, and what I expected.

* I expected the main risk to be making an unfinished project sound finished.
* I expected the twelve archetypes to be straightforward to put on screen.
* I expected reuse-only to make this build quicker than the first.

Where it resisted, and what I did next.

* The honesty problem was real and structural, not a matter of tone. A progress
  update invites the reading that something got built. I handled it by making it
  impossible to misread: the cold open's output lines state "scoped — not
  built", the one scored output on screen is captioned a design target rather
  than a result, the tension example is captioned illustrative, and a dedicated
  status beat carries two deliberately negative items — "build not started" and
  "no results yet". The closing artifact page ends on the same status so the
  summary cannot be excerpted as a capability claim.
* Twelve archetypes would not fit the chip grid. Four columns overflowed the
  frame edge, which is a hard blocker; three columns fit the width but the
  fourth row collided with the caption slot. Resolved by dropping the caption
  and moving the clarifier into the spark line.
* The build was refused by a lint I had not met before: this channel has a fixed
  series name as its slot-one kicker, so the per-video topic belongs in the
  segment slot instead. That is a channel rule, not a bug, and it was right to
  stop me.
* The executive-summary beat was cut off mid-word — the typing ran past the end
  of its own audio, so the sentence never completed on screen. The typing is
  paced in real milliseconds rather than as a fraction of the beat, so it has to
  be tuned to fit the measured narration. Verified by sampling the final frame
  rather than trusting the gate.
* The verdict page had the same empty-numbered-line defect as the first video —
  a blank spacer in a list that numbers every entry renders as an orphan
  number. Fixed in both.

What Claude contributed, and what I accepted, changed or rejected.

* The project substance is mine — the archetype framework, the critique of
  existing tools, the three design commitments and the Ruler-first scope. Claude
  drafted the beat sheet and narration from that and built the reel.
* Accepted: the register choice. This channel's register explains the mechanism
  and stops, rather than judging the design, which suits a progress update and
  keeps me from editorialising about other people's tools.
* Accepted: reuse-only. No new components, which made the render materially
  faster than the first video.
* Changed: rewrote the cold open with my own introduction and corrected the
  handle to @HumanitariansAI after review.
* Rejected: naming a real brand for the tension example. A real brand would
  imply the detector had analysed it, which would be false. The example is
  constructed and labelled as such.

What I understand now, and what I still do not.

* Understood: the useful output of archetype work is not the label. A single
  label actively hides the thing worth knowing — that a brand's copy and its
  pricing can point at two different archetypes, and the gap between them is
  what costs trust.
* Understood about reporting: stating what does not exist is the part that makes
  a progress update trustworthy. Three of the five status items in this video
  are negative, on purpose.
* Not understood: whether a brand's published copy actually carries enough
  archetype signal to score reliably. That is the open question the whole
  project rests on, and it cannot be answered without sample data.
