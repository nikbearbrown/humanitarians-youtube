Bulk Ingestion at Scale — Week 2 of the Private AI Valuation Agent

Last week I proved by hand that SEC filings give you a share price for companies that have no public price. This week I scaled it from one company to fourteen quarters — and the machine immediately found three things my hand-check was too small to catch.

What's in this video:
• The scale — 14 quarters of SEC bulk N-PORT, about 6 GB. 80,571,213 individual holdings filter down to 22,041,937 private positions, then to 5,806 marks on the companies I track. Every stage reconciles against the stage above it.
• The payoff — a price history for Anthropic rebuilt entirely from filings. 33 observation dates, from $11.79 a share in 2023 to $333.60 this April. And the shape is a staircase: the price sits flat for months, then jumps, because these marks only move when a funding round reprices them.
• Seven independent managers — BlackRock, Capital Group, Coatue, Fidelity, JPMorgan, New York Life and T. Rowe Price all report $259.14, to the cent, on the same date. 24 separate registrations, but only 7 actual decision-makers. Seven is the honest number.
• The problem — the $589 mark I verified by hand last week isn't in this data at all. The bulk archive is organized by when a fund FILES, not by the period the filing covers, and funds file about 56 days late. So the "Q2" archive only reaches 30 April. The whole thing sits two months further behind than its own label suggests.
• Two traps, both caught before they did damage — four of Fidelity's funds file under a name that never says "Fidelity", so my code counted one manager as five. And one SpaceX filing prices common stock at $112 and preferred at $1,120: exactly ten times apart, inside a single document, 309 times across the panel, and no other company in the data does it at all.

Next week: the live filings, because that's now the only way to reach anything recent.

Every number here traces to a named filing accession or a reproducible query against the public SEC bulk data set. This video makes no valuation claim about any company — a price funds mark a position at is not what a company is worth. And it makes no trading claim: the whole middle of the video is about why this data cannot reach anything recent.
