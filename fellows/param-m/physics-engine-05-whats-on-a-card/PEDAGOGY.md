## Learning outcome

After watching, a viewer with no physics or coding background can:

1. **Name** what a formula card holds: equations, required knowns, what it solves
   for, units, applicability, pitfalls.
2. **Explain** why the conditions travel with the formula (they become the
   solution's stated assumptions).
3. **Describe** how new cards are built and checked against real benchmark answers,
   and where the library is still thin.

Measurable: given any formula from an AI, the viewer asks when it applies and what
the usual mistake is.

## Audience

Students and general viewers. Defined on first use: *formula card* (B01), *known*,
*spring constant* (B02), *applicability / assumption* (B03), *pitfall*, *wave number*
(B04), *benchmark* (B05), *symbol collision* (B06).
