Building the Marks Panel and the Split Detector — Private AI Valuation Agent, week 7

This week the project finally produces a per-share price history: value divided by share count,
once per security, per fund, per quarter. 5,479 marks.

That part is one line of arithmetic. The week went into the thing that stops a stock split
entering the series as a crash — and into finding out that my detector was too narrow to catch
the case it was written for.

WHERE EVERY MARK CAME FROM. 5,806 filed holdings go in. 28 were rejected last month as
belonging to nothing. 72 sit on filings a later amendment replaced. The remaining 5,706
aggregate into marks. 5,806 − 28 − 72 = 5,706. It reconciles exactly, and nothing is dropped
for being awkward.

THE BUG. Perplexity went from $695.04 a share to $58.26. Read naively that is a 92% collapse.
It is a split. My detector used a window of ±0.02 around a whole number, and Perplexity's ratio
is 11.93 — 0.07 away from 12, which the window misses by a factor of three. It had looked
correct only because a second Perplexity step landed on exactly 10.0000, inside any window.
The detector was not broken. It was too narrow to catch its own test case. The rule is now a
relative 1% window, imported by both the detector and last month's review queue.

CATCHING IT IS NOT DECIDING IT. Three companies threw a suspected split, and the ratio cannot
tell you which is which. The share count can. Perplexity's preferred line went from 19,395
shares to 193,950 while the filed value stayed at $13,488,132.50 — identical to the cent. That
is a split. Anthropic held the same 89,078 shares in all thirteen quarters; only the value
moved, and the step reverses the next quarter, which a split never does. SpaceX the same:
constant share count, value exactly doubled. Splits move the share count. Repricings move the
value. A person made all three calls, and the reason is written down on every one.

RATIO IS NOT FACTOR. That 11.93 is not a split factor. It is a 10-for-1 split and a 16%
markdown that landed in the same quarter. Divide by 11.93 and you erase a real price move.
Divide by 10 and it survives. So the panel stores what the machine measured and what a person
decided to divide by as separate fields.

THE CHECKS. The plan listed seven for this panel. Six pass. The seventh needs filings the SEC
has not published yet, and it reports that rather than quietly passing. One of the six expected
four managers to agree on Anthropic's price; ten do, in two clusters four thousandths of a
dollar apart.

To be clear about what this is and is not: the splits are detected, quarantined and decided —
not adjusted. Applying the factor is next month's work, and Perplexity's seven marks stay
blocked from every change series until then. And a "mark" is one fund's recorded price for one
security at one quarter end. Not a valuation of the company. This project publishes what funds
wrote down, and nothing more.
