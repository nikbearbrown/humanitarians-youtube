# PEDAGOGY — joining-filed-rounds-to-fund-entry-dates (week 9)
*Joining Filed Rounds to Fund Entry Dates — Week 9 progress update · ai-explainer / claude-hai*

Eighth episode of the Private AI Valuation Agent series. Same chassis, same channel, same
persistent voice as weeks 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. Source: `narration_script.md`
(author-written, 3:00 target) plus `README.md`'s figure-to-beat map.

**This is a join episode.** Weeks 1–7 built one panel out of one source; week 8 measured it.
This week two new filings arrive beside it, and the entire argument is about what the join runs
on. The failure mode of a join episode is making the result look easy, so the cut spends its
first two body beats on why the obvious join is wrong before it shows a single thing the right
join bought.

**The structure inverts week 8's rhythm on purpose.** Week 8 was finding-then-limit three times
over. Week 9 front-loads the obstacle: two beats of why the naive join fails, one beat of the
mess the data is actually in, and only then two beats of result. A viewer who watches the
results first and the obstacle later learns that the join was hard; a viewer who watches them
in this order learns *why the key is an identifier a person confirmed*, which is the point.

**Where this cut departs from the script.** The script has six sections; they become eight body
beats. Each split is a genuine seam:

1. *0:35 carried both the name trap AND the join key* — the 84% and the X.AI collision are
   different arguments. The first says a name scan is mostly wrong; the second says no better
   normalisation rescues it. Split into B02 and B03, so the collision gets its own frame.
2. *1:55 carried BOTH `w9-entries.png` and `w9-exposure.png`* — the script names two figures in
   one section. Split into B05 and B06; a single beat could not show seven companies on a time
   axis and ninety manager–company rows without one of them becoming decoration.
3. *2:20's denominator caveat became B08* — the script mentions it inside the corroboration
   section. Promoted to the last body beat, because it is the only place the reel restates its
   own headline as a smaller number and a sentence at 2:40 would be heard as hedging patter.
4. *0:00 and the source description split into B00 and B01* — the standard series bookends.

No claim was added or dropped by the splits. Narration was expanded to fit eight body beats at
the 45–70 word budget; every added sentence is connective or judgment, and **every added
FIGURE is injected from `figdata_week9.json` under an assertion**, with the three exceptions
named below. Four wording changes are logged in `FACTCHECK.md`.

## Honesty check

A join episode can go wrong by presenting a matched row as a fact. The cut is built so that
every match on screen is traceable to who filed it, and so that the two most quotable numbers
in the reel are both shown with the thing that weakens them.

- **The 84% is a design input, not a quality note.** B02 is a whole beat and the bar is drawn
  so the feeder share dominates the frame. The injection asserts that feeders outnumber
  candidates more than five to one, so a data change that made the name scan *usable* would
  fail the build rather than ship a beat whose framing had quietly gone stale ✓
- **The archive gap is on the frame, not in the paperwork.** 2008Q2–2013Q4 are not published;
  two orphan quarters exist and are deliberately excluded, so "earliest observed filing" is a
  property of a contiguous archive. B02 says so ✓
- **The false positive is left visible.** The scan's own output carries a row labelled
  FALSE POSITIVE — a company that matched by name and is not in the universe. It is shown
  rather than quietly dropped, the same discipline week 6's canary was recorded with ✓
- **The script's entry-date claim is narrowed on screen.** The source figure was titled as
  though all seven companies reach back before the panel; only three do. B05 shows all seven,
  accents the three, dims the four, and states the reason on the frame: only the latest annual
  and semi-annual report per registrant was fetched, so this is a floor on the record ✓
- **"227 with a cost" is not allowed to imply 22 have none.** The other 22 are one filer's, at
  fund level, and the injection asserts that exactly one filer reports that way. B04 names it ✓
- **The agreement is stated as evidence, not causation.** B07 spends three lines on the
  competing reading — a secondary purchase that settled the same day — and on why the
  agreement is still not circular: neither filing cites the other ✓
- **The reel restates its own headline as a smaller number.** B08 puts 56% and 33% at the same
  size under the same numerator and declares neither correct. The narration says only that the
  denominator is doing the work, which is the claim the frame supports ✓
- **The reel ends on what it cannot say.** B08's closing block: no valuation, no return, no
  causation ✓
- **The three values that are NOT in the figure data say so on screen.** The X.AI name
  collision, the five-layout taxonomy and the $400M Databricks amount come from the script and
  the filings behind it. Each is passed into the build as a named constant and each beat that
  uses one attributes it, rather than citing a file that does not contain it ✓
- **No invented figures on screen.** Everything else is a prop injected from
  `figdata_week9.json` under assertions that fail the build ✓
