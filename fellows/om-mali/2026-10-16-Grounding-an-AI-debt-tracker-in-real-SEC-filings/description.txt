Grounding an AI Debt Tracker in Real SEC Filings — AI Debt Tracker, week 1

A new Mycroft project: an agent that tracks the bonds and loans financing the AI build-out,
graded against each company's next quarterly report. This week did not build the agent. It
built the ground under it — the answer key, the cut-off that stops it peeking, the matcher,
and the model — so that when the agent arrives there is already a score it has to beat.

THE GAP. In 2025 the five biggest cloud companies sold $121bn of US corporate bonds, 4.3× their
$28bn-a-year average over 2020–2024. Mycroft runs agents for news, patents and hiring. None of
its ~99 recipes tracks debt or credit.

WHAT IT WILL DO — and note the tense. The agent will watch SEC filings from 25 AI-sector
companies, read each debt announcement, and keep a ledger of every bond and loan: the amount,
the interest rate, the due date. From that ledger it produces a signal — total debt, new
borrowing, and what falls due in the next one to two years. None of that is written yet. What
follows is.

GRADED AGAINST A REPORT IT CANNOT HAVE READ. Each quarter's ledger is graded against that
company's next quarterly report, where the debt is machine-readable. The answer is therefore
always published after the evidence. A point-in-time server enforces it: ask for a filing dated
after the report you are being graded on and it refuses. Without that refusal, every number
below is a model that was shown the answer.

COULD IT FAIL? TESTED FIRST. On 46 real company-quarters across 23 companies, 87.5% of new
bonds — 56 of 64 — were announced in a filing before the report showed them, against a pass mark
of 80% set before the test ran. And simply copying last quarter's debt forward is wrong in 21 of
those 46 quarters. So there is something to beat.

THE ANSWER KEY, AND THE WEEK'S REAL FINDING. Every quarterly report from July 2024 to September
2026, for the 25 AI companies and 75 non-AI companies held alongside them. The first surprise
was in the tagging. Microsoft's 13 "bonds" are really 13 batches of bonds; Oracle's quarterly
reports list only the newest few. Counted by tags, 21 companies looked gradeable bond by bond.
Measured by how much of each company's debt its report actually itemises — the gate is 80%
coverage — the number is 15. Fourteen qualified outright and one moved up on a 4–4 tie.

That is not a data error being corrected. It is a weaker way of counting being replaced by a
stronger one, and it changes what the agent is allowed to be graded on at all.

WHAT IT GRADES ON. Each quarter is graded at the detail its own report gives. That leaves 495
dated test cases: 95 to build on, and 400 held back and never tuned on. On the quarters graded
bond by bond, bonds now match from one report to the next 93.4% of the time — 594 of 636 —
above the 90% line set beforehand.

THE SMALLEST MODEL WON. Three local models, 29 real debt announcements, no hints, and the rule
for picking a winner fixed before the test. Qwen3 at 4 billion parameters read 99% of the bond
details (141 of 143) in about 6.1 seconds a filing on an 8 GB laptop GPU, at $0. Llama 3.1 8B
got 91%, Qwen3 8B got 88% — and the two 8B models between them looped on their own answers
until they were cut off five times. The 4B never did.

WHAT NONE OF THIS ESTABLISHES. 87.5% is 56 of 64 genuinely new bonds carrying a stated interest
rate; nine old bonds that were only newly tagged are excluded, and counting them would put the
figure at 76.7%, under the line. "Wrong in 21 of 46" is the conservative count — raw,
carry-forward differed in 33 of 46, but part of that is re-tagging rather than new debt. 93.4%
is the per-bond quarters; across every quarter it is 85%, the gap being Intel and Oracle
quarters that list few bonds and are graded on totals anyway. The 99% is one plain prompt on a
29-document set, after correcting six expected values the filings themselves contradicted —
95% before the corrections, same winner. And the $121bn is Bank of America's figure reported by
Reuters, context for the project rather than a measurement by it.

NEXT. The company list and the model are frozen. Build the grader and the do-nothing baseline
first, so the score the agent has to beat exists before the agent does.

that the three grading levels sum to the whole 25-company universe, that the measured
bond-by-bond count stays below the assumed one, that the all-quarters matching figure stays
below the per-bond one, and that the bake-off winner is the smallest model and wins outright.
