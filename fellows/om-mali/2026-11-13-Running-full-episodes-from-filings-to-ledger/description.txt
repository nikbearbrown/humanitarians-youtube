Running Full Episodes from Filings to Ledger — AI Debt Tracker, week 5

Weeks 1 to 4 built the pieces one at a time: an answer key, a do-nothing baseline, triage, and an
event reader. This week they run as one system. For one company and one quarter, the agent goes
from the filings in the window to a ledger of that company's bonds — and then checks its own
result. It finds the new bonds that doing nothing cannot find. It also adds some that are not
there.

THE GRAPH. One LangGraph run per company-quarter. A timeline node lists the filings, triage keeps
the ones about debt, and a reader branch runs on each of those at the same time — a Send fan-out,
not a queue. Then plain code merges what the readers found, retires bonds that have matured,
applies the rest to the opening ledger, and runs the self-checks. Every step is checkpointed, so
a stopped run picks up where it left off; midway through, the laptop ran out of memory, and
nothing had to be re-read. Across all 95 development quarters: 882 filings listed, 245 kept by
triage — 174 of them read by the model and 71 readings reused by a later quarter — and 53 open
questions raised. All 95 of 95 episodes ran end to end.

THE LEDGER ENGINE, AND A BORROWED START. The second half of the graph is code, not a model. It
merges events from different filings that describe the same bond, sweeps matured bonds by the
same rule the baseline uses, and applies new issues, add-ons and redemptions. Anything it cannot
place — a repayment of a bond the ledger does not hold, a new bond with no maturity — becomes an
open question rather than a guess. One thing is worth saying plainly: the opening ledger is
BORROWED from the answer key, exactly the one the baseline carries forward. That is deliberate,
because with the same starting point any difference from the baseline is what reading added. It
is also the largest limit on every number below. Reading the opening ledger from the filings is
next week.

AGAINST DOING NOTHING. The baseline is carrying last quarter's debt forward. On the 45 quarters
graded bond by bond, from the same opening ledger:

  Bond recall        90.9%  →  97.8%     (+6.9 points, paired, +4.7 to +9.4)
  Bond precision     92.8%  →  90.2%
  New bonds caught   0 of 34  →  30 of 34

Recall rises because reading is the only way to see a new bond: a bond first issued this quarter
is not in last quarter's report, so carrying forward finds none of them. Precision falls because
a system that adds things will add some wrong things. That trade, in both directions, is the
week.

WHAT IT ADDED, CHECKED ONE BY ONE. In the quarters whose report itemises its bonds, the tracker
added 66 bonds. 40 are in the report — the new bonds it was built to find. 26 are not:

  10   existing notes read from a prospectus as if new                       (P56)
  10   launch notices with no coupon yet, so not merged with the priced bond (P57)
   4   the banks' option to buy more — "up to an additional $45 million"     (P58)
   2   plain misreads, with no shared cause and no defect id

All three named kinds are fixes to the merge step, logged for week 6 and not fixed against this
run. The diagnosis of each wrong addition is Claude's reading of it after scoring — a judgment,
not a measurement, and it is labelled as such on screen.

THE TIMING BUG (P55). The filing window runs up to the day the target report is filed, weeks
after the quarter ends, and the first run applied everything in it — so bonds sold after the
quarter closed landed inside that quarter's ledger. The design keeps the ledger by settlement
date. The fix was made on development data (only events filed by the quarter end are applied) and
the run was repeated from the saved readings. Both runs are reported: precision was 89.2% before
the fix and 90.2% after, and recall and new bonds caught were the same in both. The fix is
partial — filing date still stands in for settlement date, so a bond priced in the last days of a
quarter can still land on the wrong side.

CHECKED AGAIN, ON FILINGS NOBODY HAD READ. Each set drawn and labelled before the run it scores.

  Triage: debt found       15 of 15      (last week it FAILED this check at 8 of 9)
  Reader: fields read      91%
  Regex rules, B1          74%
  One model call, B2       96%
  Answer key               3 of 4 gradable, of 5 drawn

Triage passes this time, after two rule fixes — but 15 positives is still small: one miss would
have been 93%, below the line. Two of its three false alarms are new rule weaknesses (P53, P54).
The reader beat pattern matching again, and a single model call still reads more numbers than the
reader does — with 15 instruments that are not in the filings, and not one sentence behind any of
its values. The reader is not the most accurate thing measured here; what it has is provenance.
On the answer key, Applied Digital could not be graded (P44, P48) and TeraWulf is the one
disagreement (P47). Only 2 of the 25 companies had never been hand-checked, so the draw had to be
extended: three of the five reports are from companies checked before, in other quarters.

LIMITS. 95 of 95 is on development quarters; the test split has not been touched. Every quarter
started from the answer key's ledger, so none of this shows what happens when the tracker reads
its own opening. Repayments barely work — 3 of 14 removed bonds were removed, and 24 repayments
matched nothing in the ledger at all. A merge rule that drops launch notices could also drop a
real bond announced only once, so the fixes may cost recall.

NOT REVIEWED BY A PERSON. The week was handed to Claude end to end before it started; the one
decision taken during it was to resume the runs after the memory stop. The triage labels, the
reader truth, the hand-check transcriptions and the diagnosis of the 26 wrong additions are a
model's reading, not a person's. manifests/week5_gate.json records the week as DELEGATED, not
CLEARED.

figure quotes, that the triage line which passes this week is the same line that failed last
week, and that the one-call baseline stays above the event reader. Three claims in the video are
not measured in figdata.json: the gate record, who produced the labels and the diagnosis, and
that the opening ledger was deliberately borrowed. Each is attributed on screen. No defect id is
