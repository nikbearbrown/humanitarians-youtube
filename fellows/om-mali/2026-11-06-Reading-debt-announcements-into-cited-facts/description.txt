Reading Debt Announcements into Cited Facts — AI Debt Tracker, week 4

Last week the agent learned to find the debt news. This week it reads it: a filing becomes
structured bonds and loans, each with an amount, a currency, a coupon and a due year, and every
value pointing back to the sentence it came from. It passed the check it was set. A simpler
baseline still read more fields right.

THE READER. The rule from week 1 is that the model never types a number, so the reader works in
three steps. Code picks out the passages that state debt terms. The model lists each bond and
loan and, for every value, copies the exact words and names the passage they came from. Then code
checks those words really are in that passage, and parses the number itself. The model is
bracketed by code on both sides: it chooses what to point at, never what a number is.

THE COPY CHECK. For Alphabet's euro notes the filing says €1,500,000,000. The model reported
copying "$1,500,000,000" — it rewrote the currency sign while claiming to copy. Those words are
not in the cited passage, so the values were refused: missing, rather than wrong. Across the set
254 values were copied; 227 were found in the passage the model cited, 3 in a different one, and
24 were refused. Eight of the 24 were currency signs like Alphabet's. The other sixteen were the
model writing the word "null" — bookkeeping, with no effect on any score.

THE RESULT. Tested on 24 filings whose 66 instruments were labelled before any method ran:

  B1, regex rules      135 of 160 fields      84%     1 extra instrument
  The event reader     157 of 167 fields      94%    29 extra instruments
  B2, one model call   160 of 160 fields     100%     9 extra instruments

The week's check, fixed in the design back in week 1, was to beat B1. The reader did. But the
third row is the honest one: asking the same small model to type every value itself read all of
them. The three bars do not share a denominator, and the reader reports more instruments the
filings never announce than either baseline. On clean pricing documents — and 62 of the 66
instruments were new notes in pricing documents — a 4B model reads numbers well. What the reader
adds is not accuracy. It is a cited sentence behind every value, and a number that is not in the
filing gets refused instead of used.

TRIAGE, CHECKED AGAIN. 50 new filings, labelled before the run, after last week's two rule fixes.
Triage found 8 of the 9 that were about debt — 89%, below the 95% line. That is a failed check.
The miss was an IBM euro prospectus whose cover is blank even where the year's "20" should be, so
last week's fix for blank covers never fired, and "Preferred Stock" in its contents read as a
share sale. Logged as P46 and not tuned away. Nine positives is a small check. One of the four
false alarms is probably a labelling error — an Applied Digital earnings release does announce a
$150m Macquarie financing — but labels are not changed after a run.

THE ANSWER KEY. Last week's three reports now agree, but those are the reports the fixes were
made against, so that is a calibration. Five fresh reports, drawn by seed before reading: one
agreed, Amazon, whose five totals match exactly. Four new defects, each in a different company's
tagging — Constellation's total built from half a pair (P41), one Marvell bond tagged under two
names (P42), a range of years read as a single bond at Intel (P43), and Applied Digital graded
bond by bond with no bond to grade (P44). All logged for next week rather than fixed against the
reports that found them. One slip is disclosed: the key's totals for those five reports were
printed once before the transcriptions were written.

NOT REVIEWED BY A PERSON. The week was handed to Claude end to end before it started. The labels,
the reader truth and the transcriptions are a model's reading — machine-checked, in that all 51
labelled new bonds with a coupon and a year appear in the next quarterly report's XBRL with the
same coupon and year, but not read by anyone. manifests/week4_gate.json records the week as
DELEGATED, not CLEARED.
