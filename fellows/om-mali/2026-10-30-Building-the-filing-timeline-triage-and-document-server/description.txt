Building the Filing Timeline, Triage and Document Server — AI Debt Tracker, week 3

The agent's first real job: find the debt news among hundreds of SEC filings without missing
any, and read it in a way it can cite. The agent still cannot read a filing into structured
facts. This week built what finds the filings worth reading, and what lets a reader point into
them.

THE ANSWER KEY, RE-CHECKED. Last week's three data problems are fixed, and last week's three
test reports now all agree — but those are the reports the fixes were made against, so 3 of 3
is a calibration, not a measurement. Five fresh reports were drawn before reading and graded
once. Two of them (Cipher Mining, TeraWulf) had no debt on either side, so there was nothing to
grade. Of the three that did, one agreed:

  Dell             totals agree exactly
  Super Micro      the banks' over-allotment counted as a second bond        (P38, open)
  Core Scientific  small notes with no due date anywhere in the data         (P39, open)

Both defects are left open on purpose. Fixing them against the reports that found them would
turn next week's check into another calibration. And one reading mistake in the hand-check was
mine: a Super Micro coupon taken from the notes' original terms, which were amended in 2025. It
doesn't change the verdict — the double-counted allotment alone makes that report disagree.

THE TIMELINE. For every quarter, the timeline gathers each 8-K, 424B prospectus and FWP term
sheet filed in the window, for 25 AI companies and the subsidiaries that borrow on their behalf
— a company's debt is often issued by a financing subsidiary, and a filing missed there is a
bond missed. 593 filings across the dev quarters: 469 8-Ks, 94 prospectuses, 30 term sheets.
The window ends at the report the quarter is graded on, so nothing later can get in.

TRIAGE. Most of those filings aren't about debt at all. Five plain rules settle two thirds of
them — 394 of 593 — before any model runs: a new-obligation item on an 8-K, a prospectus cover
selling notes rather than shares, a term sheet, a debt headline, no debt words at all. Only the
doubtful 199 go to a local qwen3:4b, at a median 3.1 seconds each. 184 filings are kept: 163 by
rule and 21 by the model.

THE CHECK. 50 filings — 30 drawn at random, 20 from filings containing debt words — were
labelled against a written definition before triage ran, by Claude: a different model from the
one being tested, but not a person. The labels file is byte-for-byte unchanged after the run.

  Debt filings found              24 of 24   (recall; pass line 95%)
  Kept filings that are debt      24 of 26   (precision)
  Event type right                20 of 24
  Last week's announcements kept  22 of 22

24 of 24 is recall on 24 positives inside a 50-filing sample — not accuracy, and not a large
set. The two it kept that were not debt: a tender offer for Alphabet shares, not notes, and a
TeraWulf acquisition.

WHAT WENT WRONG. The first run crashed when a model answer was cut off mid-sentence, so now an
unreadable answer keeps the filing rather than dropping it. The check exposed a bug in last
week's search: "0%" was matching inside "20%", which had credited a 0% 2031 convertible to an
earnings release. Fixed; last week's published counts did not change. And 8 of the model's 21
"yes" quotes were not word for word in the text it was given — which can be a paraphrase rather
than an invention, so the filing is still kept and the event reader will re-check every fact.

THE DOCUMENT SERVER. An MCP server that turns a filing into numbered sentences, tables, rows
and cells, so the agent cites s5 instead of retyping $1,500,000,000. And it refuses to open the
report the agent will be graded on — every document goes through the same point-in-time rule.

NEXT. Fix the two new defects, check a fresh set of reports again, and build the event reader
that turns these filings into structured debt events.
