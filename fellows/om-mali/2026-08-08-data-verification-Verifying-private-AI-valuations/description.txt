Verifying Private AI Valuations — Week 1 of the Private AI Valuation Agent

Most of the AI industry's value sits in companies you cannot buy. OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks — no ticker, no earnings call, no public price. But the mutual funds that own pieces of them have to report every holding to the SEC on Form N-PORT, with a dollar value and a share count. Divide one by the other and you get a price per share for a company that has no public price.

Week 1 was about finding out whether that actually works. It does — and my own plan was wrong twice before I wrote a line of pipeline code.

What's in this video:
• The mechanism — value ÷ share count, straight out of the filing
• 19 Anthropic positions read by hand across six fund families. Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, Alger and ARK all landed on the same price, identical to the cent. Then a new round repriced it and BlackRock and Capital Group matched to four decimal places, two days apart.
• Defect one: a filter that was supposed to isolate private holdings dropped five of my six managers, silently, with no error
• Defect two: the plan expected a few thousand private rows per quarter. The real number was 606,028. Inverting the pipeline shrank the hardest part of the project by about 600x.
• Universe v1, frozen at six companies — SpaceX added, Cohere removed, because every Cohere match turned out to be Coherent Corp, a public optics company

Next week: eight quarters loaded into the database, then entity resolution.

Every number here is measured and traceable to a named filing accession or a reproducible query against the public 2026 Q2 SEC bulk data set. That is the project's claim to attention — not novelty, and not a trading edge. This video makes no valuation claim about any company: a price per share is not the same as what a company is worth.
