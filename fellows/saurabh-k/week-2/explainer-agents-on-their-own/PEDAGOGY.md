# PEDAGOGY — hai-agents-on-their-own

## The one thing a viewer should leave with

*An agent is not a smarter model. It is the same model in a loop, and nothing in that loop
knows when to stop — so the stopping condition has to come from outside and be written down.*

## Structure

1. **The question** (B00-B01) — stated before any definition, so the terms arrive as answers.
2. **What actually changes** (B02) — the pause for a human disappears. One idea, one beat.
3. **Tool, loop, autonomy, guardrail** (B03) — the vocabulary, placed after the viewer can
   feel why it is needed.
4. **Evidence** (B04-B06) — three consecutive beats of first-party material: a real
   unattended task, a real guardrail quoted word for word, six real refusals.
5. **When to let one run** (B07) — the test.
6. **Your turn** (B08) — the transfer.

## Why the evidence is arranged this way

The three evidence beats escalate deliberately. B04 shows autonomy working — a job finished
with nobody watching. B05 shows a limit that had to be written by a person, because the
loop could not derive it. B06 shows limits actually firing, six times, against the maker of
this very video.

That last move is the pedagogical core. The film does not argue that agents need guardrails
using someone else's cautionary tale; it uses the gates that blocked its own production.
A viewer can check the claim by reading this folder's BUILD-LOG and CHECKS-REPORT.

## The transferable move

Notice when a thing is a mechanism and when it is a judgement. The reel keeps the two
visually and verbally separate: the loop and the refusals are shown, the three-part test
is spoken as advice. FACTCHECK.md marks the same split as VERIFIED-LOCAL versus ARGUMENT.
A viewer who starts asking which of the two they are being handed has learned the method.

## What the viewer is asked to do

Take an automation you already let run unattended, and try to state what would make it stop.
If the sentence does not come out, that is the finding.
