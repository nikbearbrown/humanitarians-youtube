# PEDAGOGY — hai-repo-sensei-v2

## The one thing a viewer should leave with

*An answer you cannot check is not an answer — and in code that matters more than anywhere
else, because a confident wrong answer gets reviewed, approved and shipped.*

## Why this reel is drawn rather than written

The three earlier reels in this series were Remotion-only, which caps every beat at a
typographic card: an eyebrow, a heading, bullet lines. That is adequate for stating a claim
and useless for explaining a mechanism.

This episode's subject *is* a mechanism — a four-stage pipeline, a parse tree, a trace
running down through three layers, a citation pointing at a file and a line. Those are
drawings. So 13 of the 20 beats are Manim, authored in `scenes.py`, and the reel runs 2:27
at roughly seven seconds a beat instead of 1:48 at sixteen.

## The three acts

- **I — The problem** (B01-B04). A hundred thousand lines, one question, and an answer with
  an empty box where its source should be. The viewer should feel the problem before any
  solution is named.
- **II — How it works** (B05-B11). Pipeline, parser, queries, trace, citation. The
  explanatory spine, entirely drawn.
- **III — Nine days in** (B12-B19). Measured numbers, the repo map, a real query, and a
  status bar that admits what does not work.

Each act opens with a two-second numeral card so the shape is felt rather than announced.

## The transferable move

Demand the source. The film's "your turn" is deliberately something a viewer can do in
thirty seconds with a tool they already have: ask which file and which line, then open it.
That single habit is the whole thesis, and it does not require repo-sensei to exist.

## The honesty constraint

The project is nine days old and the answering path is unfinished. `B16_Honest` says so on
screen with a half-filled bar, and the closing caption reads *"No demo of something that
does not exist yet."* A mocked-up result would have been the exact failure this series has
argued against since week one.
