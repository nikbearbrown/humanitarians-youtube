Why Did the AI Say No? Reason codes for AI credit decisions

When an AI credit model turns down a loan, the lender still has to say why. In the US, Regulation B (under the
Equal Credit Opportunity Act) asks for specific reasons, the principal ones; "you failed to reach our score" is not
enough. In my small experiment on synthetic applicants (20,000 made up, five plain features, no protected traits),
a simple logistic scorecard and a gradient-boosted "black box" rank risk equally well (test AUC 0.84 each). Both deny
made-up Applicant A (risk 53% and 34% against a 30% line I chose). The scorecard's reasons are exact, weight × (A −
average): debt-to-income, card use, a missed payment. The black box needs Shapley values, 16,000 model runs for one
person, and gives missed payments, card use, credit history: 2 of 3 reasons shared. Its list also shifts with the
comparison group and with retraining. Paying card balances down about $3,850 flips both models to yes. A 2022 CFPB
circular said the duty holds for complex algorithms; it was withdrawn in 2025, but Regulation B's wording remains.
This is what the rule asks for, not legal advice.
