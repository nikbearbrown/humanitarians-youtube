Hedging When Trading Isn't Free #Shorts

The textbook Black–Scholes hedge adjusts every day — with a 0.5% fee that costs $1.11 per option, about
40% of its price. Deep Hedging (Buehler et al., 2019) trains a network with the fees in its objective.
In my small replication (40,000 test paths) it trades 1.30 shares instead of 2.23 and pays $0.65 in fees.
With zero fees it lands close to Black–Scholes. The catch: it's a toy world, and a learned hedge is only
as good as its simulator.
