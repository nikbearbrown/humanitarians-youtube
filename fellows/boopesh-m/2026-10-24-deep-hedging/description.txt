Hedging When Trading Isn't Free: Deep Hedging, Replicated

The textbook Black–Scholes hedge is exact only if trading is free. "Deep Hedging" (Buehler, Gonon,
Teichmann & Wood, Quantitative Finance, 2019) trains a neural network to hedge with trading costs inside
its objective. In my own small replication (simulated Black–Scholes market, 40,000 test paths, 0.5% fee),
the learned hedge trades 1.30 shares instead of 2.23, pays $0.65 in fees instead of $1.11, and loses less
on average (−$0.65 vs −$1.11 per option), though its outcomes are more spread out. With zero fees it lands
close to Black–Scholes. It's a toy world: a learned hedge is only as good as its simulator.
