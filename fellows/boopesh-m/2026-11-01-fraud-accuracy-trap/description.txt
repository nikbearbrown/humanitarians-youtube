The 99.9% Accuracy Trap: why fraud models need recall and precision

Card fraud is rare. In the widely used credit-card dataset from the ULB Machine Learning Group and Worldline,
there are 492 frauds in 284,807 transactions — about 1 in 580. So a "model" that always says legit is 99.8%
accurate and catches nothing. In my own small experiment (synthetic data with the same size and fraud rate,
85,443 test transactions, 148 frauds), a logistic regression and a gradient-boosted model are both 99.9%
accurate — but one catches 66 frauds and the other 127. Accuracy can't tell them apart; recall and precision
can. Then the threshold dial: lower the cutoff and you catch more fraud, with more false alarms; the right
cutoff depends on what each mistake costs. Honest limits: the data is synthetic, and real fraud systems use
far more signals.
