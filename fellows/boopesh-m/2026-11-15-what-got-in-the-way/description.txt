What Got in the Way: Humanitarians AI, Nov 8–14, 2026

A weekly progress update from a Humanitarians AI fellow. Between November 8 and 14, 2026, the fellows added 32
frictional logs (short, honest notes on what resisted them) to their shared YouTube repository, from 13 fellows;
31 of them describe obstacles. By my rough keyword sort, the most common obstacle was layout and picture checks
(19 logs, from 8 fellows): things running off the edge, overlapping, or too small to read. Then facts and data
(11), length and time (10), tools and setup (10), timing and pacing (8), and the AI voice saying a word wrong (5).
A few examples, in my words: a chart passed the frame checks but showed the opposite of the narration; a working
title didn't survive the data; a 4:22 script was trimmed five times for a 4:00 slot without cutting a number; a
40-minute checking script now streams frames in 87 seconds; words that appeared late were timed to the voice.
What fixed them? Most often, someone looked: in 17 of the 31 logs a problem was found by watching the cut,
viewing the frames, or re-reading the words. Compared with last week, recounted by the same script: merged pull
requests 27 → 19, new work folders 75 → 53, fellows with new folders 19 → 17, frictional logs 95 → 32. My own two
videos from last week (What the Fellows Taught and The Backtest Mirage, each with a Short, all 4K) were delivered
for review; my own obstacle is access, and my GitHub pull request is still pending.

The one idea: a passing check tells you the frame fits. Only looking tells you it's right.
