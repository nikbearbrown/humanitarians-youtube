## 2. Literature Exploration: Adversarial Attacks & Verification in Physical AI

In classical image classification, an adversarial perturbation only needs to fool a model on a single static frame. In a closed-loop robotic system running at 20 Hz, as soon as an attack nudges the robot arm at Step 1, the camera viewpoint shifts at Step 2, breaking static perturbations. A survey of 23 recent papers (2024–2026) across robot jailbreaking (_RoboPAIR_, _BadRobot_, _POEX_), VLA adversarial attacks (_AttackVLA_, _TMA_, _EDPA_, _UPA-RFAS_, _RoboGCG_, _SilentDrift_, _DropVLA_, _TRAP_, _DURA_, _Tex3D_), and formal safety shields (_RoboGuard_, _SENTINEL_, _SafeVLA_, _PatchCleanser_) revealed a critical gap highlighted by the **AttackVLA** benchmark ([arXiv:2511.12149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12149)):

- **The Unsolved Gap:** Almost all existing test-time visual attacks on VLA models only induce _untargeted failures or static action states_ (causing the robot arm to freeze, twitch in place, or miss an object). Smoothly and stealthily steering a **clean, unpoisoned, standard VLA model** to execute an attacker-chosen multi-step trajectory—without modifying the user's text prompt or poisoning training data—remains largely unsolved.

## 3. Initial Hypotheses, Peer Feedback from Dev & Iterative Refinement

From this literature gap, three initial research directions were shortlisted: (1) **Semantic Object-Identity Swap Steering** (tricking the vision-language representation rather than forcing raw `[dx, dy, dz]` motor outputs at every frame), (2) **Test-Time Action-Chunk Boundary Drift**, and (3) **Runtime Spatial Consistency Shielding** (masking camera regions to detect and neutralize patch steering without retraining).

To ensure the scope was realistic without physical robot hardware, **Hypothesis 1 (Stealthy Attack)** and **Hypothesis 2 (Runtime Consistency Defense)** were selected for a pure-simulation study using `LIBERO` (`MuJoCo`) and `OpenVLA-7B`. Following a detailed technical review from **Dev**, several key refinements were made to strengthen both hypotheses:

- **Upgrading the Defense from Random Masking to Systematic Grid + Two-Round Verification (Hypothesis 2):** Dev pointed out that with 5 random $10\%\text{–}15\%$ masks, most views still contain a small adversarial patch—meaning a naive median vote actually selects the _attacked_ action, and random masks frequently miss the patch altogether. To address this, we kept **Method 1 (Naive Random 5-View Masking)** as a baseline and added **Method 2 (Systematic $3 \times 3$ Covering Grid + Two-Round Minority Check, extending _PatchCleanser_ to closed-loop control)** so both can be compared side-by-side. We also calibrated false-positive rates **per full episode ($<5\%$)** with a 3-consecutive-step persistence rule and neutral tabletop-color/token masking instead of black pixel boxes.
- **Eliminating Object-Coordinate Matching via "Blind" Steering Methods (Hypothesis 1):** Dev noted that matching visual tokens between Object A (e.g., top-left) and Decoy Object B (e.g., bottom-right) is vulnerable to Vision Transformer 2D positional embedding mismatches and shifting camera viewpoints. We resolved this by redesigning Hypothesis 1 so the optimizer **only updates the grid squares covered by the patch itself**, requiring zero prior knowledge of where Object A or Object B sit on the table:
  - **Way 1 — "The Attention Magnet Patch" (Cross-Attention Hijacking):** Trains the patch across diverse scenes and prompts to pull $100\%$ of `Llama-2`’s text-to-image cross-attention score onto the patch's own squares, luring the robot arm to whatever decoy sits beside the patch.
  - **Way 2 — "The Auto-Feature Copy Patch" (Vision-Encoder Shortcut):** Automatically identifies the top-3 highest-attention squares on a clean frame (`mean(tokens_A)`) and trains the patch’s squares to copy that pooled visual ID vector. Because `OpenVLA`'s 256 visual tokens are produced by the `SigLIP + DINOv2` vision encoder (~0.6B parameters) _before_ the 7B `Llama-2` backbone, Way 2 can be trained in ~3–4 GB of GPU VRAM $5\text{–}10\times$ faster.
- **Two-Stage 3D Rendering & Testbed Alignment:** Standardized the testbed exclusively on **`LIBERO` (`LIBERO-Object` and `LIBERO-Spatial`)** using the official fine-tuned checkpoints (`openvla-7b-finetuned-libero-object` and `-spatial`), explicitly separated differentiable `kornia` homography warping (during patch training) from native `MuJoCo` 3D coaster rendering (during rollout evaluation), and documented four primary technical risks and mitigations (`## Risks & Points of Failure`).
