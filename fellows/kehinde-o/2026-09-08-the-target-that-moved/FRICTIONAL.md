# Frictional Log -- The Target That Moved

**Date:** September 8, 2026
**Fellow:** Kehinde Obidele
**Work:** Brutalist video (project update, Week 2)

## What I set out to do

Produce a project update video for Week 2 reporting on progress with the Medhavy textbook fact-checking. The title "The Target That Moved" reflects how AI-generated statistics shift when you check them against original sources.

## What I expected

I expected the Brutalist workflow to be smoother this time since I had completed one video already. I planned to focus the content on real examples from my Chapter 1 and 2 reviews.

## Where it resisted

The challenge was deciding which examples to highlight. I had dozens of corrections but needed to pick the ones that would be most meaningful to a general viewer. Balancing technical accuracy with accessibility took several rounds of editing the narration text.

The 9:16 (vertical) render required thinking about visual framing differently. Content that works in landscape does not always read well in portrait, especially text-heavy frames.

## What I did next

I picked three concrete examples that show different types of errors: a fabricated author name, a wrong percentage, and a citation that does not support its claim. These three cover the main error categories without overwhelming the viewer. I adjusted visual descriptions in the beat sheet to work for both aspect ratios.

## What Claude or another person contributed

Claude helped structure the beat sheet and suggested framing the video around the concept of "moving targets" in AI-generated content. I kept the framing but rewrote the narration to use my own examples and voice.

## What I accepted, changed, or rejected

I accepted the three-example structure as a good way to cover the main error types. I changed the closing to include a call to action about why human review matters. I rejected a suggestion to include too many technical details about PubMed search syntax since that would lose the audience.

## Result

Week 2 project update video completed. Both aspect ratios rendered. Uploaded to Google Drive, source files committed to GitHub.
