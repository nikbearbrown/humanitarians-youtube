# Pedagogy Review

## Topic
Fact-Checking Chapter 23: Chemotherapy in the Medhavy Cancer Textbook

## Learning Objective
Viewer understands the scope and findings of the Chapter 23 chemotherapy
fact-check, including why triple-auditing matters and the patient-safety risk of
presenting unapproved drug uses as standard practice.

## Audience
General audience interested in AI, healthcare, and textbook quality.

## Key Findings
- 474 sentences in chapter, 136 flagged for verification, 86 rows reviewed
- 52 Needs Revision, 34 Approved
- Critical patient-safety finding: venetoclax presented as approved for multiple
  myeloma (it is not; FDA label carries a mortality warning)
- Methotrexate half-life stated backwards relative to FDA label
- Historical errors: arsenicals date off by 35+ years, WWI/WWII mustard gas misattribution
- Character-encoding defect across Files 2-5 corrupts Greek letters
- Triple-audited evidence document with 100 unique source URLs
- Self-audit caught 5 errors in reviewer's own work

## Register check (Plain)
States the method, the decision trigger, and where it fails: the reviewer's own
work contained five errors, which the video reports rather than hides.

## Honesty

The attrition fractions are computed from the counts (136/474 = 0.287,
86/136 = 0.632), not estimated. The venetoclax finding is stated as the FDA
label and the CANOVA trial report it, and the video does not offer clinical
