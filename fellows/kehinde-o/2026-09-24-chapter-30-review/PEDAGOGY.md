# Pedagogy Review

## Topic
Fact-Checking Chapter 30: Future Directions in Cancer Treatment Research

## Learning Objective
The viewer leaves able to tell the difference between "this citation is real" and "this
citation supports this sentence", and knows that the second question is the one that
catches errors. They can apply it to any sourced document.

## Audience
General audience interested in AI, research integrity and citation practice. No cancer
biology background assumed.

## Key Findings Reported
- 1,654 sentences, 55 flagged across 111 evidence rows, 35 inside my review scope
- 24 need revision, 11 approved as written. 24 + 11 = 35
- All 11 rows previously marked FALSE were confirmed as genuine errors, none a false alarm
- A drug screen reported backwards: 2,427 compounds screened yielding 15 hits, written as
  a screen of 15 compounds
- Mycoplasma hominis for M. hyorhinis, an error already present in published literature
  where one review's body text and its own figure caption disagree
- Four audit passes; the fourth asked whether sources supported the rows they sat on
- Character-encoding damage in 7 files, not the 4 the workbook recorded

## Accuracy Gate
Every figure traces to the completed Chapter 30 review record. See FACTCHECK.md.

## Restraint Gate
- No clinical advice.
- No individual named as responsible for any error.
- The three unverified figures are reported as unverified, never as wrong. That
  distinction is itself a teaching point in the video.
- Encoding damage is presented as a file defect that blocks edits, not a content error.

## VERDICT: PASS
