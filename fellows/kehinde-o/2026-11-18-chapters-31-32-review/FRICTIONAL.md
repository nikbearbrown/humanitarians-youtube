**What I was working on.**

Chapter 31 (solid tumors by organ and system): 1,183 sentences, 591 flagged, 159 in scope, 99 revised,
59 approved, 1 rejected, 143 edits across 8 files, done over 30 and 31 October. Chapter 32 (central
nervous system tumors): 1,167 sentences, 336 flagged, 101 in scope, 58 revised, 40 approved, 3
pending, 50 edits across all 6 files.

**What I tried, and what I expected.**

Chapter 31 was the biggest single review I have done. I expected volume. What I did not expect was
how many claims point the wrong way: I recorded eight, like smoking's share of lung cancer swapped
between men and women. Several drug claims were right once and have since lost their approval; one
treatment the chapter describes no longer exists, which is the one row I rejected outright.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**

Two trial figures got past review because their control numbers were correct: the intraoperative MRI
trial and PALOMA-2. Recognising one number made me relax about the other, which is the lesson I took.
Chapter 32 also made me check the audit itself: one supplied finding cited a paper that turned out to
be a different trial entirely, and another flagged a quotation the chapter had right. Three figures I
could not confirm because the abstract lacked them and I could not get the full text, so I left them
pending rather than guess. After the review I also had to correct 34 cells in my own workbook.

**What Claude contributed, and what I did with it.**
- Mine: the review itself, every verdict and source, the edit guides and the evidence documents.
- Claude's: help drafting and checking parts of the written record.
- Accepted: corrections to wording where they matched the sources.
- Rejected/changed: any suggestion I could not trace to a source.
- Evidence: the evidence documents and workbooks in the chapter folders, and the merged textbook pull requests.

**What I understand now, and what I still do not.**
- Understood: check the number I do not recognise first.
- Not understood: where the chapter's untraceable hazard ratios of 24 and 27 came from.
- Open: the three pending Chapter 32 figures need the full text.
