## One wrong word, and where it came from

The chapter calls the BRCA and PARP relationship **synergistic lethality**. The
field's term is **synthetic lethality**, and they mean different things: synergy
is the size of a combined effect, synthetic lethality is a dependency that only
turns fatal when both partners are lost.

The chapter did not invent the wrong term. It reproduces **43 consecutive words**
from an NCBI GeneReviews page, and that page uses the wrong term too. The error
was copied, and it outlived its source.

## A disease called rare when it is the majority

The chapter says non-inherited retinoblastoma is rare. Non-heritable disease is
**60 to 70 percent** of all retinoblastoma. What is rare is the event the sentence
describes, two independent somatic hits in the same cell, not the disease.

## The defect in my own work

Four passes. The second found the worst defect in the whole review, and it was
mine: most of the narrative in my evidence document had been carried over from a
template and still described **Chapter 30's** findings. My summary table said zero
TRUE rows were in scope when there were six, and six editorial findings when there
were thirteen. Every section was rewritten and every count recomputed.

The same pass re-tested every link and found three of my own citations resolve but
serve a **bot challenge** rather than the fact. The link works; the fact is not
readable. Of 120 links checked, 115 resolve and 5 return an error.
