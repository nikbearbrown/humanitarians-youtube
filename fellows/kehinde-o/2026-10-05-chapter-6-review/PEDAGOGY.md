# Pedagogy Review

## Topic
Fact-Checking Chapter 6: Tumor Suppressor Genes in the Medhavy Cancer Textbook

## Learning Objective
The viewer leaves understanding that a copied error is still an error and outlives
the source it came from, and that checking whether a link resolves is not the same
as checking whether the fact is readable. They can apply both to their own notes.

## Audience
General audience interested in AI, research integrity and how textbooks are
checked. No cancer biology background assumed; BRCA and PARP are explained by
their relationship rather than their biochemistry.

## Key Findings Reported
- 212 sentences, 134 flagged for verification, 16 inside my review scope
- 8 need revision, 8 approved as written. 8 + 8 = 16
- "Synergistic lethality" where the field says synthetic lethality, copied in a
  43 word verbatim run from an NCBI GeneReviews page that uses the wrong term
- Non-heritable retinoblastoma called rare when it is 60 to 70 percent of cases
- Four passes. Pass two found the worst defect in the review, and it was in my own
  evidence document: narrative sections carried over from a Chapter 30 template,
  and a summary table that said zero TRUE rows in scope when there were six
- Three of my own citations resolved but served a bot challenge rather than the
  fact. 120 links checked, 115 resolve, 5 return an error

## Accuracy Gate
Every figure traces to the completed Chapter 6 review record. See FACTCHECK.md.

## Restraint Gate
- No clinical advice.
- No individual is named as responsible for any error.
- The video does NOT say the chapter invented "synergistic lethality". It copied
  it from a source that uses it. That distinction is the point of the beat.
- The video does NOT say the hypermethylation sentence was fabricated. The
  workbook suspected it; it is a verbatim quote with missing attribution.
- The dead links are in the audit workbook's evidence column, not the textbook's,
  and are reported as my own problem rather than the chapter's.
