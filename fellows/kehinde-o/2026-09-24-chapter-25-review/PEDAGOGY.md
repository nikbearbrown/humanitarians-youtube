# Pedagogy Review

## Topic
Fact-Checking Chapter 25: Targeted Therapies in the Medhavy Cancer Textbook

## Learning Objective
The viewer leaves knowing that a fact-check can be wrong in both directions, and that
"is this accurate" and "is this still accurate" are two different questions. They can
apply both to a correction someone else has already made.

## Audience
General audience interested in AI, healthcare and how textbooks are checked. No oncology
background assumed. Every drug name is explained by its role, not by its chemistry.

## Key Findings Reported
- 288 sentences in the chapter, 45 flagged for verification, 22 inside my review scope
- 14 need revision, 8 approved as written. 14 + 8 = 22
- Cetuximab, a monoclonal antibody, listed among tyrosine kinase inhibitors
- Daraxonrasib approved 26 August 2026, roughly a month before the review, which turned a
  true sentence false
- Two rows where the earlier pass planned to correct a textbook that was already right
  (EMA ctDNA authorisation September 2014; the NCI 30-point omics checklist)
- Three audit passes, each finding errors in my own work; 44 sources in the final record

## Accuracy Gate
Every figure traces to the completed Chapter 25 review record (filled workbook, Evidence
Document, MDX TextEdit Guide). See FACTCHECK.md for the claim-by-claim table.

## Restraint Gate
- No clinical advice. The video reports what a label or a trial says.
- No individual is named as responsible for any error.
- The daraxonrasib row is presented as going out of date, not as anyone's mistake.
- The chapter is not characterised as unreliable. The errors are specific and mostly
  category or currency errors.

## VERDICT: PASS
