# Pedagogy Review

## Topic
Fact-Checking Chapter 9: Cell Cycle Control in the Medhavy Cancer Textbook

## Learning Objective
The viewer leaves able to ask a different question of a fact: not only "is this
true" but "when was this true". Most of what is wrong in this chapter was correct
when it was written and nobody measured it again.

## Audience
General audience interested in AI, research integrity and how knowledge goes
stale. No cell biology background assumed; each mechanism is explained by what it
does before it is named.

## Key Findings Reported
- 292 sentences, 145 flagged, 30 inside my review scope, plus 28 editorial findings
- 20 need revision, 10 approved. 20 + 10 = 30
- "The 100,000 genes" is a pre-genome-project estimate; NCBI records 19,116 known
  protein-coding genes and consensus sits near 20,000
- Double-strand breaks attributed to UV, which produces dimers and photoproducts
- CDKN1C called maternally imprinted when only the maternal allele is expressed
- Trilaciclib, approved Feb 2021 for small cell lung cancer, corrects a claim in
  MY OWN earlier finding that approval for the class remains in breast cancer

## Accuracy Gate
Every figure traces to the completed Chapter 9 review record. See FACTCHECK.md.

## Restraint Gate
- No clinical advice. The video reports what a registry or label says.
- No individual is named as responsible for any error.
- The video does NOT say the dinaciclib trial was terminated for futility. The
  workbook says that and the registry says COMPLETED. Repeating the workbook's
  error would reproduce it, and the video says so explicitly.
- The video does NOT say the CDK count of 20 is wrong. The nomenclature paper
  proposes naming the family CDK1 to CDK20, so the chapter follows the accepted
  scheme and that row is approved.
- No claim that prexasertib showed no activity. Response rates of 33.3 and 28.6
  percent in heavily pretreated disease are real activity; the problem with that
  sentence is its selection criterion.

## VERDICT: PASS
