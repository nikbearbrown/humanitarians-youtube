**What I tried, and what I expected.**

For Chapters 11 and 15 I decided to rule on every sentence, not just the flagged ones, because I
wanted to know how clean the unflagged sentences really were. The answer: cleaner, not clean. To
settle one claim I counted the reviewed human proteome myself from UniProt and found alanine is the
fourth most common amino acid, not the second.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**

The most visible defects were the ones no check caught: a 170-word paragraph printed twice, the same
caption under two photographs, eleven repeated sentences. When I looked at why, the duplicates had been
removed from the workbook data before checking. Three flags would have added errors if I had applied
them: a wrong chemical isomer, a real trial called fabricated, a misspelled author called invented. And
my own replacement sentences failed my own re-check nine times across the two chapters; on the first
pass I also fixed only one of two places where the same wrong gene appeared.
