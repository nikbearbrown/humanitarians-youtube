# Pedagogy Review

## Topic
Fact-Checking Chapters 16, 11 and 15: Tumor Immunology, Cancer Metabolism, and Cancer
Stem Cells and Tumor Heterogeneity, in the Medhavy Cancer Textbook

## Learning Objective
An automated checker cannot see what it removed before it looked, and checking every
sentence once lets you measure how good its flags really are. The viewer leaves able to
ask what a filter discards before anyone looks.

## Audience
General audience. "Flagged" and "cleared" are explained by what the pipeline did.

## Key Findings Reported
- Ch16: 28 claims, 12 revise, 16 approved; 20 edits in 6 of 7 files.
- Ch11: every sentence decided. Main tab 90 claims, 13 revise, 77 approved; AI-only 143,
  8 revise. 21 edits in 8 of 9 files.
- Ch15: every sentence decided. Main tab 238 claims, 28 revise, 210 approved; AI-only 280,
  5 revise. 34 edits in 3 of 6 files.
- A 170-word paragraph printed twice (Ch16) and a caption printed twice (Ch11), unflagged
  because the pipeline de-duplicated sentences before checking. Ch15 repeats eleven sentences.
- Needing revision: Ch11 flagged 14.4%, cleared 5.6%; Ch15 flagged 11.8%, cleared 1.8%.
- Three flags would have added errors: the BPTES isomer, a real 43-patient trial called
  fabricated, and a misspelled author called invented.
- Ch15 files one cancer's findings under another seven times (four flagged, three not).

## Accuracy Gate
Every figure traces to the Chapter 16, 11 and 15 review records. See FACTCHECK.md.

## Restraint Gate
- Rates are reported per chapter and never pooled.
- The de-duplication diagnosis is attributed to Chapters 16 and 11, where the record makes it.
- No clinical advice. No individual is named as responsible for any error.
- The narration is an AI voice. The outro says so on screen.

## Takeaway
Ask what a filter throws away before anyone looks, and look at some of it yourself.
