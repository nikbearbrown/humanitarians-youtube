How AI Image Generators Turn Noise Into a Picture — Humanitarians AI

You type a prompt into Midjourney and a moment later there are four images that
never existed before. Nothing was searched for and nothing was pasted together.
Every one of them started as random static. This is how that works, explained
from nothing: no maths or AI background needed.

EVERY IMAGE STARTS AS STATIC
Midjourney's own documentation says it: an image begins as random noise, like
the static on an old TV, and that starting noise comes from a number called a
seed. A model then does one thing, over and over: it looks at the static and
removes a little of the noise.

A MODEL, TRAINED ON A LAPTOP
Every picture in this video comes from a real model trained from scratch on a
laptop, with no graphics card: 12,000 tiny pictures of hearts and music notes,
16 pixels across, and 31 minutes of training. Given pure static and asked for a
heart, it takes 1,000 small steps, and a heart is left. It is a toy, not
Midjourney, but it runs the same method (Ho, Jain & Abbeel, 2020).

THE ONE RULE
The noisy picture is part picture, plus part noise. At every step the picture's
share shrinks; by step 1,000 less than one percent of it is left. Training asks
the model one question: which part of this is the noise?

WHAT TO DO WITH IT
When you test a change to your prompt, lock the seed (add --seed and a number).
Midjourney calls it a control in an experiment: now the only thing that changed
is your words. Don't use a seed to keep a style or a character. It only sets the
starting static, and Midjourney says it has the least effect of anything. Use
style references for that.
