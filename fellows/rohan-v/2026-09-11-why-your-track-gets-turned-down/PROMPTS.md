# PROMPTS — "Why Your Track Gets Turned Down"

Design intent behind each visual. Nothing here was generated by an image or
video model — every beat is a deterministic Remotion render, so these are
component briefs, not generation prompts.

## The library-first gate

All four were genuine misses — the closest candidates were wrong-subject with
low scores:

Four punts, four components built, each with a portrait sibling.

**Plus one unplanned punt:** `HaiTitleOutro916` did not exist. Visual QC caught
the landscape outro rendering `@NikBearBrown`, and fixing it to `HaiTitleOutro`

## The constraint that shaped every label

Rohan, choosing the topic:

> keep in mind that a lot of people are not audio professionals. so they might
> not understand what a master or a squeezed mix is so keep that in mind

So the components carry **no jargon in their labels**:

| Not used | Used instead |
|---|---|
| peak / true peak | **TALLEST SINGLE MOMENT** · *one instant — the single loudest sample* |
| LUFS / integrated loudness | **HOW LOUD IT ACTUALLY SOUNDS** · *the average over time, weighted like your ear* |
| LRA / dynamic range | **GAP BETWEEN THE QUIETEST AND LOUDEST MOMENTS** |
| master | *the file you upload* — or nothing |
| limiting / compression | **SQUEEZED LOUDER** |

LUFS appears exactly once, in B02's footnote, which says outright that the
viewer does not need the acronym.

## B00 — `ClaudeComposerAsk` (chassis, reused)

The fixed opener. The command is written as the viewer's own complaint — "my
track sounds quieter than other songs, so I pushed it louder. Spotify turned it
back down." The three output lines pre-load the answer.

## B01 — `LoudPeakVsLoudness` (new)

**Brief:** destroy the peak meter's credibility in one frame.

Two waveform rows from the same source. The top one keeps its shape — you can
see the quiet passage in the middle. The bottom one is a near-solid block,
because its quiet moments were pulled up to meet the loud ones. A **dashed
terracotta ceiling rule crosses both**, so the viewer can see the tallest
points line up.

Then two readouts per row, side by side: the tallest-point figures (−1.0 and
−0.2) sit in neutral cards, the loudness figures (−15.3 and −6.6) in terracotta
ones. Same ceiling, wildly different loudness — and the accent colour tells you
which number matters.

Envelopes are deterministic, so the render is reproducible and the two rows are
provably the same signal with one transformation between them.

**QC fix:** the readouts were initially sized to their text, which left the
right column ending well short of the waveform rows. They now fill the full
height of their row.

## B02 — `LoudLufsWindow` (new)

**Brief:** show *why* the two meters disagree, using one piece of audio.

A waveform with one tall spike early and a quiet body — the worst case for a
peak meter. First a marker darts to the single tallest bar and locks there,
labelled `THIS ONE MOMENT`. Then a terracotta window sweeps left to right
across the whole track, tinting what it has covered, with a running-average
line that settles as it accumulates.

Two cards then state the two readings in plain language. The peak card lands
with the marker; the loudness card lands with the sweep. The timing is the
argument: one reading is instantaneous, the other takes the whole song.

## B03 — `LoudNormalizeGain` (new)

**Brief:** the frame that carries the reel — two markers finishing level.

A vertical loudness axis, 0 at the top and −24 at the bottom, with a glowing
terracotta target line at ≈−14. Each track starts at its measured loudness. At
44% of the beat both markers **slide onto the target**, their readouts counting
as they travel, and the gain each one needed appears beside it: +1.3 dB for the
untouched file, −7.4 dB for the squeezed one.

They finish level. That is the whole beat: one of them fought hard to be higher
and arrives in exactly the same place.

Carries a footnote that targets differ between platforms and change over time —
the behaviour is what generalises, not the number.

## B04 — `LoudRangeLost` (new)

**Brief:** the permanent half of the trade, with proof that it is permanent.

Two vertical spans measuring the distance between the quietest and loudest
moments: 12.0 LU, then 0.7 LU. The second is visibly almost nothing, which is
the point — you can see 94% of something disappear.

Underneath, a two-row proof table. The same volume change was applied to both
files; the `loudness` row is stamped **MOVED** with the before/after figures,
the `the gap` row is stamped **DID NOT BUDGE** with identical figures either
side of the arrow. Neutral grey for the thing that moved, terracotta for the
thing that did not.

That table is the reel's strongest claim and it is entirely measured.

## The portrait siblings

**Brief for all five:** reflow the logic, never shrink the landscape layout.
Type sized off `height`; everything inside the active band (y 230–1440).

| Sibling | The rotation |
|---|---|
| `LoudPeakVsLoudness916` | each version becomes a **block** — label, full-width waveform, then the two readouts beneath. The shared ceiling still crosses both |
| `LoudLufsWindow916` | the waveform keeps its horizontal time axis; the two reading cards **stack**, so each explanatory line fits on one line |
| `LoudNormalizeGain916` | **portrait is the better frame** — the loudness axis is already vertical, so the plot gets taller and the 7.4 dB drop reads longer |
| `LoudRangeLost916` | spans stay vertical and get taller; proof rows move their detail to a **second line** so nothing truncates |
| `HaiTitleOutro916` | same two rules and three elements, centred **within the active band** rather than the frame — a frame-centred card would put the handle behind platform UI |
