# PEDAGOGY — "Why Your Track Gets Turned Down"

Narration sign-off. The audience is explicitly **not** audio professionals —
Rohan said so when he picked the topic:

> keep in mind that a lot of people are not audio professionals. so they might
> not understand what a master or a squeezed mix is so keep that in mind

The reel fails if a viewer who has never opened a DAW comes away unsure what
they were supposed to stop doing.

## The one thing this reel has to land

**Loudness is borrowed. The gap is spent.** Pushing a track louder is a trade
where one side gets reversed and the other does not. The platform gives the
loudness back; nobody gives the gap back.

## Vocabulary discipline — the whole point of this reel

| Avoided | Used instead |
|---|---|
| master | "your finished track", "the file you upload" — or nothing |
| mix | never used |
| dynamics / dynamic range | **the gap** between your quiet and loud parts |
| compression / limiting | **squeezed** — "I pulled the quiet moments up until they nearly matched the loud ones" |
| peak / true peak | **the tallest point**, defined as "one instant, the single loudest sample" |
| LUFS | appears once, in a footnote that says you do not need the acronym |
| headroom | "about a decibel under the ceiling" |
| normalisation | "the platform turns the whole track up or down to match a target" |
| K-weighting | "weighted the way your ear hears" |

The one unavoidable unit is **LU** for the gap, shown as a label beside a
number whose meaning is established by the picture: a tall span versus a flat
one. The viewer never needs to know what the letters stand for.

## Deliberate decisions

**The experiment came first.** Measurements were taken before the script was
written, so the narration describes what happened. That ordering also caught
the two errors below.

**Two narration errors were caught by the factcheck and fixed, not shipped.**
A draft said the peaks were "half a decibel apart" (measured: 0.8 dB) and that
the squeezed version "sounds nearly nine times louder" — which treats an 8.7 LU
difference on a logarithmic scale as a ratio. That second one is a real error,
not a rounding. Both lines were rewritten and B01's audio regenerated. Recorded
in [FACTCHECK.md](./FACTCHECK.md) notes 1 and 2.

**No platform is named in the graphics.** Spotify appears once, in B00's typed
command, because that is how a viewer would actually phrase the question. Every
on-screen label says "the platform", and B03's footnote says targets differ and
change.

**The advice does not require equipment.** B05's four steps are decisions, not
plugin settings. A volunteer exporting from Suno can act on all four.

**"Loud is temporary" is the closing frame, not "louder is worse".** The reel
is not an argument against loudness; it is an argument that loudness is not
bankable and the gap is.
