Measured on this machine, 2026-09-10, with ffmpeg's EBU R128 scanner.
Nothing in this reel is quoted from memory.

METHOD
A 30s synthetic music-like signal was built with real crest factor: a
sustained three-note chord bed, a decaying low hit and a short high tick on
every beat, plus a genuinely quieter passage from 10s to 18s.

  ffmpeg -filter_complex "sine=f=110...,sine=f=180,volume='0.80*exp(-22*mod(t,1))'..."

Two versions were made from that one source:

  A  "kept its dynamics"  — gain only, brought up until the loudest moment
                            nearly touches the ceiling.
       -af "volume=18.0dB,alimiter=limit=0.891"

  B  "pushed loud"        — compressed hard, then driven into a limiter, so
                            the quiet parts are pulled up to meet the loud ones.
       -af "volume=12.5dB,acompressor=threshold=0.02:ratio=20:makeup=18,
            volume=5dB,alimiter=limit=0.82"

Measured with:
  ffmpeg -i <file> -filter_complex ebur128=peak=true -f null -

RESULTS
file                           LUFS      LRA   true peak
--------------------------------------------------------
A_dynamic.wav                 -15.3     12.0        -1.0
B_loud.wav                     -6.6      0.7        -0.2
B_at_target.wav               -14.0      0.7        -7.6   (B with -7.4 dB)
A_same_gain.wav               -22.7     12.0        -8.4   (A with -7.4 dB)

WHAT THE NUMBERS SHOW

1. A PEAK METER CANNOT SEE LOUDNESS.
   A peaks at -1.0 dBFS, B at -0.2 dBFS — 0.8 dB apart, visually identical on
   a peak meter. But B measures 8.7 LU louder. Peak describes one instant;
   loudness describes the whole track.

2. PUSHING LOUD COSTS LOUDNESS RANGE.
   LRA falls from 12.0 LU to 0.7 LU — a 94% reduction. The distance between
   the quiet parts and the loud parts is almost entirely gone.

3. THE PLATFORM ERASES THE LOUDNESS ADVANTAGE.
   To reach a -14 LUFS playback target:
       A needs  +1.3 dB   (almost nothing)
       B needs  -7.4 dB   (a large cut)
   B's entire 8.7 LU advantage is removed before anyone hears it.

4. GAIN MOVES LOUDNESS BUT NEVER RESTORES RANGE.
   The same -7.4 dB applied to both files:
       B: -6.6 -> -14.0 LUFS,  LRA unchanged at 0.7
       A: -15.3 -> -22.7 LUFS, LRA unchanged at 12.0
   Turning a file up or down cannot give back range that was compressed out.
   The loss is permanent; the loudness was never permanent to begin with.

CAVEATS, STATED PLAINLY
- This is a synthetic signal, not a commercial song. It was chosen so the
  crest factor and the quiet passage are known exactly rather than guessed.
  The mechanism generalises; the specific numbers belong to this file.
- -14 LUFS is used as the playback target because it is the figure most
  commonly published for streaming normalisation. Platforms differ, change
  their targets, and some apply a limiter when turning quiet tracks up.
  The reel says "about -14" and does not name a platform's internals.
- LRA (loudness range, EBU R128) is the measure used for "the distance between
  the quiet and loud parts". It is not the same as crest factor or dynamic
  range in the peak sense.
