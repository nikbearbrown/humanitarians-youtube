IT LEARNS FROM WHAT YOU DO
Spotify says the most important input is your taste profile: what you search, play, skip and save. There are no
star ratings. It also looks at people with similar taste: collaborative filtering.

A GRID THAT'S MOSTLY EMPTY
Picture listeners down the side, songs across the top, and most boxes empty. Matrix factorisation fills it in by
giving every listener and every song a short list of hidden taste numbers; a song's score is how well the two
lists match.

I BUILT ONE TO TEST
A made-up service, not Spotify's data: 3,000 listeners, 1,600 songs, 24 taste numbers each. It was never told a
genre, yet a song's five closest songs were the same genre 84% of the time. With a fifth of each listener's songs
hidden, 74% of listeners got at least one back in ten picks; just recommending the hits got 65%, random 6%. Only
6% of its picks came from the less popular half of the songs, where a quarter of the listening happens, and a
brand-new song with no plays appeared in nobody's list. Giving two of every ten slots to songs few people had
heard brought almost the whole catalogue into play.

WHAT TO DO
Play, skip and save on purpose, exclude playlists like sleep sounds from your taste profile, and go looking for
new music yourself.
