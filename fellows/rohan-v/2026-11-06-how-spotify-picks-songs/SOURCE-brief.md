# SOURCE-brief — "How Spotify Picks Songs"

## What was asked for

One of two STEM videos for the week of 2–6 Nov, following the current Brutalist framework (see
[PROMPT.md](./PROMPT.md)). It pairs with this week's Google Maps video: both are apps that learn from what
millions of people do.

## Who it is for

Lyrical Literacy fellows and the wider Humanitarians AI audience: people who use Discover Weekly or a daily mix
and have wondered why it keeps suggesting the same kind of song. Nothing technical assumed.

## What it must leave them with

There are no ratings: the app learns from what you play, skip and save, and from people with similar taste. It
turns every listener and every song into a short list of taste numbers and recommends the closest matches. That
works better than random and only a little better than the charts, it leans on the hits, and a song nobody has
played can't be recommended. So: use the signals on purpose, exclude playlists that aren't your taste, and go
looking for new music yourself.

## Evidence the reel stands on

`recs.py` and its outputs in `evidence/`; the 2008 implicit-feedback paper and the 2009 matrix-factorisation
article for the method; Spotify's own recommendations and help pages. Details in [SOURCES.md](./SOURCES.md).
