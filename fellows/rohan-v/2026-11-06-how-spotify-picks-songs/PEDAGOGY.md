# PEDAGOGY — "How Spotify Picks Songs"

How the reel is built to teach someone who uses recommendations every day but has never been told how they work.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: one picture (a grid that's mostly empty) before
any mechanism, every step shown on one made-up service small enough to check, measured against "just the hits"
and random, and an ending on three things to do.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the question; Spotify's recipe is unpublished | expect a working model, not Spotify's secret |
| B01 SIGNALS | plays, skips, saves; people with similar taste | name what the app learns from |
| B02 THE GRID | listeners × songs, mostly empty; 24 taste numbers each | say what matrix factorisation fills in |
| B03 WHAT IT LEARNED | genres it was never told; cosine similarity | explain why "similar songs" cluster |
| B04 THE TEST | 74% against 65% against 6% | judge how much better than the charts it is |
| B05 THE CATCH | 6% of picks from the less popular half; cold start | expect a lean toward hits, and new songs left out |
| B06 EXPLORE | two slots in ten for the unknown | see why apps deliberately show less certain picks |
| B07 WHAT TO DO | signals on purpose, exclude, search | steer their own recommendations |
| B08 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **A service with known answers.** The genres are known because the data was built from them, so "it found the
  genres itself" can be graded; the narration says the service is made up and never calls it Spotify's.
- **The honest baseline.** B04 shows "just the hits" doing nearly as well, so the viewer sees the size of the gain
  instead of a big number alone.
- **The narration follows the measurement.** The feedback loop broadened the lists instead of narrowing them, so
  there is no filter-bubble claim; B06 is framed as exploration, which the run did measure.
