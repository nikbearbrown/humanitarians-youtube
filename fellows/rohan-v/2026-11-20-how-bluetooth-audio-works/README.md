## This week's contribution

**Question.** How does music get from a phone to wireless earbuds, and why does it lag or cut out?

**Prediction.** No prediction was written down before the build. The working assumption going in was that Bluetooth's
lag comes mostly from compressing the music (the codec), and that Wi-Fi is what makes it cut out.

**What I built/tried.** `bluetooth.py`: a synthetic pop track (60 s, CD quality) encoded with the real codecs in ffmpeg
(SBC at the High Quality setting, aptX, aptX HD, and ffmpeg's AAC at 256k), data rates measured from the encoded bytes;
then a simulated A2DP link: packets of 14.5 ms of music hopping among 79 channels, a busy Wi-Fi network on 23 of them,
body-blocking moments, re-sends, and the headphones' buffer, with and without adaptive hopping and for six buffer sizes.
The track is played through the link with a 50 ms and a 200 ms buffer (listening clips). The six body scenes are three.js
stages in HyperFrames (`build_hf.py`, `hyperframes/scenes/`).

**Observed result.** CD quality is 1,411.2 kbit/s; SBC used 330.7 kbit/s (1/4.3), aptX 352.8, aptX HD 529.2, ffmpeg's AAC
272.1. Outside the body-blocking moments, 17.4% of tries failed with a busy Wi-Fi network and 0.9% with adaptive hopping;
most of what remained came from body blocking. In two minutes, a 50 ms buffer glitched 30 times, 100 ms 19, 200 ms 3 and
300 ms never. The codecs' own frames are milliseconds long; the reserve that rides out the losses is hundreds, so the lag
part of the assumption was wrong, and body blocking, not Wi-Fi, caused most of the remaining gaps once hopping was on.

**Next experiment.** Measure a real phone and earbuds (latency with a loopback recording) against the simulation's
buffer sizes, and add LC3 once an encoder is available to compare it with SBC at half the data rate.
