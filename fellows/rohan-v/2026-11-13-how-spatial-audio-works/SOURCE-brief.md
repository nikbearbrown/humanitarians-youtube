# SOURCE-brief — "How Spatial Audio Works"

## What was asked for

One of two STEM videos for the week of 9–13 Nov, following the current Brutalist framework (see
[PROMPT.md](./PROMPT.md)), with its motion beats built in HyperFrames and three.js. Rohan asked for an
audio-related topic and chose spatial audio. It pairs with this week's other explainer, "How Netflix Streams".

## Who it is for

Lyrical Literacy fellows and the wider Humanitarians AI audience: people who wear earbuds every day and have
noticed a film or a song seem to come from around them, but have never been told how two small speakers do that.
Nothing technical assumed.

## What it must leave them with

Two ears find a sound with a tiny delay (about two thirds of a millisecond at most) and with the shadow of the head,
which dims high sounds in the far ear. Those cues can't tell front from back; the outer ear's folds can. Measure
how a head filters sound from every direction, run a sound through one direction's pair, and headphones play it
from there. Head tracking keeps it put when you turn, and a profile tuned to your ears helps with front and back.
So: use headphones with head tracking, set up the personalized profile, and pick music mixed in Dolby Atmos.
