## What is new about this reel's motion

Earlier reels on this channel drove reveals off hand-picked fractions of
`durationInFrames`. That is guessing: the motion lands *near* the phrase it
illustrates, and the drift is what makes an explainer feel loose. This reel is
bound to the **word clock** — `align.py` measures when every word is actually
spoken, `sync_cues.py` resolves an anchor phrase to a fraction, and the
components read those fractions from their props. The door locks still on "does
not move at all".

Four disciplines carried across all four new components:

1. **Exclusive emphasis** (`exclusive()` in `cueKit`) — a highlight retires as
   the next arrives, so the eye is always directed at exactly one thing.
2. **Continuous motion** — scrolling lanes, a breathing cone, a door that swings
   on a sine and then jitters against itself. A graphic held still for twenty
   seconds reads as a screenshot.
3. **Shared motifs** (`cueKit`) — one waveform generator, one sine generator,
   one spark line and one head block across every component in both week-04
   reels.
4. **Shell early, content on the word.** A beat's *layout* arrives when its
   section is introduced; the thing that teaches waits for the phrase that earns
   it. Adopted after Gate V failed B02 for underfill — see
   [BUILD-LOG.md](./BUILD-LOG.md).

## Type

Every scene loads its fonts through `lib/haiType.ts`: `Source Serif 4` for
display and prose, `Inter` for labels and data. Both are bundled via
`@remotion/google-fonts`, whose `loadFont()` blocks the render until the face is
ready — a determinism fix as much as a fidelity one.

## B00 — `ClaudeComposerAsk` (chassis)

The channel's fixed opener, unchanged. The three output lines pre-load the reel:
stereo means two slightly different sides; one speaker has to add them together;
opposite parts add up to nothing.

## B01 — `MonoTwoSidesOneSpeaker` (new)

**Brief:** establish three facts — music has two sides, headphones keep them
apart, most speakers cannot — and land on the consequence, without a single
audio term.

- **Two lanes**, LEFT in ink and RIGHT in blue, carrying visibly *different*
  waves. The difference is the point: SLIGHTLY DIFFERENT — THAT IS THE WIDTH.
- **Left card, headphones:** two separate cups, two separate paths descending
  from the two lanes. *kept apart — each ear gets its own side.*
- **Right card, one speaker:** PHONE · LAPTOP · SMART SPEAKER, and a single cone
  whose rings breathe on `noise2D` so it reads as something producing sound.
- **The event:** both paths bend inward and meet at a terracotta **+**. *added
  first — one cone, one position.*

Both cards stay on screen together. The beat is a contrast, and a contrast needs
its halves visible at the same instant.

## B02 — `MonoDoorToWaves` (new)

**Brief:** make cancellation obvious with an object, and make the mapping to
audio one sentence long.

- **The door**, on a hinge, rotated with a CSS 3D `rotateY` about its left edge.
  Two figures either side, each with an arrow showing which way they push.
- **Phase 1:** both arrows one way. The door swings open and back on a sine —
  genuinely moving. IT SWINGS OPEN.
- **Phase 2:** the right arrow flips. The door pins to zero with a faint jitter
  against itself. NOTHING MOVES · *both of them working just as hard.*
- **The bridge:** *Your track's two sides do exactly this — and the speaker is
  the door.*
- **The same thing as waves:** LEFT and RIGHT lanes, the right one phase-flipping
  as the pushing goes opposite, and a SUM lane beneath a rule that collapses to a
  flat line reading NOTHING.

The door moving is the whole beat. **A bug worth recording:** in an earlier pass
both arrows pointed the same way during the opposed state, contradicting the
narration on screen. `person(1, opposed > 0.5 ? -1 : 1, …)` fixes it; it was
caught by reading the contact sheet frame by frame, not by any gate.

## B03 — `MonoWhatSurvives` (new)

**Brief:** show the measured result in a way that predicts rather than reports.

- **Three rows** — bass, vocal, wide airy layer. Each carries a **stereo-field
  strip**: one dot in the middle, or two dots pushed to the edges.
- **Then the outcome**, as a bar plus a verdict: *unchanged*, *unchanged*,
  *−49.5 dB — effectively gone*.
- **Provenance:** a MEASURED badge naming the method, and saying the track was
  built for the measurement.

Position before outcome is the design. A viewer can see which parts are in the
middle *before* any bar moves, so the result reads as predictable — and that is
the thing they can apply to their own mix.

Position markers use `Circle` from `@remotion/shapes` rather than a div with
`borderRadius: 999`: a real SVG circle stays perfectly round when the 1080
layout is rendered at 2× for 4K, where a rounded div can show flat spots.

## B04 — `MonoHolesNotSilence` (new)

**Brief:** volunteer the caveat before the evidence, and show the shape of the
real result without a single number.

- **The caveat first:** *Total silence is the clean example. In a real track it
  is almost never that dramatic.*
- **Where width comes from:** two sine paths, one dashed and arriving slightly
  late. SAME SOUND, SLIGHTLY LATE.
- **The comb:** a strip of solid segments, low notes to high. Roughly a third of
  them **punch out** on "lose holes out of it", comb-spaced by a deterministic
  function of the segment count.
- **The axis carries no numbers, on purpose.** LOW NOTES → HIGH NOTES. The
  measurement behind this beat predicted nulls at 830 Hz and 2500 Hz and landed
  on all four predicted positions; it is the strongest result in either week-04
  reel and it is not on screen. See [PEDAGOGY.md](./PEDAGOGY.md).
- **The verdict:** *You do not lose the layer. You lose holes out of it — which
  is why a folded-down mix sounds hollow rather than silent.*
