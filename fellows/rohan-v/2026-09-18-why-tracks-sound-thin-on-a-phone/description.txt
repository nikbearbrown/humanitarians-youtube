Why Your Track Sounds Thin On A Phone — Humanitarians AI

Your track sounds full and wide in your headphones. You send it to a friend,
they play it on their phone, and it comes back thin and hollow. Nothing broke.
Part of your music is cancelling itself out — and it is completely predictable
once you can see it.

WRITTEN FOR SOMEONE WHO HAS NEVER OPENED A DAW
No jargon. Nothing is "out of phase" — two people push a door. Push the same
way and it swings open. Push against each other, just as hard, and it does not
move at all. Both of them are working. Nothing happens. Adding two sides of a
track together does exactly this, and the speaker is the door.

Recorded music has two sides, a left and a right, slightly different from each
other — and that difference is what makes music feel wide. Headphones keep them
apart. A phone, a laptop, a smart speaker: that is one speaker, and one speaker
cannot play two things at once. It has to add them together first.

THE NUMBERS WERE MEASURED, NOT LOOKED UP
Two experiments, run before the script was written.

A 20-second stereo track was built with three parts: bass and vocal identical
on both sides, and an airy layer inverted on the right. Then it was summed to a
single speaker and each part measured:

  110 Hz bass, centred      unchanged, 0.0 dB
  440 Hz vocal, centred     unchanged, 0.0 dB
  3 kHz air, spread wide    -49.5 dB, effectively gone

What survives one speaker is whatever sits in the middle.

Second experiment, and this one was PREDICTED FIRST. Most stereo width comes
from delaying one side by a fraction of a millisecond, and summing a signal
with a delayed copy cancels at specific, calculable frequencies. For a 0.6 ms
delay the prediction was nulls at 833 Hz and 2500 Hz, with peaks between them.
Measured: -10.2 dB at 830, -0.4 at 1660, -9.8 at 2500, -0.6 at 3300. All four
predicted positions landed.

So in a real track you do not lose the whole layer. You lose holes out of it.
That is why a folded-down mix sounds hollow rather than silent — and hollow is
the thing to listen for.

THE FIX COSTS TEN SECONDS
Before you export, switch your track to mono and listen once. If the vocal
jumps forward and the air disappears, you have found it. The usual culprits are
stereo wideners, doubled parts, and wide reverb on something that should be
sitting in the centre. Keep the bass and the lead voice in the middle — those
are what has to survive.

A NOTE ON THE NUMBERS
Both signals were built for the measurement, so their placement is known
exactly rather than guessed. The mechanism generalises; the specific figures
belong to these files. The nulls measure about -10 dB rather than minus
infinity for two reasons: the analysis filter is 120 Hz wide and collects
energy from frequencies that are not cancelling, and the delayed layer is
broadband noise rather than a pure tone. That is exactly why the video says
holes, not silence.
