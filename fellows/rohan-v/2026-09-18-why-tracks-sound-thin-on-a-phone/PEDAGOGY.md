# PEDAGOGY — "Why Your Track Sounds Thin On A Phone"

How this reel is built to teach, and the corrections that shaped it.

## The brief that set the register

Rohan, on both week-04 reels:

> Do the same for the other video as well. Make sure it is understood by a
> general audience. Explain the scenario where one might encounter such a topic
> or a setting such as bit depth and the other video too.

and then, after the rewrite went too far the other way:

> Okay. and dont make the video too high level either. it has to be a good
> balance. at the end of the day the viewer has to learn somthing

The first says *start from the scenario, assume nothing*; the second says *the
reel still has to contain a mechanism*. The structure below holds both.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | name the scenario they have actually lived | recognise the symptom |
| B01 BACKGROUND | two sides, headphones vs one speaker | say why a phone is different from headphones |
| B02 ANALOGY → IDEA | a door two people push | explain adding-to-nothing without the word phase |
| B03 MECHANISM | measured: what survived and what didn't | predict which parts of a mix are at risk |
| B04 SUBTLER CASE | holes, not silence | recognise the real symptom, which is hollowness |
| B05 WHAT TO DO | the ten-second mono check | catch it before anyone else hears it |
| B06 OUTRO | the through-line | repeat it to someone else |

The word "cancel" does no work until B02. B00 says *part of your music is
cancelling itself out* as a promise, and the reel then spends 42 seconds earning
it.

## Vocabulary: what is never said

| Not used | Used instead |
|---|---|
| phase, out of phase, anti-phase | *opposite*, *pushing against each other* |
| polarity, inverted | *one pushes while the other pulls* |
| mono fold-down, summing | *one speaker has to add them together* |
| comb filtering | *holes*, *only certain frequencies cancel* |
| null, notch | *holes punched out of it* |
| destructive interference | *adding two opposite things gives you nothing* |
| correlation, mid/side | not needed at all |
| stereo image, stereo field | *where it sits*, *the centre*, *both sides* |
| Hz, kHz (in B04) | *low notes* to *high notes* — the axis carries no numbers |

"Decibel" is kept once, in B03, because −49.5 dB is the reel's one hard number
and the bar beside it does the interpreting.

## Why a door

Cancellation analogies usually reach for water waves or two people on a
see-saw. Both fail the same way: they explain *addition*, but not the thing that
makes cancellation surprising — that **both sources are still working at full
strength**.

A door fixes that:

1. **Both pushers are visibly exerting effort.** The frame says *both of them
   working just as hard* while the door sits dead still. That is the whole
   counter-intuition, and it arrives without a single word of physics.
2. **It moves.** A viewer sees the door swing when the arrows agree and refuse to
   move when they oppose. A static diagram of two arrows is a physics figure.
3. **The mapping is one sentence.** *Your track's two sides do exactly this — and
   the speaker is the door.* No new vocabulary crosses the bridge.

The door keeps a faint jitter during the opposed state, because a perfectly still
object reads as a paused render rather than a balanced one.

## Why position is shown before outcome

B03 could have been a before/after table. Instead each row shows **where that
part sits in the stereo field** — one dot in the middle, or two dots pushed to
the edges — *before* any bar moves.

That ordering converts the beat from a report into a prediction. A viewer who
sees "centre" next to bass and vocal, and "spread across both sides" next to the
wide layer, can guess what happens next; when it happens, the rule is already
theirs. A table would only have told them what happened to a track they have
never heard.

## Why B04 exists at all, and why it has no numbers

B03 is the clean case, and the clean case is a lie by omission: a fully inverted
layer is something you build on purpose, not something a mix does. Ending there
would leave a viewer listening for *silence* and missing the real symptom.

So B04 volunteers the caveat first — *"Total silence is the clean example. In a
real track it is almost never that dramatic"* — and then shows what a delay
actually does: a comb of solid teeth with holes punched between them.

**And it carries no frequency numbers.** The measurement behind it predicted
nulls at 830 Hz and 2500 Hz and landed on all four predicted positions. That is
the most interesting thing built for either week-04 reel and it is not in the
video, because a viewer meeting stereo for the first time cannot use a
comb-filter chart. The axis says LOW NOTES to HIGH NOTES. The shape is the
lesson, and the shape is what makes the word *hollow* click.

## What was deliberately left out

- **The whole comb-filter frequency table.** Measured, predicted, logged, cut.
- **Mid/side and correlation meters.** The right professional tools, and a second
  vocabulary the reel cannot afford.
- **Haas effect and precedence.** Why a delay reads as width in the first place.
  A better reel than a paragraph.
- **How to fix it in a DAW.** The reel names the culprits and stops. Naming
  specific plugin settings would date instantly and assumes a DAW the viewer may
  not have.
- **Any claim that stereo width is bad.** It is not. B01 says the difference
  between the sides *is* the width — the point is only that a single speaker
  cannot deliver it.

## The one thing a viewer should remember

> What survives one speaker is whatever sits in the middle.

And the action that follows: switch to mono and listen once, before anyone else
hears it.
