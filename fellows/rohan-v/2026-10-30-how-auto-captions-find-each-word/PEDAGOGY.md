# PEDAGOGY — "How Auto-Captions Find Each Word"

How the reel is built to teach someone who uses auto-captions but has never been told how they are made.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: one picture (sound as a spectrogram) before any
mechanism, every step shown on one real sentence, measured on speech whose answers are known, and an ending on
three things to do before publishing.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the question; our own videos are timed by it | name the answer's shape: picture, predict, line up |
| B01 BACKGROUND | the spectrogram; a model that predicts text | say what the model actually reads |
| B02 MECHANISM | word times from where it looked, by time warping | explain where a caption's timing comes from |
| B03 MEASURED | 234 of 238 right, a quarter second early | trust it on clean speech, and know its timing isn't exact |
| B04 NOISE | the error rate as noise rises, and the 0 dB clip | predict when captions will fall apart |
| B05 THE CATCH | words never said; the name and price it "corrects" | doubt confident text, especially names and numbers |
| B06 WHAT TO DO | read them, check names and numbers, record quietly | publish captions they have checked |
| B07 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **Speech with known answers.** Built word by word, so every true start is known exactly; the test says it was
  built that way, and the timing result is stated as my test's.
- **The narration only claims what both runs agree on.** The two noisiest results vary between runs, so the
  narration says "over seventy percent" and "over three hundred", and the screen shows the final run.
- **B05 uses the sister reel's evidence.** The name and price are the same clips the voice reel plays, so the two
  videos tell one story: the voice says the sounds, the recogniser writes what it expects.
