# PEDAGOGY — "How AI Voices Read a Script"

How the reel is built to teach someone who uses an AI voice but has never been told how it reads.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: the reel starts from the one fact that explains
everything after it (the voice only gets the sounds), shows each step on our own narrator, and ends on three
things to do when writing a script.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the question, about the voice they are hearing | name the answer's shape: clean up, sounds, voice |
| B01 BACKGROUND | three steps; the voice never sees the spelling | say what the voice model actually receives |
| B02 MECHANISM | letters to sounds: a dictionary, then rules | explain why a new word or name gets guessed |
| B03 MEASURED | 16 heteronym sentences, 10 right | predict that "read" or "lead" can come out wrong |
| B04 NUMBERS | the clean-up step: six of seven, not the price | spot the kind of text that trips it up |
| B05 THE FIX | respelling, on the narrator's own mistakes, heard | fix a mispronounced word themselves |
| B06 WHAT TO DO | numbers as words, respell names, reword | write a script an AI voice reads right |
| B07 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **Our own narrator as the evidence.** Every IPA string is from the step that voices this video, so the viewer
  hears the subject while learning about it, and the mistakes shown are real ones from earlier videos.
- **B01 sets up B03 to B05.** Once the voice is known to see only sounds, every later mistake has one explanation.
- **The narration practises the fix.** It says "dhole", "sitaar" and "Row-Haan" because the plain spellings would
  have been misread, which is B05's point.
