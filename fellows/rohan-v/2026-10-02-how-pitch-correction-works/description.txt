How Pitch Correction Works — Humanitarians AI

How does a tool like Auto-Tune hear that a note is slightly off, and pull it into tune? It
can't hear the song the way you do. It measures, very fast. This is how, explained from
nothing: no music theory or maths needed.

A NOTE IS A VIBRATION THAT REPEATS
The A above middle C repeats 440 times a second: 440 hertz. Double it and you're an octave
up. Between two neighbouring piano keys, musicians count 100 cents.

FINDING THE PITCH
Slide a copy of the sound along itself. When it has moved exactly one cycle, the two line up
and the difference drops almost to zero. That shift is the period; one over the period is the
pitch. The first Auto-Tune, in 1997, was built on this idea, called autocorrelation.

I BUILT ONE AND MEASURED IT
A small detector and corrector, run on a synthesised test melody: on average 40 cents out
before, and under 3 cents when measured again after correction. You hear both.

SPEED, AND THE KEY
Correct instantly and the vibrato disappears: the robotic "Cher effect" of 1998. Correct
slowly and the voice keeps moving. And a corrector picks the nearest note, not the one you
meant: set the key first, or it will land, perfectly in tune, on the wrong note.
