## What is new about this reel's motion

Earlier reels on this channel drove reveals off hand-picked fractions of
`durationInFrames`. That is guessing: the motion lands *near* the phrase it
illustrates, and the drift is what makes an explainer feel loose. This reel is
bound to the **word clock** — `align.py` measures when every word is actually
spoken, `sync_cues.py` resolves an anchor phrase to a fraction, and the
components read those fractions from their props. The samples snap on "pushed to
the nearest one".

Four disciplines carried across all four new components:

1. **Exclusive emphasis** (`exclusive()` in `cueKit`) — a highlight retires as
   the next arrives, so the eye is always directed at exactly one thing. Without
   it every stage of a beat is lit by its end and the frame reads as five
   competing emphases rather than a walkthrough.
2. **Continuous motion** — live hiss traces, a wave whose amplitude wanders on
   `noise2D`. A graphic held still for twenty seconds reads as a screenshot.
3. **Shared motifs** (`cueKit`) — one waveform generator, one hiss generator,
   one spark line and one head block across every component in both week-04
   reels, so eight new scenes do not each reinvent a wave.
4. **Shell early, content on the word.** A beat's *layout* — card, panel, axis,
   footnote — arrives as soon as the section is introduced; the thing that
   teaches still waits for the phrase that earns it. This was adopted after
   Gate V failed four beats for underfill: the canvas-fill law measures the
   bounding box of all ink, so a frame with an empty lower half at its midpoint
   is a real defect and not a gate artefact. See [BUILD-LOG.md](./BUILD-LOG.md).

## B01 — `BitSoundToNumbers` (new)

**Brief:** get a viewer from "sound" to "a list of numbers" without a single
audio term, then show that storing it answers *two* questions — and say which one
this video is about.

- **Chain, left to right:** a wave whose amplitude wanders on Perlin noise
  (`@remotion/noise`) so it reads as a live signal rather than a drawing → a
  microphone, capsule lighting as the wave reaches it → a row of signed decimals
  sampled from the wave above them.
- **The fork:** two cards. *How often do I take a measurement?* → SAMPLE RATE,
  glossed "not this video". *How precisely do I write each one down?* → BIT
  DEPTH, glossed "this is bit depth".
- **The decision:** on "the second one", question 1 dims to 45% and a full-width
  terracotta bar under question 2 reads THIS IS WHAT THIS VIDEO IS ABOUT.

Naming sample rate and then setting it aside is the point. Every beginner
conflates the two numbers.

## B02 — `BitStaircase` (new)

**Brief:** make precision physical with an object from a drawer, then put that
same object on a sound.

- **Left:** two rulers, same length. Four markings, captioned *you can only be
  roughly right*; then sixteen markings in terracotta, captioned *same length —
  just more precise*. A counter ticks to 256 MARKINGS AT 8-BIT.
- **Right:** a sound wave, with the fine ruler's markings drawn across it as a
  ladder. Every sample dot then **snaps** to its nearest rung; the smooth
  original stays behind at 26% as a ghost.
- **Honesty, on screen:** *"ladder drawn with 16 markings so they stay visible —
  256 markings would be solid."* The caption is generated from the constant that
  draws the rungs, so it cannot drift from the drawing.

Both rulers stay up while the wave snaps. The analogy is the explanation, not a
stepping stone.

## B03 — `BitGapIsHiss` (new)

**Brief:** the causal chain in the fewest possible moves — one error is nothing,
tens of thousands a second are a sound, and a coarser ruler makes that sound
louder.

- **One error:** a sample's true position, and a terracotta drop to the marking
  it had to take, labelled OFF BY THIS MUCH.
- **How often:** a counter to 44,100, captioned *tiny on its own — not tiny all
  together*.
- **The sound:** the music thins to 30% and a hiss trace appears beneath it,
  reseeded every two frames so it genuinely moves. A FAINT HISS, UNDER
  EVERYTHING.
- **The tie-back:** two cards — coarse ruler → LOUDER HISS, fine ruler → QUIETER
  HISS, their traces at 4× different amplitudes.

The last move is what makes the beat pay: it connects the ruler from B02 to the
loudness of the hiss, so bit depth and noise become one idea.

## B04 — `BitRoomUnderneath` (new)

**Brief:** spend the reel's only hard numbers, and make them mean something to
someone who has never seen a decibel.

- **One dB axis, two columns.** The 8-bit floor travels to −53; the 16-bit floor
  to −101, with nearly twice the shaded usable room above it. Live hiss rides
  each floor at an amplitude proportional to that floor.
- **The yardstick:** rock concert / conversation / quiet room / a distant
  whisper, laid out one row at a time via `StaggeredMotion` so it reads as a
  scale being built rather than a block of text appearing.
- **The payload:** a dashed rule across the plot — THE ROOM YOU ARE SITTING IN —
  with the 16-bit floor beneath it. *You will never hear it.*
- **Provenance:** a MEASURED badge naming the method, on screen.

Counters use `SPRING_SETTLE` (damping 22): a measured value should arrive and
stop, not overshoot past itself and come back.
