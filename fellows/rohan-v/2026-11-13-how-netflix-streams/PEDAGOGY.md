# PEDAGOGY — "How Netflix Streams"

How the reel is built to teach someone who watches Netflix every week but has never been told how it reaches them.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: two test clips carried through the first four
beats, each idea shown on them and measured, Netflix's own figures kept apart from my run's numbers, and an ending
on what the viewer can do about their own connection.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the question: why it keeps playing when the internet slows | expect three parts, each tested |
| B01 THE LADDER | one show stored at six qualities | say what a "quality level" is, and why there are several |
| B02 VMAF | a score for how it looks; 38, 96, and the grainy clip's 75 | explain why the same bitrate looks different on different pictures |
| B03 PER TITLE | the card reaches 93 at about 4 Mbit/s; the grainy clip never does | say why each show gets its own ladder |
| B04 THE PLAYER | chunks; three ways to choose; the buffer one never stalled | explain why a picture goes soft for a moment instead of stopping |
| B05 CLOSE BY | servers inside your provider; 1.5 s against 0.5 s | say why distance matters even on a fast line |
| B06 SCALE | 18,000 servers and 175 countries by 2022, 325M+ memberships today, AV1 | picture the size of the system, and that codecs keep shrinking it |
| B07 WHAT TO DO | 5 Mbit/s for Full HD, 15 for 4K, a steady line | check their own connection against those numbers |
| B08 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **Two clips for the first four beats.** The same clean card and grainy zoom carry the ladder, the scores and the
  per-title curve, so each new idea lands on a picture the viewer already knows. B01 shows their real frames, so
  the loss of quality at the low rungs is seen, not described.
- **A clean card against grain.** The grainy clip is a deliberately hard case (random grain on fine detail). It
  makes the point of B03 at a glance; the docs say it is synthetic test material, not a show.
- **Netflix's figures and mine are kept apart.** "Netflix built", "Netflix suggests" and "Netflix's network"
  introduce sourced facts; "my test card", "I streamed" and "in my model" introduce every measured number; no beat
  says which algorithm Netflix's player runs.
- **Every Netflix figure keeps its date.** B06 says "by twenty twenty-two" for the server count and "today" only
  for the newest membership figure, and labels the 2022 numbers on screen in both cuts.
- **Three players, not one.** Always-best shows the problem (it stalls), speed-guessing and buffer-watching show two
  answers; the narration claims only what the run measured between them.
