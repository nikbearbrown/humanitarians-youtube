## This week's contribution

**Question.** How does a show keep playing without stopping to load, even when your internet slows down?

**Prediction.** Going in, the working assumption was that always streaming the best quality the connection could
carry would look best, and that a server's distance would matter much less than the connection's speed.

**What I built/tried.** `stream.py`: two test clips made with ffmpeg (a clean test card and a grainy fractal zoom),
each encoded at a six-rung libx264 ladder (235 to 5,800 kbit/s) and scored with VMAF (libvmaf) against the 1080p
original; three players simulated over ten minutes (150 four-second chunks) of a home connection that sags and
then drops; and a TCP slow-start model of one chunk's download from a far server and from one inside the provider.
The six body scenes are three.js stages in HyperFrames (`build_hf.py`, `hyperframes/scenes/`), a first for the series.

**Observed result.** The test card scored 38 at the lowest rung and 96 at the top; the grainy clip topped out at 75.
The card reached a score of 93 at about 3.9 Mbit/s; the grainy clip never did, even at 5.8. Always picking the top
rung stalled for 6.9 s; picking by buffer level never stalled and averaged 5.0 Mbit/s with 13 switches (by speed:
0 s, 4.6 Mbit/s, 17 switches). One chunk took 1.50 s from a far server and 0.49 s from inside the provider. Both
parts of the prediction were wrong.

**Next experiment.** Split a clip into shots and give each shot its own rung (shot-based encoding), and measure
the bits saved at the same VMAF.
