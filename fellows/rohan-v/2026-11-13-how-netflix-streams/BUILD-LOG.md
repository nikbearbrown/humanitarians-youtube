# BUILD-LOG — "How Netflix Streams"

What broke, what it cost, and what changed because of it. Built 9–10 Nov 2026 for the week of 9–13 Nov,
alongside this week's spatial audio reel. Rohan chose the topic and chose HyperFrames for both of the week's
videos, then asked for three.js and "beautiful motion designs". B01–B06 are HyperFrames compositions with three.js
stages; B00, B07 and B08 are the channel's Remotion cards.

## 1 — Evidence: real encodes, a simulated player

Netflix's video, current ladder and player aren't available, so `stream.py` builds a small version of each part:
two 10-second 1920×1080 test clips generated by ffmpeg (a clean test card, `testsrc2`; a grainy zoom, `mandelbrot`
with added noise), each encoded with libx264 at six rungs from 235 to 5,800 kbit/s and scored with VMAF through
ffmpeg's libvmaf; the bitrate each needs for a VMAF of 93; three players (always-highest, throughput-based,
buffer-based) streaming 150 four-second chunks over a simulated connection that sags and drops (seed 1113); and a
TCP slow-start model of one chunk's download from a far and a near server. Big intermediate files, the encodes
included, go to `D:/Rohan/Claude/HyperFrames/tmp/stream`, out of the reel folder.

- **libvmaf and the drive letter.** libvmaf rejected a `log_path` containing `D:`: the colon breaks ffmpeg's filter
  syntax. The score is now written to a relative `vmaf.json`, with ffmpeg run from the work folder (`cwd`).
- **The run, briefly** (`evidence/run.log`): card 37.5 → 96.0, grainy 10.5 → 74.8; the card reaches 93 at 3,947
  kbit/s, the grainy clip not by 5,800; always-highest stalled 6.9 s, throughput-based and buffer-based 0.0 s
  (average 4,572 and 5,006 kbit/s); one chunk 1.50 s from 150 ms away, 0.49 s from 8 ms away.
- **B01's frames.** `evidence/rungs/*.png`, one frame of every rung of both clips, are cut from `stream.py`'s
  encodes in the work folder: `ffmpeg -ss 2.5 -i <WORK>/<clip>_<kbps>.mp4 -frames:v 1 -vf
  "scale=1920:1080:flags=bicubic,crop=1280:720:320:180" evidence/rungs/<clip>_<kbps>.png`. `build_hf.py` copies
  them into `hyperframes/assets/rungs/`.
- **Sanity checks.** The throughput-based player also never stalled, so the narration claims the buffer one's edge
  only as "streamed at a higher quality": a higher average bitrate (and fewer switches), not a picture score. The
  near-server time is close to the line's own limit (0.46 s), so the gap is mostly round trips on a fresh
  connection; the round trips are my choices. The grainy clip's grain is random noise, a harsher case than film
  grain.

## 2 — Script

`author_sheet.py` writes `beat_sheet.json` and `cues.json`. Every spoken number comes from `evidence/run.log` or
`week-12/_research/facts-netflix.md`, with the run's numbers introduced as "my test card", "I streamed", "in my
model". Plain language for a beginner audience, a plain title ("How Netflix Streams"), and the handoff name
`NetflixStreaming_RohanV`.

The first fact check (FACTCHECK, "Flags, corrected") raised three wordings, all changed in `author_sheet.py` and the
sheet regenerated:

- **B04.** "…and looked better than picking by speed" became "…and streamed at a higher quality than picking by
  speed": the run measured bitrate, not how it looked.
- **B06.** "That network has about eighteen thousand servers…" (Dec 2022 figures in the present tense, on the fact
  sheet's "do not say" list) became "By twenty twenty-two, that network had…", then "Today it serves more than
  three hundred and twenty-five million memberships." Both cuts now label the servers and countries "· 2022".
- **B07.** "A steady connection matters more than a fast one" became "…as much as a fast one", the fact sheet's
  safe wording. The card's "Steady beats fast: use a cable if Wi-Fi drops." (the cable tip was unsourced) became
  "A steady connection matters too." (phone: "Keep it steady").

## 4 — HyperFrames + three.js

- **The split.** Claude recommended three.js for the 3D stage and the HUD kept in HTML; Rohan approved. Each beat
  is one page per aspect (`hyperframes/B0x-l.html`, 3840×2160; `B0x-v.html`, 2160×3840): a WebGL canvas drawn by

- **Downloads, approved by Rohan.** three.js 0.187.0 (MIT), and Netflix's N logo from Wikimedia Commons
  (`Netflix_2016_N_logo.svg`, as `hyperframes/assets/netflix-01.svg`) for LOGO LAW. three.js ships inside
  `hyperframes/assets/` (`three.module.min.js`, `three.core.min.js`, `three-LICENSE.txt`); the week's copy is in
  `week-12/_vendor/`.
- **three.core.js.** `three.module.min.js` imports `./three.core.js`, so `three.core.min.js` had to be added and
  the imports rewritten to it.
- **Shared kit.** `kit3.js` (three.js helpers) and `hfkit.py` (the page, HUD and phone layout) serve both of this
  week's films, with `add_qc.py` and `vertical_strings.py`. The kit ships in this folder as `_kit/`; `build_hf.py`

  build.

## 5 — Snapshot review fixes

Frames of every beat in both aspects were snapshotted (`_snap/`, `../snap.sh`) and looked at before rendering:

- **B02 columns** were too fat and too close together; narrowed and spaced.
- **3D labels in the wrong place.** Labels placed over 3D points were projected with a camera matrix from before the
  frame's camera move. `kit3.js` `pin` now calls `cam.updateMatrixWorld()` before projecting.
- **Phone labels colliding.** B02's rung labels overlapped on the phone; only the end labels (235 and 5,800 kbit/s)
  are kept there.
- **B05 labels.** YOU and YOUR PROVIDER overlapped on the globe; each label now has its own offset.
- **Globe too dim.** The globe was brightened.
- **Phone layout.** Rohan's rule from week 11 carried over: no overlapping panels or text on the phone; if it
  doesn't fit, remove text from the bottom and use the room to space the panels. The phone HUD is one evenly spaced
  column under the stage, with no closing line, and B03 and B06 drop a label each on the phone.
- **B04 stall marks.** The first build marked a block red when its download outlasted the buffer or took more than
  8 s, so slow downloads that didn't stall were red too, on lanes the narration says never stalled. Now a block is
  red only where the player emptied its buffer (the buffer after that chunk is exactly one 4 s chunk), and
  `build_hf.py` asserts the stall total against run.log: one red block (always-the-best, chunk 50, 6.89 s), none
  in the other two lanes.
- **B04 speed ribbon.** It was plotted per 2 s of clock time on the blocks' spacing, so its dips sat to the right
  of the chunks they slowed. Each sample is now placed at the chunk the buffer-based player was fetching at that
  moment.

Gate pre-check (`../hfgate.py`, Gate V and GATE T tests on frames before `./art final`):

- The CSS ground gradient counted as ink past the title-safe left edge. The landscape background's radial gradient
  was re-centred and darkened, so past that edge it no longer differs from the corner colour by more than Gate V's
  ink threshold; the 3D stage was also moved inside title-safe (from 5.5% of the width).
- A few labels were under the 4K size floor; all HUD type was raised to at least 0.9× the label size.
