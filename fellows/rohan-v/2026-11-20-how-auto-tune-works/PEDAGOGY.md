# PEDAGOGY — "How Auto-Tune Works"

How the reel is built to teach someone who has heard of Auto-Tune but has never been told what it does to a voice.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: one synthetic singer, one short tune and one piano
roll carried through the middle of the video; every number measured on that singer and introduced as mine; then
the viewer hears the three versions for themselves; Antares credited only with what it has published; and an
ending on three things to do.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the question: find the note you sang, move it to the right one | expect a small version, built and tested |
| B01 PITCH | a note is a wave that repeats; slide a copy along until it lines up; my detector within 5 cents 93% of the time | explain how software finds a pitch |
| B02 OFF KEY | a semitone is 100 cents; my singer about 29 cents off on average, never a wrong note | say what "off key by a little" means in numbers |
| B03 SNAP | nearest note in the key; retune speed: 60 ms keeps the vibrato, zero snaps; "Believe", 1998 | explain where the robotic sound comes from |
| B04 SHIFTING | speeding up shortens the sound; repeating cycles keeps the length; both move the vowel; PSOLA keeps it | say why a big pitch shift can sound like a chipmunk, and how that is avoided |
| B05 LISTEN | the three takes, back to back | hear the difference between natural and snapped correction |
| B06 THEN AND NOW | 1997; maths first used on seismic data; now a standard studio tool | place Auto-Tune in time, and know it is usually too subtle to notice |
| B07 WHAT TO DO | set the key, start slow, zero for the effect | try pitch correction on their own recording |
| B08 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **One piano roll for the middle of the video.** B02, B03 and B05 draw the same tune on the same lit lanes of C
  major, with as sung always coral, gentle always gold and retune speed zero always cyan, so each new idea lands on
  a picture the viewer already knows.
- **Measured, not drawn from the targets.** B03's gold and cyan tracks are the pitch measured again on the
  corrected audio, with the same detector, so the picture shows what the correction actually did, wobble included.
  The corrector only ever sees the detected pitch, never the true one.
- **Hear it, don't just see it.** B05 plays the run's own three takes, unedited, so the viewer can test the idea
  on their own ears.
- **The patent's way is shown honestly.** B04 includes my reconstruction of the patent's method next to plain
  resampling: it keeps the length but moves the vowel about as much (+27.8% against +25.8%). The film speaks of the patent, not of today's
  product, which later added formant correction; it never says current Auto-Tune makes chipmunks.
- **Antares' claims and mine are kept apart.** "My detector", "my singer", "in my test" and "my version" introduce
  every measured number; the synthetic singer's 93%, 29 cents and 91% are not presented as facts about real singers.
- **No names beyond the song.** The inventor and the producers are not named; B06 says what the maths was first
  used for, which is the part that teaches.
