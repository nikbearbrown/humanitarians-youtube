## 2026-10-12 – 2026-10-16 — The English + Hindi proof of concept

> Written 2026-10-17 from my project summary (PDF) and the video build; the song work happened
> during the week, the video on Saturday.

**Working on.** A small proof of concept to pitch Lyrical Literacy to Homes of Hope: one language
pairing (English + Hindi), both tiers, two songs per tier, so they can hear the idea.

**Tried, and expected.**
- Two themes ("Sisters Side by Side", "I Will Be"), lyrics, styles prompts and meta tags drafted with
  Claude from the creative brief, the example songs and the Homes of Hope articles; versions generated
  in Suno. I wanted catchy, singable songs that sound like Bollywood-influenced pop, not generic AI music.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**
- The first versions leaned on violin and weren't energetic enough. I used a Suno style I liked as a
  reference and reworked "I Will Be" around dhol, tabla and sitar, without copying its soft vocal tone,
  because call and response needs a strong lead.
- The Substack links only loaded partly, so I added the articles as PDFs.
- The Hindi still needs a native speaker: I asked a teammate to review pronunciation, and the meaning
  and tone need a fluent reviewer too.
- For the video: a 90-second limit, visuals only (my choice, no song clips).

**What Claude contributed, and what I accepted, changed or rejected.**
- Claude drafted the songs with me, then built this video from my summary. I chose the sound direction,
  picked my three favourite versions, and set the safeguards (no names, places, backstories or likeness).
- In the video, Claude respelled the instruments so the AI narrator says them right (BUILD-LOG §2).

**What I understand now, and what I still don't.**
- Now (from my summary): every song is built from one recipe (themes, call and response, meta tags,
  a styles prompt), so any song can later be made in all eight pairings at both levels.
- Still don't: which versions the team will pick, and whether the profession words read better in
  Hindi or as loanwords. Both wait on the reviews.
