# PROMPTS — "What MP3 Throws Away"

Design intent behind each visual. Nothing here was generated by an image or
video model — every beat is a deterministic Remotion render, so these are
component briefs, not generation prompts.

## The library-first gate

`./art scenes` was run for each visual need before authoring. Two searches and
their verdicts:

| Need searched | Closest candidate | Why it was not a match |
|---|---|---|
| "spectrogram frequency content bands over time" | `CadencesFig10Dream` | Rising theme bands from an economics deck — a skyline, not a time-frequency plot |
| "masking one loud tone hides a quiet one threshold" | `FluencyThreshold` | A gate/threshold diagram from an unrelated reel; no notion of one object suppressing another |

Both genuine punts. Also checked: the week-00 reel "What a Spectrogram Shows"
used **reel-local Manim scenes**, not shared Remotion components, so there was
nothing in the library to inherit. Building in Remotion keeps the portrait path
clean — `shorts.py` re-lays-out Manim beats, whereas a `916` sibling gives exact
control.

## B00 — `ClaudeComposerAsk` (chassis, reused)

The channel's fixed opener. Output lines pre-load the three moves the reel makes:

## B01 — `LossySpectrumCut` (new)

**Brief:** show the deletion before anyone explains it. A viewer pausing at any
point in the second half should see a hole.

A white-noise spectrogram: frequency on the vertical axis, time across, every
cell lit because white noise contains everything. At 42% of the beat the
lowpass lands and every cell above 16 kHz drops to bare page — *erased*, not
dimmed, because the beat's claim is deletion. A terracotta rule marks the cliff
and the measured drop appears inside the emptied region, so the number occupies
the space the data used to.

The caption under the plot flips from "source — white noise, every frequency" to
"128 kbps MP3 — decoded" at the same moment, which is what makes it a before/after
rather than an animation.

Cell energy comes from a deterministic hash of position, so there is no
frame-to-frame shimmer and the render is reproducible.

## B02 — `LossyMaskingCurve` (new)

**Brief:** make an ear fact visible. The viewer should watch something become
inaudible.

Two bars on a frequency axis: a tall loud tone, a short quiet one nearby. The
masking skirt sweeps up out of the loud tone — asymmetric, spreading further
upward in frequency, which is the real shape. As it rises past the quiet tone,
that tone greys to `GHOST` and takes a `MASKED — 0 BITS SPENT` stamp.

**Deliberately unnumbered.** No axis values anywhere. This build measured
lowpass behaviour, not masking thresholds, and a labelled axis would imply a
measurement that was not taken.

## B03 — `LossyBitrateCompare` (new)

**Brief:** turn the number in an export dialog into a picture.

Three rows, one per encode, strips aligned so the cliffs line up vertically and
the comparison is one glance. Each strip is truncated at its own **measured**
cutoff; the discarded remainder stays visible as a greyed block, so the viewer
sees what was given up rather than just a shorter bar. Ratios and byte counts
are the real ones from the experiment.

The lossless row runs full width — the point of the beat is that bitrate is a
permission slip, and one of the three grants no permission at all.

Carries the encoder-dependence footnote, because without it the reel would be
teaching 16 kHz as a property of MP3 rather than of this encoder.

## B04 — `LossyGenerationLoss` (new)

**Brief:** the thesis's strongest proof. If this were compression, repeating it
would be free.

A source reference line across the top, then four rungs stepping down, one at a
time, each with its measured deficit counting up beside it. Nothing on screen is
ever permitted to rise — the beat's claim is that nothing recovers, so the
motion vocabulary contains no upward move at all. The total block lands last.

## The portrait siblings

**Brief for all four:** reflow the logic, never shrink the landscape layout.
Type sized off `height`; everything inside the active band (y 230–1440).

| Sibling | The rotation |
|---|---|
| `LossySpectrumCut916` | Plot gets **taller** — portrait gives height, the axis a spectrogram wants, so the erased shelf is a bigger share of frame than in landscape |
| `LossyMaskingCurve916` | Plot squarer and tones fatter; the verdict moves from a floating stamp to a **card below the plot** |
| `LossyBitrateCompare916` | Three columns collapse into a **card per encode** — header line, full-width strip, footer line |
| `LossyGenerationLoss916` | Steps descend **vertically**, indenting further as loss grows; total bar spans the width at the bottom |
