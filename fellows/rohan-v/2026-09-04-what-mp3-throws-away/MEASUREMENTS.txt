Measured on this machine, 2026-09-04, with ffmpeg + libmp3lame.
Method: 10s of white noise (anoisesrc, 44.1kHz stereo, flat spectrum) encoded
to MP3, decoded back to WAV, then mean_volume measured in 500Hz-wide bands
via ffmpeg bandpass + volumedetect. White noise is used because a flat source
spectrum makes whatever the encoder discarded directly visible.

14000Hz     -31.8     -32.2      -31.8

16000Hz     -33.1     -33.7      -33.1
17000Hz     -34.0     -47.2      -34.0

19000Hz     -36.4     -58.7      -36.4
20000Hz     -38.3     -63.5      -41.8

=> 128 kbps lowpass sits at ~16 kHz. 16k is intact (-0.6 dB); 17k is 13.2 dB
   down; 18k is 18.9 dB down.
=> 320 kbps is bit-identical to source through 19 kHz; 20k is 3.5 dB down.

FILE SIZES (10s stereo 44.1kHz)

128 kbps MP3     160,958 bytes   -> 10.96 : 1
320 kbps MP3     402,329 bytes   ->  4.38 : 1

GENERATION LOSS (repeated 128 kbps re-encode, mean_volume dB)
generation      12kHz     14kHz     15kHz
source (wav)    -30.8     -31.8     -32.4
1 encode        -31.1     -32.2     -32.9
4 encodes       -33.3     -34.3     -35.0
6 encodes       -34.7     -35.7     -36.4
11 encodes      -37.8     -38.8     -39.5

=> Degradation is monotonic and compounding. From 1 encode to 11, the 12 kHz
   band loses 6.7 dB (~7 dB). Nothing recovers.

CAVEAT: these are this machine's libmp3lame defaults at CBR. Other encoders,
VBR modes and versions choose different lowpass points. The shape of the
result (a hard cliff whose position tracks bitrate) is general; the exact
frequency is encoder-dependent.
