## The self-imposed constraint

**Measure first, script second.** Nothing about the brief demanded this, but a
reel that tells people to distrust an encoder should not itself be built on
half-remembered figures. The experiment was run before the beat sheet existed,
which had a real consequence: a drafted line claiming 320 kbps "keeps
everything" was contradicted by the data and rewritten to "survives intact all
the way to nineteen."

The cost is that the reel can only claim what one encoder did on one machine.
That limit is stated on screen in B03 rather than hidden, and the alternative —
asserting a general fact about MP3 with no evidence — would have been worse.

## Why the masking beat carries no numbers

B02 explains a mechanism this build did not measure. Attaching an axis to the
masking curve would imply a measurement that was never taken, so the curve is
drawn qualitatively and the beat's authority rests on it being a standard
result, clearly separated in FACTCHECK from the things that were measured here.

## What 2:19 bought

Seven beats: one deletion shown, one mechanism explained, one comparison, one
trap, one verdict, plus bookends. Not enough for a sixth body beat, which is why
the practical advice is compressed into a single artifact card rather than
given its own worked example.
