How Shazam Finds a Song — Humanitarians AI

How can an app hear five seconds of music in a noisy room and name the exact recording? It doesn't
listen the way you do. It keeps a handful of loud points, and the timing between them. This is how,
explained from nothing: no maths or audio background needed.

KEEP ONLY THE LOUDEST POINTS
The sound becomes a spectrogram, and only the points louder than everything around them are kept:
about twelve a second. The 2003 paper that described Shazam's method calls it a constellation map.

PAIR THE POINTS INTO CODES
Each point is paired with a few just after it. Two frequencies and the time between them make one
code, a hash.

LET THE TIMING VOTE
For every matching hash, take the time in the song minus the time in your clip. A real match agrees
on one moment; chance matches scatter.

I BUILT ONE AND TESTED IT
Six songs I generated from scratch, five seconds of one of them buried in cafe noise as loud as the
music: 33 votes for the right song at exactly the right moment, 2 for the next best. Across 180
random clips it never named the wrong song. But play the same song a semitone higher, a little
faster, or hum it, and it's a different recording.
