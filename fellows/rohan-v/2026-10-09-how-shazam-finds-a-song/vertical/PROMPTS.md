# PROMPTS — "How Shazam Finds a Song"

Design intent per visual. Nothing here was generated by an image or video model: every beat is a
deterministic Remotion render of recorded data, so these are **component briefs**, not generation
prompts. The listening clips are the run's own WAV files.

## The library-first gate

`./art scenes` was run before anything was authored:

| Need searched | Closest candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "spectrogram peaks" | `LossySpectrumCut` (a noise spectrogram with a lowpass) | miss: no peaks, no real data |
| "audio fingerprint hash" | `HaiSubmitAudioClock` (an audio timing rail) | miss |
| "bar chart histogram votes" | `BarChart` (Codex template library, teal accent) | near-miss: another channel's palette, no offsets |
| "line chart accuracy sweep" | `CwcSweepAccumulation` (workshop items) | miss |
| "typeset equation math reveal" | the house renderer `typeset_math.py` | **hit**, used for B03's rule and worked example |

`ClaudeComposerAsk`, `HaiApplyCard` (`phoneType` on the phone) and `HaiTitleOutro` are reused as-is.

## Shared kit — `scenes/shazam/`

- `shazamData.ts`, **generated** by `export_shazam_data.py` from `evidence/fingerprint.json`: peaks,
  surviving peaks, the worked hash, the clip-vs-song match pairs, every song's offset histogram,
  the noise sweep, the re-performance votes, the typeset equations.
- `public/shazam/spec-clean.png`, `spec-noisy.png`: the run's own STFT (3 s, 0–3 kHz) as ink on
  transparent, louder = darker.
- `shzKit.tsx`: `SpecImage`, `SpecAxes`, `useSpec` (columns/bins → pixels), `Stat`, `PhoneBaseline`,
  `histBars`; `Chip` and `listening` reused from last week's `pitchKit`.

## Per beat

**B01 `ShazamPeaks`**: the spectrogram, then it fades back and the 40 peaks pop in left to right;
stats 35,862 numbers → 40 points → 12.5 per second; last, the noisy phone version crossfades in and
the 16 lost peaks go hollow ("24 of 40 survive").

**B02 `ShazamHashPairs`**: the faint constellation, one anchor ringed, its dashed target zone, lines
to its five targets; the first pair lit and read out in a THREE NUMBERS card (786 Hz, 1,174 Hz,
0.05 s) and its packed hash; "57 hashes per second".

**B03 `ShazamOffsetVote`**: the 2003 paper's scatter: every hash the noisy clip shares with Song C at
(time in clip, time in song). The 33 on one diagonal turn red when the narration says they agree;
the typeset rule and a real pair (13.07 s − 1.07 s = 12.00 s); the offset histogram's spike.

**B04 `ShazamMatch`**: the clip card (5 s of Song C, cafe noise as loud as the music, phone band) and
six song cards, each with its offset histogram on one shared scale; Song C spikes (33 votes at
12.00 s), the rest stay flat; "33 vs 2". The card being heard is lit during the two clips.

**B05 `ShazamNoiseSweep`**: six columns of 30 squares, one per random query: filled = right song,
red outline = "no match"; counts under each; "0 wrong songs in 180 clips".

**B06 `ShazamVersions`**: four bars of Song C's votes (same recording 52; a semitone up 1; 6% faster
4; hummed 1) against the 8-vote line, MATCH / NO MATCH; the hum-search footnote.

**Portrait (`*916`)**: each component carries a native phone layout on the PHONE type scale, with
short titles and strings in `vertical/beat_sheet.json` (`vertical_strings.py`), content across
y 20–78% and a ghost baseline at 78% from the first frame.
