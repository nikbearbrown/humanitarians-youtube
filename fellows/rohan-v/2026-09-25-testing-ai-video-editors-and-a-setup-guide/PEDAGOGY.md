# PEDAGOGY — "Testing AI Video Editors, and a Setup Guide for New Fellows"

A progress video still has to teach something: the viewer should leave knowing
what an AI editing agent was actually good at in a real test, and what a new
fellow's first week of setup looks like.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer takes away |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | name the two threads | what this week was |
| B01 WHY | one cause, two symptoms | why these two, together |
| B02 THE FIELD | four tools, four ideas of what editing is | code vs web page vs timeline vs pro editor |
| B03 THE TEST | the agent's real output | what "agentic editing" produced on our footage |
| B04 WHERE IT RESISTED | four snags and what each took | the practical cost of the tool |
| B05 WHAT IT SHOWED | good at / not yet / not tested | a fair, scoped conclusion |
| B06 THE GUIDE | the four-account chain, in progress | the order a new fellow gets set up in |
| B07 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Decisions that carry it

- **Show the output, not a picture of it.** B03 plays real frames of the agent's
  render. The FELLOW'S-WORK carve-out allows it: it is Rohan's own footage.
- **The snags are the finding.** B04 is the most useful beat for anyone deciding
  whether to try the tool: an invisible style, a copied phonetic spelling, 61 GB
  of scratch, a 1080p ceiling. Each row pairs the problem with what it took.
- **Scope the verdict.** B05's pending strip keeps "one test" from sounding like
  "the answer".
- **Discord first** is the one ordering fact a new fellow needs, so B06 leads
  with it and draws the chain from it.
