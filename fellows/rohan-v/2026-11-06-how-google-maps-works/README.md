## This week's contribution

**Question.** How does a maps app find you, pick a route, see traffic, and know how busy a cafe or a bus will be?

**Prediction.** Going in, the working assumption was that each feature is a simple idea (ranging, shortest paths,
averaging phones, counting visits, asking riders) that works well once enough data is pooled.

**What I built/tried.** `maps.py`: GPS ranging from simulated satellites solved for position and clock; a 30 x 30
grid city with Dijkstra and A*; rush-hour traffic estimated from simulated phone speeds; arrival times from speed
limits, history and live data; a cafe's visits counted from a quarter of phones with privacy noise; bus crowding
predicted from a few rider answers. The body of the film is built in HyperFrames (`build_hf.py`), a first for the
series, in a night-mode look with Google-Maps-style cards.

**Observed result.** GPS: about 5 m in open sky, about 69 m between tall buildings. Routing: Dijkstra checked 826
of 900 corners, A* 547, same route. Traffic: history alone coloured 87% of roads right but missed a crash; 500
phones turned it red. Arrival times: 19% off from speed limits, 3% with history + live. Popular times matched the
real crowd (correlation 0.988). Bus crowding: right on 81% of trips from 3 in 100 riders answering.

**Next experiment.** Replace the grid city with a real open map (OpenStreetMap) and test routing and traffic on
real road shapes.
