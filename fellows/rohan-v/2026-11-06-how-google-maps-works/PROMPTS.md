# PROMPTS — "How Google Maps Works"

Design intent per visual. Nothing here was generated by an image or video model. B00, B08 and B09 are the
channel's Remotion cards; B01–B07 are HyperFrames compositions (HTML and GSAP on one paused timeline, written by
`build_hf.py`) rendered frame by frame from the run's data. The Google Maps UI on screen is drawn to resemble the
app's night mode: illustrations built from my run's numbers, not screenshots.

No HyperFrames registry blocks are used: every element is drawn by `build_hf.py`. The first version,
`build_hf_v1_cream.py`, used the channel's cream cards and is kept for comparison.

## Shared kit — `build_hf.py`

- **The look, "night city"**: a deep navy ground, the simulated city as a tilted 3D plane with glowing roads in a
  fog-masked window, a camera that drifts and pushes through each beat, light trails for phones and cars, glass
  panels for the numbers. Source Serif 4 for titles (embedded, OFL), Inter for the panels.
- **Colour carries meaning**: cyan = you (position), coral = the answer (route, trip, bus line), green / amber /
  red = traffic, pale blue = the search.
- **Data**: `evidence/scene.json` and `results.json` (900 corners, 1,670 segments, both searches, every
  segment's traffic level, the example trip, the cafe, the bus) and each beat's measured duration and word-clock
  cues from the beat sheet, so each reveal lands on its word.
- **Google Maps UI kit** (night mode, drawn): `gm_card`, `blue_dot`, `gm_pin`, `route_lines`; `gtag` puts
  Google's mark beside its name (LOGO LAW).
