# SOURCE-brief — "How Google Maps Works"

## What was asked for

One of two STEM videos for the week of 2–6 Nov, following the current Brutalist framework (see
[PROMPT.md](./PROMPT.md)), with its motion beats built in HyperFrames. It pairs with this week's Spotify video:
both are apps that learn from what millions of people do.

## Who it is for

Lyrical Literacy fellows and the wider Humanitarians AI audience: people who follow the blue dot every day and
have wondered how it knows where they are, why it picked that route, or how it knows a cafe is busy. Nothing
technical assumed.

## What it must leave them with

The phone finds itself by timing satellites, and needs four because its own clock is off. Roads become a graph
that a search can cross, and a smarter search checks fewer corners for the same answer. Traffic, arrival times,
busyness and crowding all come from people's phones and answers, pooled and anonymised. So: when the blue circle
is big, trust your eyes; report crashes and closures; answer the crowding question.

## Evidence the reel stands on

`maps.py` and its outputs in `evidence/`; Google's own blog and help pages for what Maps does; ESA's GNSS
reference; the 1959 and 1968 papers for the two searches. Details in [SOURCES.md](./SOURCES.md).
