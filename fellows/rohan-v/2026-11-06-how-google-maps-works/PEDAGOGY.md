# PEDAGOGY — "How Google Maps Works"

How the reel is built to teach someone who uses Google Maps every day but has never been told how it works.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: one city carried through every beat, each job
shown on it and measured, Google's own claims kept apart from my simulation's numbers, and an ending on three
things to do.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the question: finding you, the route, traffic, crowds | expect four jobs, each tested |
| B01 WHERE AM I | four satellites, the clock, the blue circle | explain why the blue circle grows between buildings |
| B02 THE MAP | photos, partners, edits, read by AI | say where the map itself comes from |
| B03 THE ROUTE | a graph; Dijkstra's 826 corners, A-star's 547 | explain how a route is searched, and why aiming helps |
| B04 TRAFFIC | history knows the usual, phones catch the crash | say why a jam shows up only when people drive it |
| B05 ARRIVAL TIME | speed limits 19% off, history plus live 3% | trust an arrival time, and know where it comes from |
| B06 HOW BUSY | opted in, a quarter counted, noise added | read a popular-times chart, and why some places have none |
| B07 HOW CROWDED | GTFS; a few riders' answers | see how a handful of answers predicts a crowd |
| B08 WHAT TO DO | trust your eyes, report, answer | help the map that helps them |
| B09 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **One city for every beat.** The same 900 corners carry the route, the traffic, the trip, the cafe and the bus,
  so each new idea lands on a picture the viewer already knows.
- **Google's claims and mine are kept apart.** "Google says" introduces the 2 billion and the 97%; "in my
  simulation" or "in my city" introduces every measured number; B03 says Google doesn't publish its recipe.
- **App-like visuals, honestly labelled.** The cards look like the app so the viewer recognises them, and the
  docs say they are drawn from my numbers, not screenshots.
