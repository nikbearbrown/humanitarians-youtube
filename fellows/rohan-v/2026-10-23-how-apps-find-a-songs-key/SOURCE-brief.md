# SOURCE-brief — "How Apps Find a Song's Key"

## What was asked for

One of two STEM videos for the week of 19–23 Oct, following the current Brutalist framework (see
[PROMPT.md](./PROMPT.md)). Rohan chose key detection from four options; it follows last week's tempo video.

## Who it is for

Lyrical Literacy fellows and the wider Humanitarians AI audience: people who have seen "A minor" or "8A" next
to a track, or who write a key into a song generator's prompt, and never been told where it comes from. No
music theory assumed beyond "notes".

## What it must leave them with

An app finds a key by counting which notes a song uses and matching that against each key's shape. Because a
minor key and its relative major share every note, the most natural mistake is the right notes with the wrong
home, and a song that changes key needs more than one label.

## Evidence the reel stands on

`key.py` and its outputs in `evidence/`; Krumhansl & Kessler 1982 for the profiles; the MIREX scoring for
partial credit; Mixed In Key's Camelot docs for the mixing chart. Details in [SOURCES.md](./SOURCES.md).
