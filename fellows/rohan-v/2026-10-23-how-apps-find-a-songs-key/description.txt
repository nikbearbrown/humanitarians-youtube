How Apps Find a Song's Key — Humanitarians AI

Where does the "A minor" or "C major" next to a track come from? The app has to work out which note the song
keeps coming home to. Explained from nothing: no music theory needed.

SEVEN NOTES AND A HOME
A key is seven notes with one of them as home. C major and A minor use the very same seven notes; only the
home differs. That's exactly where apps go wrong.

STEP ONE: COUNT THE NOTES
Every frequency is folded into one of twelve note names, whatever the octave: chroma. Over a whole song that
gives twelve bars, the song's note profile.

STEP TWO: MATCH A KEY'S SHAPE
Listeners' ratings from 1982 give every one of the 24 keys a shape. The app compares the song's bars with all
24 and keeps the closest match.

I BUILT ONE AND TESTED IT
On sixty Western songs I generated, it named the key right on fifty, including every pop, rock and blues
song. The ten misses were minor ballads read as the major key with the same notes. And on a song that changes
key halfway, one label was wrong for both halves; listening in windows got both.
