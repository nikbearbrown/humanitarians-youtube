How Noise-Cancelling Headphones Work — Humanitarians AI

How can headphones make an engine's hum nearly disappear, but still let you hear someone talking? The trick is
to add more sound, not less. Explained from nothing: no physics background needed.

ADD THE WAVE, UPSIDE DOWN
Sound is a wave of pressure. Add the same wave flipped upside down and the two cancel. That copy is anti-noise.

LISTEN, FLIP, CHECK, ADJUST
A microphone outside the ear cup hears the noise first; a chip turns it into anti-noise for the speaker. In my
simulation the noise reaches the ear in 83 microseconds and the electronics take 125, so the chip has to
predict a little of the future.

LATE MATTERS MORE AT HIGH PITCH
A tenth of a millisecond is a tiny slice of a 100 Hz hum, which still drops 24 dB. At 2,000 Hz the same delay
makes the anti-noise add to the noise.

WHAT IT CANCELS
In my simulated headphone: engine hum 25 dB quieter, low rumble 31, voices only 11, clatter none. The ear cups
and tips block the high sounds the old way.
