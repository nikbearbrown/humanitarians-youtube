# PEDAGOGY — "How Noise-Cancelling Headphones Work"

How the reel is built to teach someone who uses noise-cancelling but has never been told how it works.

## The register

*No assumed knowledge* and *the viewer has to learn something*: one picture (a wave and its upside-down copy)
before any mechanism, one rule (late matters more at high pitch) that explains every later result, and an
ending on three things to do.

## The shape

| Beat | Job | What the viewer can do after it |
|---|---|---|
| B00 ASK | the puzzle: hum disappears, talking doesn't | name the answer's shape: add sound, on time |
| B01 BACKGROUND | anti-noise, on the simulation's own waves | explain destructive interference |
| B02 MECHANISM | mic, chip, speaker, inside mic; the timing race | say why the chip has to predict |
| B03 MATH | the same delay is a bigger mistake at high pitch | predict which sounds cancel |
| B04 MEASURED | four kinds of noise, and the hum off/on | believe it: measured and audible |
| B05 THE CURVE | the cancellation curve; the cups do the highs | read an ANC frequency curve |
| B06 WHAT TO DO | steady noise, a good seal, lower volume | get the most from their headphones |
| B07 OUTRO | sign-off | — |

## Choices worth defending

- **A simulation, stated as one.** The numbers are my model's, so the narration says "my simulated headphone"
  and FACTCHECK lists the model's parameters; the principle and the rule are general.
- **B03 explains B04 and B05.** The phase rule is the reason behind both results, so it comes first.
- **One clip, one gain.** The off/on clip is one file levelled once, so the viewer hears the real difference.
