How Apps Find a Song's Tempo — Humanitarians AI

Where does the BPM number next to a track come from? Nobody tells the app where the beats are: it has
to hear them. And the hard part isn't finding a beat. It's choosing which one. Explained from nothing:
no maths or audio background needed.

STEP ONE: WHERE NEW SOUNDS START
The sound is split into forty frequency bands, and at every moment the app asks how much louder each
band just got. Added up, that's the onset curve: every drum hit is a spike.

STEP TWO: FIND THE REPEAT
Slide the curve against a copy of itself. Where the spikes line up, the match jumps: that's
autocorrelation. Sixty divided by the shift is the tempo, but a groove repeats at more than one shift.

STEP THREE: CHOOSE THE BEAT
I built a detector this way and tested it on forty Western grooves I generated. Picking the strongest
repeat, it was right on 13 of 40. With a preference for tempos near 120, like the free library
librosa, rock came out right every time, but every slow ballad came out double and every drum and bass
groove half: the octave error. 33 of 40 were right or exactly double or half, and librosa gave the same
verdict on 34.

REAL DRUMMERS DRIFT
On a groove that speeds up from 120 to 123 BPM, a grid at one fixed tempo is 140 ms off at worst; a
beat tracker keeps half its beats within 6 ms.
