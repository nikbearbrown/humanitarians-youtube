How AI Music Generators Turn Text Into Audio — Humanitarians AI

You type a few words, and a minute later there's a song. What happens in between? Suno hasn't published
how its own model works, but many published music generators share one idea: turn sound into tokens,
and predict them one at a time, the way a chatbot writes words. Explained from nothing: no maths or
machine learning needed.

AUDIO IS A LOT OF NUMBERS
A CD stores 44,100 numbers a second for each ear: almost sixteen million for a three-minute song.
Far too many to predict one by one.

A FRAME BECOMES A TOKEN
A codec cuts sound into frames about a hundredth of a second long and swaps each for the number of
the closest shape in a learned codebook of 1,024. Stack a few codebooks and you get close to the
original at nearly 400 times less data than a CD.

I BUILT ONE AND MEASURED IT
My own codec, trained on music I generated and tested on a piece it had never heard: with one
codebook the treble is down 17 dB; with twelve, under 6. You hear all three, and the original. Then a
tiny model that guesses the next token: right 16.5% of the time, against one in a thousand for a
blind guess.

THE WORDS TILT THE ODDS
In published systems such as Meta's MusicGen, a text encoder turns your prompt into numbers that
steer every prediction. The prompt steers; it doesn't write the notes.
