## 2026-10-07 — script, sources and both review cuts

**What I was working on.** A 60-second, 12-beat explainer: Claude only knows what
it learned before its cutoff, so for anything recent or anything that may have
changed, you bring a current source or use web search.

**What I tried, and what I expected.** I started from the lessons of video 1 and
used only patterns that had passed a final master with a working portrait version.
I expected fewer component surprises, and there were fewer.

**Where it resisted, and what I did next.**
- Model names and cutoff dates change with every release, so I removed them from
  the screen and the narration to keep the video from dating.
- Anthropic lists two dates per model. The first draft implied the training-data
  range always runs later than the reliable-knowledge date; on the current lineup
  they are the same month, so the card now uses the doc's own words, "the broader
  data range".
- Two beats are inference, not quotation: knowledge thinning between the two dates,
  and getting an old answer to a question about something that changed. Both are
  hedged ("thins", "may") and labelled `ILLUSTRATIVE`.
- The 4.6 s opening summary is shorter than the ≥ 9 s the doctrine asks for. An
  accepted trade for a 60-second video.
- Portrait gate fixes: a two-line title above the title-safe line, a label on the
  axis, a chip crossing a label mid-move, and two cards touching. All fixed in the
  scene source, none with lenient mode.

**What Claude contributed, and what I did with it.**
- Claude drafted the sources, narration and both cuts, and carried video 1's
  lessons into a table in `BUILD-LOG.md`.
- In script review I changed two lines: "Near that edge" became "Between those
  dates", so the beat says which dates it means, and the "stable facts" line now
  says only that the cutoff matters less for them, not that they are always right.

**What I understand now.** An explainer about time has to avoid dating itself, and
"may" belongs only where the source supports an inference, not a fact.
