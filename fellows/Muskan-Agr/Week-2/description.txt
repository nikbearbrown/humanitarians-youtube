Opinions Don't Ship. Numbers Do. — Humanitarians AI website restructure, Week 2 of 4

Week 1 diagnosed the humanitarians.ai homepage and changed the plan: no full
rebuild, targeted fixes first. Week 2 turns those findings into a specification
you could hand to a developer.

Presented by Muskan Agrawal.

What's in this one:
— A link-level audit of the primary navigation. Seven destinations, and neither
  About Us nor Contact Us among them.
— The footer, counted: six columns, thirty-three links, thirty-nine anchors,
  and a Projects column of eleven items — six of them bare proper nouns a
  first-time visitor cannot decode.
— Typography, measured: nineteen distinct text styles across eight font sizes
  in a single family, with bold doing the work at six of those eight sizes.
  Many sizes, almost no levels.
— The proposed four-step scale: 60 / 36 / 20 / 16, plus 14 for captions, with
  bold stopping at headline.
— Line length: the longest body paragraphs run 84 characters. Comfortable
  reading is 45 to 75.
— The accessibility finding of the week: the site's secondary text grey
  measures 4.12:1 against white. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1. Sixty-four elements use
  it, fifty-two of them at body size. Seven levels darker clears the standard.
— Accent discipline: four maroon buttons above the fold, identical fill,
  identical height. Four equal emphases is the same as none.
— The hero, measured: the video embed occupies 2.10× the area of the entire
  message column, which holds 26% of the frame.

Every figure in this video was measured against the live site — computed styles
and bounding rectangles from the running page, plus pixel analysis of the
screenshots on screen. Nothing was estimated by eye.

Week 1 was the audit. Week 2 was the spec.
