# PEDAGOGY — humanitarians-ai-week4-navigation-cleanup-handoff

## The one thing a viewer should leave with

*When you are blocked, ship the part that isn't blocked — and agree how the
work will be handed over before you start making it.*

Both halves of this week are unglamorous, and both are what separates a project
that moves from one that waits. The stakeholder meeting hadn't happened. A less
experienced version of this project stalls for a week. Instead: find the work
that does not depend on the missing answers, do that, and spend the remaining
time removing the friction that would have hit the next phase anyway.

## Structure

1. **The block** (B01–B02). State honestly what is still pending, then refuse
   to be stopped by it.
2. **The unblocked work** (B03–B08). The link cleanup, measured, with the
   status stated precisely at the end so the claim survives scrutiny.
3. **The turn** (B09–B11). Process before pixels, and the question that should
   always come first.
4. **The framework** (B12–B15), with the two parts that most often get skipped
   — the shared library and the interaction-state spec — given their own beats
   arguing why.
5. **The arc** (B16–B18). Four weeks, honestly summarised, including what the
   site still does not look like.

## Deliberate teaching choices

- **B08 is the most important beat in the video.** The brief said the links
  were removed; the live site says otherwise. Putting "not yet deployed" on
  screen costs nothing and buys the whole reel its credibility. A status video
  that overstates progress teaches the wrong lesson twice — once about the
  project, once about how to report.
- **The five names carry across four videos.** B05 says it plainly: flagged in
  Week 1, counted in Week 3, scoped in Week 4. That is the clearest argument
  the series makes for auditing before designing.
- **The framework is argued, not listed.** B13 and B15 exist because "shared
  component library" and "interaction states" mean nothing as bullet points.
  One says why a mirrored library compounds; the other says what happens when
  states go unspecified — they get invented at build time and argued about at
  review.
- **Nobody is named.** The brief spells the developer's name two ways, so the
  reel says "the developer". Getting a colleague's name wrong on a published
  video is a small error with an outsized cost.
- **No layouts drawn, still.** Four videos in, the site has never been shown
  altered. The restructure has not been designed yet, and the videos say so.

## What this video does not do

It does not claim the site is fixed. It claims four weeks of decisions are now
written down, measured, or agreed with someone — which is what actually
happened, and is a more useful thing to have than a mockup nobody signed off.
