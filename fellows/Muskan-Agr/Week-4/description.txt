Move the Part That Isn't Blocked. — Humanitarians AI website restructure, Week 4 of 4

The stakeholder meeting prepared in Week 3 hadn't happened, so the answers it
owes — on audience priority, messaging and success metrics — were still
pending. Rather than lose a week waiting, this week moved the work that didn't
depend on them.

Presented by Muskan Agrawal.

What's in this one:
— A navigation and footer link cleanup done directly with the developer, using
  the Week 2 link audit as a working document. One question asked of every
  link: is there a defined project behind this?
— The Projects column, where the answer was most often no. Eleven links, five
  of them pointing at names a first-time visitor cannot decode and with no
  built-out concept behind them: Dewey, Madison, Medhavy, Mycroft, Popper.
— The same five names Week 1 flagged and Week 3 counted. Flagged in Week 1,
  counted in Week 3, scoped for removal in Week 4.
— The arithmetic: the Projects column goes from eleven links to six, the
  footer's column links from thirty-three to twenty-eight, and of the six bare
  proper nouns counted in Week 3, one remains. No redesign required.
— A precise status note: this is decided and specified, not yet deployed.
  Measured on the live site, the old links are still there.
— And the second half of the week: agreeing how design and development actually
  work together before any visuals start. A shared component library mirroring
  the site's coded components; dev mode access with structured layer naming; a
  written spec for interaction states — hover, focus, active, mobile collapsed;
  and agreed breakpoints so navigation behaves predictably across mobile,
  tablet and desktop.

Four weeks: an audit, a specification, a set of questions, and a working
agreement. The site doesn't look different yet. What changed is that every
decision behind the rebuild is now written down, measured, or agreed with
someone.
