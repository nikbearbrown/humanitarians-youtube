# PEDAGOGY — humanitarians-ai-week3-stakeholder-strategy-hierarchy

## The one thing a viewer should leave with

*Knowing what you cannot decide is a design skill.*

Weeks 1 and 2 were about turning opinions into numbers. The obvious next move
would be to keep going — measure more, specify more, ship a redesign. Week 3
does the opposite, and that is the lesson: past a certain point, more
measurement produces false confidence rather than better decisions. A contrast
ratio has a right answer. "Who is this site for" does not, and no amount of
auditing will produce one.

The transferable skill is recognising the boundary, and having a method for
crossing it — which is what a discussion guide is.

## Structure

1. **Establish the boundary** (B01–B02). Name what the numbers already
   settled, then name what they can't touch. Short, so the viewer feels the
   turn.
2. **Prove the boundary is real** (B03–B09). Seven measured findings, every one
   of which resolves to a question rather than a specification. This is the
   heart: the viewer must believe these are genuinely undecidable by design
   before the questions feel necessary rather than procedural.
3. **The pivot** (B10). One card. "None of that is a design decision."
4. **The method** (B11–B14). Eight themes, nine questions.
5. **The work that continued anyway** (B15–B16). Hierarchy strategy and hero
   refinement — held deliberately short, because the point is that they are
   provisional until the meeting happens.

## Deliberate teaching choices

- **The questions are shown as questions, in full sentences.** Not summarised
  into chips. A viewer should be able to pause and steal them.
- **Every question is anchored to a measured finding.** Generic UX-interview
  questions teach nothing; a question that exists because you counted 24 named
  concepts teaches the method that produced it.
- **B08 quotes the organization against itself, carefully.** Both statements
  are verbatim, both are attributed to their position on the page, and the
  narration says "both cannot be the promise" rather than alleging anything.
  The video asks; it does not accuse. That distinction is itself the lesson in
  how to raise an uncomfortable finding with a client.
- **Nothing is redesigned on screen.** B15 draws an abstract content order, not
  a page. B16 says in words why the hero is not being redrawn yet. Showing a
  speculative layout in the same video that argues for asking first would
  contradict the argument.
- **The four-week arc appears in the close.** By Week 3 the viewer should be
  able to see the shape of the whole project.

## What this video does not do

It does not report stakeholder answers, because the meeting has not happened.
Presenting prepared questions as if they were findings would be the exact error
the video is arguing against.
