# STAKEHOLDER DISCUSSION GUIDE
## Humanitarians AI website restructure · prepared Week 3

**Who this is for:** stakeholders, mentors, donors and volunteers.
**Time:** 45–60 minutes. Don't attempt all of it — pick the sections that
match who is in the room.
**Why it exists:** Weeks 1 and 2 measured the site and produced a
specification. What measurement cannot decide is *who the site is for, what it
should say first, and what counts as success.* Those are organizational
decisions. This guide surfaces them.

---

## How to run it

- **Ask, then wait.** The most useful answer usually arrives after the pause
  that feels slightly too long. Resist filling it.
- **Don't lead with the finding.** Ask the question first, hear the honest
  answer, and only then show the evidence. Showing the evidence first turns
  the interview into a defence.
- **Ask for stories, not opinions.** "Tell me about the last person who
  emailed you confused" beats "Do you think the site is confusing?"
- **Capture verbatim.** Record the words people actually use for their own
  programs — that vocabulary is the raw material for the new navigation labels.
- **Watch for disagreement between participants.** Where two stakeholders
  answer the same question differently, that is the real finding, and it needs
  resolving before any structure is finalised.
- **Never ask a question whose answer you intend to override.** If a decision
  is already made, say so and ask for reaction instead.

## 1 · Audience priority

*Measured: the homepage addresses eight distinct audiences — fellows and
international graduates, donors, mentors, educators, partner organizations,
children and families, volunteers, and the general public — with no stated
order of priority.*

1. If only one of those audiences could leave the homepage satisfied, which
   one should it be?
2. Which audience matters most *this quarter*, as opposed to in principle?
3. Who actually shows up — where does your real traffic and real email come
   from?
4. Is there an audience currently on the page that could be served better by a
   dedicated page instead of a share of the homepage?
5. Who do you most want to reach and are currently not reaching?

## 2 · First impression and messaging

*Measured: the hero's message column holds 26% of the frame; the video embed
occupies 2.1× its area.*

6. If a first-time visitor reads exactly one sentence and then leaves, what
   should that sentence have told them?
7. In your own words, with no jargon: what does Humanitarians AI do?
8. What do people most often misunderstand about the organization?
9. When you explain this at a dinner party, where do people's eyes light up —
   and where do they glaze over?
10. Is "We teach what AI cannot do" the line you want carrying the site, or is
    it a line you inherited?

## 3 · Primary call to action

*Measured: three equally weighted asks sit side by side mid-page — Donate Now,
Volunteer, YouTube. Four maroon buttons appear above the fold.*

11. If a visitor does exactly one thing, what should it be?
12. Which of your current calls to action actually converts, and how do you
    know?
13. Is donation the primary goal of this site, or a secondary one you'd rather
    not foreground?
14. What is the second-best action for someone not ready for the first?

## 4 · Terminology and internal shorthand

*Measured: 24 named concepts in roughly 1,200 words — about one every 50
words. Tier 1, Tiers 3–6, Tier 7, Irreducibly Human, Botspeak, Addams,
AIMagineering, Conducting AI, The AI Sherpa, Dewey, Madison, Medhavy,
Musinique, Mycroft, Popper, and more. "Tier 4" is referenced in the copy but
never defined in the framework block.*

15. Which of these names would you keep if a stranger had to understand the
    page with no one to explain it?
16. Which are public-facing brands, and which are internal project names that
    happen to have leaked onto the site?
17. What does each project name actually deliver, in one plain sentence?
18. Is the Tier framework something visitors need to understand to act — or is
    it the intellectual backbone that belongs one level deeper?
19. Which terms would you be sorry to lose, and why?

## 5 · Navigation relevance

*Measured: the primary nav carries seven destinations. About Us and Contact Us
are not among them. The footer carries 33 links across six columns, and the
Projects column alone holds eleven. Thirteen "learn more" links leave the
homepage.*

20. Which navigation links exist because someone still needs them, rather than
    because they always have?
21. Should About and Contact be in the primary navigation? What replaced them?
22. Of the eleven Projects entries, which are live and maintained, and which
    are archives?
23. If the footer could only carry twelve links, which twelve?

## 6 · Tone

24. Where should this site sound like a research group, and where like a
    program a family could join?
25. Who wrote the current copy, and who has authority to change it?
26. Is there anything on the site you'd be uncomfortable reading aloud to a
    prospective fellow? To a major donor?
27. Which organization's tone do you envy, and what specifically about it?

## 7 · Trust signals

*Measured: the site states 501(c)(3) status, an EIN and a state registration.
It does not publish an annual report, a Form 990, a named board, or a
third-party charity rating. Two different statements about donation use appear
on the same page: mid-page, "100% of donations fund the programs, mentorship
and project support"; in the footer, "100% of all donations support our direct
operational costs, including program expenses, legal fees, and staff
salaries."*

28. **Which of those two donation statements is correct?** They promise
    different things, and a careful donor will notice.
29. What would a first-time donor need to see on this site to give with
    confidence?
30. Are there financials, a board list, or an annual report that could be
    published but currently aren't? If not, what's the blocker?
31. Whose endorsement would carry the most weight with the people you're
    trying to reach?
32. What objection do you hear most often from people who consider giving and
    then don't?

## 8 · Success metrics

*Measured: every number on the homepage is a framework label, a legal
identifier, a chapter count, a donation percentage, or a copyright year. None
is an outcome.*

33. What is the one number that would show this year was worth funding?
34. What outcomes do you already track internally that never make it onto the
    site?
35. Are there numbers you deliberately don't publish? Why?
36. Six months after this restructure ships, what would have to be true for you
    to call it a success?
37. What would tell us it had gone wrong?

---

## Closing questions — ask these of everyone

38. What have I not asked about that I should have?
39. Who else should I be talking to?
40. If you could change one thing about the site tomorrow, what would it be?

---

## After the session

- Write up within 24 hours, while tone and hesitation are still recallable.
- Separate **decisions** (someone with authority settled it) from **opinions**
  (someone had a view). Only decisions go into the structure.
- Send each participant the specific decisions attributed to them and ask them
  to confirm. That is the sign-off, and it is what makes the navigation and
  hero organizational choices rather than design preferences.
- Log unresolved conflicts explicitly. An unresolved conflict is a blocker for
  Week 4, not a detail.
