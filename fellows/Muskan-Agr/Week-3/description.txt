The Audit Can't Choose the Audience. — Humanitarians AI website restructure, Week 3 of 4

Week 1 diagnosed the site. Week 2 turned the diagnosis into a specification: a
type scale, a contrast target, a line length. Week 3 is about the decisions no
audit can make for you.

Presented by Muskan Agrawal.

What's in this one:
— Eight distinct audiences addressed on one homepage, with no stated order of
  priority.
— Twenty-four named concepts in about twelve hundred words — roughly one piece
  of internal shorthand every fifty words.
— Thirteen "learn more" links leaving the homepage. Thirteen ways to leave, no
  way to be led.
— Three calls to action side by side, identically weighted: donate, volunteer,
  watch.
— The trust ledger: 501(c)(3) status, an EIN and a state registration are
  published. An annual report, a Form 990, a named board and a third-party
  charity rating are not.
— Two different statements about how donations are used, both beginning
  "100%", both on the same page.
— Every number on the homepage is a framework label, a legal identifier, a
  chapter count or a copyright year. None of them is an outcome.
— And the output of the week: a stakeholder discussion guide across eight
  themes — audience priority, first impression, primary action, terminology,
  navigation, tone, trust signals and success metrics.

Every figure was measured against the live site. What changed this week is that
each measurement resolves to a question for the organization rather than a
specification for the designer.

Week 3 is the questions. Week 4 moves to navigation cleanup with the developer,
and to agreeing how design and development hand work over.
