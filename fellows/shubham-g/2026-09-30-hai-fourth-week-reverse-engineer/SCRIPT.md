**B02 · FRAMEWORK** (14.04 s) — Here's the idea. First, we build one video, start to finish: idea, script, voice, visuals, render. Then we walk it backwards. At every step we ask what we decided, and how to save it, so nobody has to decide it again.

**B04 · TEMPLATE** (16.38 s) — Next, the finished video becomes a template. The structure stays fixed: cold open, summary, body, recap, your turn, outro. Only the slots change: the topic, the examples, the prompt. A new recruit fills in slots, instead of inventing a structure.

**B05 · ASK** (3.93 s) — Then I ask Claude to write the playbook from what we actually did this week.

**B06 · PLAYBOOK** (14.25 s) — The result is a six-step playbook: brief, script, voice, visuals, check, export. Each step has a short checklist, so nothing depends on memory. And the voice comes first, because its length sets the timing for everything else.

**B07 · FIXES** (14.91 s) — The most useful part is the mistakes. Every fix we made becomes a rule. Labels too small? Set a minimum size. A chip covering the logo? Keep that corner clear. A fix you make once is luck. A fix written down is process.

**B08 · GAINS** (14.78 s) — So what do we get? Speed, because nobody starts from a blank page. Efficiency, because the checks catch problems before a long render. And consistency, because every video follows the same spine, so it looks like part of the series.

**B09 · LIMITS** (13.16 s) — One warning: a playbook is a starting point, not a cage. Keep the spine, the checks and the brand fixed. Keep changing the examples, the visuals and the hooks. And update the playbook whenever something new works.
