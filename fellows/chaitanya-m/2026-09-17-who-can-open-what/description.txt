Who Can Open What — Medhavi Hub

Every time a student clicks a textbook, something has to decide whether they're
allowed to open it. That sounds simple, and it isn't — because there are several
different ways a person can end up with permission. This is the whole rule in
plain language: every path to access, and the one place the decision actually
gets made.

WHAT YOU WILL KNOW AFTER WATCHING
The three kinds of people in the system and what each gets automatically. Why
an instructor never has to be granted anything, and what "hidden" is for. The
exactly two ways a student ends up with a book — a direct grant, or a class they
joined — and how those two lists combine. What happens when a class is archived
at the end of a semester. And the design idea underneath it: the same question
gets asked at two completely different moments, and because it's answered in one
place, the two answers cannot disagree.

A NOTE ON ACCURACY
Every claim in this video was checked against the medhavi-hub source after
filming — fifteen claims, all fifteen verified, including the sharp edge: when a
class is archived, the books that came with it stop opening and nothing tells
the student why. The full verification trail is published with the source
package.

One nuance that didn't fit on camera: the two callers reach the same answer for
hidden books by slightly different reasoning, so the guarantee holds in
behaviour today but isn't enforced by a single shared branch.
