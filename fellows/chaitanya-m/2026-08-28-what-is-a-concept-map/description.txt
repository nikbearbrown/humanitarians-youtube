What Is a Concept Map — Medhavy Research Log, Aug 28 2026

A table of contents tells you the order the author wrote things. It tells you
nothing about the order a student can actually learn them. This week I went
through the Concept Map subsystem — the part of the hub that tries to fix that.

A concept map is a knowledge graph of a textbook. Every teachable idea becomes a
node. Every edge records what has to already be in your head for the next thing
to mean anything. The nodes are machine-generated, and machine extraction is
roughly right and locally wrong — so the whole editor exists as one thing: a
human gate between a machine guess and a trusted artifact.

WHAT YOU WILL KNOW AFTER WATCHING
What a concept map actually is, and how a dependency graph differs from a
sequence. What one node record contains, and why the edge matters more than the
name. Why the review gate is strict — export is hard-blocked while a single node
is still pending, with no ship-it-mostly-reviewed path. Which three fields get
stripped on the way out, and why scaffolding is not content. And the finding I
wanted on the record: the verified output currently has zero consumers.
