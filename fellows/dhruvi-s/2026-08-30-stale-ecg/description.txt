A Stale ECG Is Worse Than No ECG

Adding a routine ECG to an in-hospital mortality model moves AUROC from 0.8574
to 0.8595 across 144,668 cardiac admissions in MIMIC-IV. That +0.0021 looks like
nothing — and it hides a sign change.

Split those admissions by how old the ECG was at the moment of admission:

  ≤ 1 day       +0.0046   (95% CI  0.0028 to  0.0061)   real gain
  1–30 days     +0.0053   (95% CI -0.0002 to  0.0110)   underpowered
  1–12 months   +0.0003   (95% CI -0.0042 to  0.0045)   underpowered
  > 1 year      -0.0048   (95% CI -0.0079 to -0.0018)   real harm

A same-day ECG helps. An ECG more than a year old makes the model worse than
leaving the ECG out entirely. The decay is tested rather than eyeballed:
fresh-minus-stale contrast +0.0092 (95% CI 0.0057 to 0.0126, bootstrap
p < 0.001) and a slope of -0.0033 AUROC per decade of days. The contrast stays
positive in 5 of 5 fold seeds, though its magnitude varies across them
(+0.0061 to +0.0094) — the sign is what replicates, not the size.

Patients with a same-day ECG are being actively worked up, so that comparison is
confounded by case mix. To remove it, the analysis keeps only admissions where
the patient had both a same-day ECG and older ones on file (40,764 admissions,
16,157 patients, 1,262 deaths), then scores those same admissions repeatedly
while feeding the model progressively older ECGs from that same patient. Rows,
labels and EHR features stay fixed; only the ECG's age changes. The harm
survives: +0.0056 at a median age of 0.2 days, -0.0051 at a median of 508 days.

The mechanism is a train/deploy mismatch. The model learned to trust the ECG
because most training ECGs were fresh, then applied that trust to an input whose
reliability had changed. Train it on stale ECGs instead and it discounts them,
landing back at EHR-only performance — so the harm is not intrinsic to old ECGs.

The obvious fix did not clearly work. Supplying the ECG's age as a feature helps
by +0.0007 to +0.0013 AUROC with every interval crossing zero. Matching the
training age distribution to deployment is also inconclusive (+0.0032, 95% CI
-0.0018 to 0.0078). Both are reported as negative results. Demonstrating the
problem is the easy part.

LIMITATIONS
Report and measurements, not waveform — a waveform model would likely find more,
so these numbers bound ECG value from below. Absolute effects are small
(~0.005–0.010 AUROC); the sign change is the finding, not the magnitude. Single
centre, retrospective (BIDMC), so association rather than cause. The
within-patient design removes case mix but not unmeasured time-varying
confounding. Admissions with no pre-admission ECG (23.5%) are excluded
throughout, and that exclusion is not random.

DATA USE
MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-IV-ECG are credentialed PhysioNet resources under a Data Use
Agreement. Only aggregate results appear in this video. No patient-level data,
no row-level records, no identifiers.
