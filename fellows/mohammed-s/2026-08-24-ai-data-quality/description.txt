The Rule, Not The Report — How AI Manages Data Standards and Data Quality

Your data quality dashboard says 98.7%. It is not lying to you, and it is not
helping you either — because what it actually measures is whether a field is
present and whether it is a string. Six different spellings of one country all
pass that test.

This is the honest version of "AI for data quality." The bottleneck was never
the cleaning. It was the rule-writing: deciding what "valid" means for four
thousand columns, and keeping that decision true while the business changes
underneath it. That is the part that scales badly for people and well for a
model — and it is the part nobody demos.

The test to take away: can this rule fail a row, and does that failure reach a
person? If not, it is a wish, not a standard.

The prompt from the end of the video, to run on a table you own:

  Here is the schema and a 1,000-row sample of one table I own. For each
  column, propose one executable expectation with a severity and an owner,
  rank them by what a silent failure would cost, and list every column where
  you cannot infer a rule and must ask a human. Do not modify any values.

Watch the last list hardest. The columns it refuses to guess on are the ones
your organization never actually decided.
