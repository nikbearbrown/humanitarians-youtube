## B02 — what Godot is
*Landscape scene:* `GodotFreeChips` · *Portrait scene:* `GodotFreeChips916` · *Measured:* 17.54s

> So what is it, exactly? Godot is a free, open-source tool for making games. Free with no catch — there's no licence to buy, and no fee later if your game makes money. It handles flat 2D games, like a side-scroller or a puzzle, and it handles full 3D worlds you can walk around inside. One download, both kinds.

## B03 — how you build — nodes
*Landscape scene:* `GodotNodeTree` · *Portrait scene:* `GodotNodeTree916` · *Measured:* 18.86s

> Now, how you actually build something in it. A Godot game is made of small pieces called nodes, and you snap them together a bit like LEGO bricks. One node is your character. Another is the picture you see on screen. Another plays a sound. You arrange a handful of them into what's called a scene, and that scene becomes your player, or your level. There's no wall of code to get started.

## B05 — the heart — why this matters for AI
*Landscape scene:* `GodotTextVsBinary` · *Portrait scene:* `GodotTextVsBinary916` · *Measured:* 28.00s

> And here is the part that matters most, the reason we're talking about Godot at all. Godot saves everything you make as plain text — the kind you could open in any notepad. Your scenes, your settings, your scripts, all of it. So an AI can open your project, actually read it, change it safely, and then show you exactly what changed, line by line. Other big engines seal their work inside a format only that engine can open. An AI can't safely touch that. This is the reason Godot is the fit.
