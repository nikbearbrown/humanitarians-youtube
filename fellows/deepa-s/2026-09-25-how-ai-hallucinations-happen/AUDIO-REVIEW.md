### B01 — The Overview

A language model can give a convincing answer without checking every claim. Let’s see why plausible wording and verified facts are different. That is the gap.

### B02 — What Hallucination Means

An AI hallucination is a fluent answer that includes false or unsupported information.

### B03 — How It Happens

A language model predicts tokens from patterns learned during training. That can produce useful text, but ordinary generation does not automatically check each new claim against a trusted source.

### B04 — Fictional Example

Fictional example: imagine a made-up country called Bellora. Asked for its capital, an AI confidently answers, “Lumen.” Both names are invented; a confident sentence is not evidence.

### B05 — Why It Matters

That matters when people rely on invented citations, instructions, or details in consequential decisions.

### B06 — Reduce the Risk

Check important claims against reliable sources. Give useful context. Retrieval can bring relevant documents into an answer when appropriate. These steps reduce risk; they cannot guarantee truth.

### B07 — Verdict

Remember: plausible is not verified. Treat important claims as questions to check.
