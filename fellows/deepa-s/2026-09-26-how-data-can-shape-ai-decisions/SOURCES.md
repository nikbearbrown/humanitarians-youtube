# Sources

The narration’s general claims are supported by NIST’s primary technical publications. The one academic source is primary research describing evaluation under real distribution shifts. Claims are simplified for a beginner audience; the source notes below point to the relevant sections.

1. **NIST, *Towards a Standard for Identifying and Managing Bias in Artificial Intelligence*, Special Publication 1270 (2022).** https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1270.pdf — Sections 2.1 and 3 discuss contexts and challenges for AI bias; §3.1 covers dataset availability, representativeness, sampling, and historical/systemic patterns (printed pp. 11–16); §3.2 discusses construct validity and measurement (printed pp. 16–19); §3.3–3.4 cover testing, evaluation, and lifecycle considerations. The report explicitly cautions that mitigation approaches are not a panacea (§3, printed p. 14).
2. **NIST, *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*, NIST AI 100-1 (2023).** https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1 — Core §5.3 “Measure” says systems should be tested before deployment and regularly while in operation; Measure 2.3 says evaluate performance under conditions similar to deployment; Measure 2.4 calls for production monitoring; Measure 2.11 addresses evaluation and documentation of fairness and bias (printed pp. 27–30).
3. **NIST AI RMF Playbook, Measure.** https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/measure/ — Suggested actions include disaggregated evaluation where relevant, monitoring representativeness, and monitoring behavior after deployment (Measure 2.3 and 2.4). The Playbook is supplemental guidance, not a mandatory ordered checklist.
4. **Malinin et al., “Shifts 2.0: Extending The Dataset of Real Distributional Shifts” (2022).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15407 — Primary research describing mismatches between training and deployment/test distributions and benchmarks designed to study generalization under shift. Used only to support the simplified point that evaluation on training-like data alone may not show performance in different deployment conditions; no paper-specific statistic or result is narrated.

The fictional workplace-safety system, conditions, and possible differences are invented solely as an educational illustration. They are not a source-backed case study or claim about a real system.
