## B01 — How models learn

Machine-learning systems learn patterns from examples to produce outputs for a task; they do not independently decide what the world should look like. People set tasks, labels, and measurements.

## B02 — Data composition

Training-data quality and composition matter. Incomplete or unrepresentative examples may leave relevant conditions absent. That is a possible issue to evaluate, not an automatic outcome.

## B03 — Sampling and history

Sampling concerns which examples are collected and how. Data can also reflect historical or institutional patterns. NIST treats bias as involving data, measurement, and human or organizational context.

## B04 — Labels and measurement

Labels and measurements need review. A proxy label may not capture the intended concept; construct validity asks whether a measure represents what it is meant to.

## B05 — Training and deployment conditions

Training conditions may differ from deployment: lighting, camera angle, equipment, or other inputs can shift. Research on distribution shift studies how such mismatches may affect performance.

## B06 — Fictional safety example

Fictional example: a vision system learns to recognize required safety equipment in worker photos, such as hard hats and reflective vests. Suppose most training images show similar workplaces, lighting, camera angles, and equipment. It may learn patterns for those represented settings.

## B07 — When conditions change

Under glare, from a different angle, or with unfamiliar equipment, its performance may change. That outcome must be tested; this illustration is not evidence about a real company or system.

## B08 — Look beyond one number

One overall score describes a particular evaluation set, but may not show results for every relevant condition or group. Evaluate conditions similar to intended use, examine appropriate groups, and investigate meaningful differences; no single metric answers every question.

## B09 — Evaluation and mitigation

Review how data was collected and what it represents; check labels and measurements; then test under relevant conditions. If differences appear, investigate their causes and consider adjusting data, model design, or evaluation.

## B10 — Monitoring and limits

Continue monitoring after deployment, because conditions and inputs can change. Testing and mitigation may reduce identified problems, but cannot guarantee that every source of bias or error is eliminated.

Takeaway: outputs reflect more than algorithms; data, measurements, assumptions, evaluation, and conditions of use matter too. How Data Can Shape AI Decisions. This is Liam, narrating for Deepa, for Humanitarians AI.
