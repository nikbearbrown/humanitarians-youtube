# Fact-check notes — revised narration

| Claim in narration | Source and limits |
|---|---|
| Machine-learning systems use examples to fit patterns for a task; people define tasks, labels, and measurements. | NIST SP 1270, §3.1–3.2; this is beginner-level simplification, not a claim that all architectures work identically. |
| Incomplete/unrepresentative examples and sampling choices may matter. | NIST SP 1270 §3.1, printed pp. 14–16. Stated as possibilities to evaluate, not inevitable harms. |
| Data may reflect historical/institutional patterns; bias also involves measurement and human/organizational context. | NIST SP 1270 §§2.1, 3.1–3.2. |
| Construct validity checks whether a measure represents its intended concept. | NIST SP 1270 §3.2, printed pp. 16–19. |
| Training/deployment mismatch may affect generalization. | NIST AI RMF Measure 2.3 and 2.5; Malinin et al. (2022), *Shifts 2.0*. No quantified outcome is claimed. |
| A single overall score may not reveal every relevant condition/group difference. | Qualified inference from NIST’s deployment-like testing and disaggregated evaluation guidance. Aggregate metrics remain useful but may not answer every context-specific question; no universal group list or metric is asserted. |
| Review collection, representation, labels/measurements; evaluate relevant conditions/groups; investigate and consider changes. | NIST SP 1270 and NIST AI RMF/Playbook Measure guidance. These are prudent practices, not an ordered universal recipe or guarantee. |
| Monitor after deployment; mitigations do not guarantee every issue is eliminated. | NIST AI RMF Measure 2.4; NIST SP 1270 says bias mitigants are not a panacea. |
| Workplace-safety example. | Fictional/illustrative only. No company, system, incident, result, or statistic is represented. |

The script does not imply all AI systems share the same bias risks, that data alone explains every issue, or that mitigation eliminates bias.
