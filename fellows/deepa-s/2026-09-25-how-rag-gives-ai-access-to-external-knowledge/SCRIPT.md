## B01

A language model draws on patterns learned during training and on the context it receives. It may not know a specific, current, or private fact you need.

## B02

Imagine asking an assistant: What is our company's remote-work policy? That policy is private, and it may have changed.

## B03

Before answering, the system searches an approved company knowledge base and retrieves a relevant policy passage.

## B04

It adds that passage to the question as context. Now the model has specific information to work from.

## B05

In our fictional example, if the policy says remote work is allowed two days a week, the assistant can explain that rule.

## B06

That is retrieval-augmented generation: retrieve first, add context, then generate. External sources can include documents, databases, or other approved knowledge sources. The model is not retrained for each question.

## B07

This connects to my last video on AI hallucinations: relevant retrieved context can help reduce unsupported claims. But RAG does not guarantee correctness. Retrieval and source quality still matter.
