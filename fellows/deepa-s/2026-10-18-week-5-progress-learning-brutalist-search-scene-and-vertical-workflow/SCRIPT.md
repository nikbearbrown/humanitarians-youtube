## B00 — Find before building

This is my Week 5 progress update on learning the Brutalist film-as-code workflow. This is Liam, narrating for Deepa. The focus is how discovery, reusable assets, Beat Sheets, and separate video formats fit together. These are workflow lessons supported by the toolkit, not a claim that I personally ran every command.

## B01 — The skill catalog

Art dash dash list displays the available skills. It helps me choose an appropriate starting point, but listing a skill does not run its entire production process. Skill-specific help exposes its instructions. I am learning to read the workflow before assuming that one command will produce a finished video.

## B02 — Search the icon library

Art icons searches the existing icon library. Art icon build prepares the canonical icon set from its source assets. Those are different jobs: finding a useful mark does not mean rebuilding the library. I want the icon to explain something in the scene, rather than act as decoration beside unrelated narration.

## B03 — Find renderable scenes

Art scenes searches existing compositions, while its check option helps inspect a particular scene. Art scene index rebuilds the catalog from registered compositions. A source file alone is not proof that a scene can render. I need to check its registration, properties, and suitability for the content and format.

## B04 — The Beat Sheet is the plan

Art todo derives a production ledger from the Beat Sheet. That connects the intended narration and visuals to the remaining work. If the plan changes, the source should change first, and the derived receipts should be regenerated. A filled slot is still not the same as a reviewed, accurate scene.

## B05 — Vertical is not a crop

Art shorts prepares a shorter plan, while art vertical prepares a full-length portrait companion. Those outputs serve different editorial needs. Landscape can compare ideas side by side. Portrait needs deliberate stacking and reflow. I should preserve the meaning and the complete narration for a full-length companion, not merely crop the wide version.

## B06 — Apply the lesson carefully

The Data Leakage topic gives me a practical way to apply these ideas. A split diagram should show where information belongs, and a comparison should make the contamination visible. Rendering produces an artifact to inspect; it does not establish that every label fits, every claim is correct, or the video is approved.

## B07 — A repeatable process

My takeaway is to search first, keep the Beat Sheet current, and inspect the result in both formats. I will document corrections and preserve earlier decisions rather than erase them.
