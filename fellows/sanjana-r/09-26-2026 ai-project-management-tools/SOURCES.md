## What is claimed on screen, and where it comes from

This is a **concept explainer**, not a data/report film. It teaches an original,
reusable decision rubric — **THE TICKET TEST** — and every on-screen verdict is
derived from that rubric applied to a worked example. There are **no external
statistics, benchmarks, or quotes** on screen, so there is nothing to mis-cite.

- **THE TICKET TEST (framework, T03)** — original pedagogy authored for this reel:
  three questions (MECHANICAL? / REVERSIBLE? / IN THE TOOL?) → route to
  *AI drafts & surfaces* vs *a human decides & commits*. Not lifted from a source.
- **Tool names (T00, T02, S00):** Jira, Trello, Asana, Notion, ClickUp are real
  products that ship AI assistants (e.g. Atlassian Intelligence, Notion AI). These
  are stated only in general terms — "AI is now inside these tools." No
  version numbers, no feature counts, no dated claims (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW: nothing
  that will drift or date the video).
- **Worked example (T05):** the stand-up note ("login flaky on Safari…") and the
  four tickets it becomes are an **illustrative, self-authored example**, not a
  real user's board. It demonstrates the *mechanical/reversible/in-the-tool* case.
- **Falsifiability case (T06):** "pick the sprint and commit the dates" scored
  three reds, plus the stale-board flip of "summarize progress" green→red. Both
  are reasoned from the rubric's own axes — the method's honest boundary shown on
  screen, not an outside claim.
