# PEDAGOGY — Seven Million Alerts a Night

A teaching audit of the 16-beat cut, written against the finished narration and rendered frames.
This is a critique, not a sign-off sheet: where the film makes a trade, the trade is named and the
cost is stated.

---

## 0. The presenter introduction (B00)

The film opens: *"Hi, I am Dhrumil Shah, and this video is about how artificial intelligence helps
astronomers sort millions of nightly sky alerts, to find the exploding stars that are actually worth
a telescope's time."*

**What it adds.** An introduction that states the film's question before the film asks it is an
*advance organiser* — viewers who know where an explanation is going retain more of it. B00 goes a
step further than naming the topic: its frame lists the six pipeline stages (SCAN → FOLLOW-UP) with
a plain-language gloss each, so the rail that appears from B03 onward is already familiar rather
than new chrome. It also establishes a human presenter, which on a vertical feed builds trust in a
film that will later tell the viewer to distrust a headline accuracy figure.

**What it costs.** The brief's original hook — the counter racing to seven million — now lands at
0:11.8 instead of 0:00. On Shorts, Reels and TikTok the first seconds decide whether the viewer
stays, and a greeting is weaker than a number. That is a real trade, and it was made deliberately.

**How the cost is contained.**

- The greeting is one clause; by 0:05 the narration is already on "millions of nightly sky alerts,"
  so the stake arrives inside the first swipe window.
- The summary names the payoff ("exploding stars worth a telescope's time"), which is itself a
  curiosity gap — *which* ones are worth it, and who decides?
- The frame is animated evidence of structure, not a static name card: the pipeline preview draws in
  on "sort millions", timed to the words.
- The intro says "millions", not "seven million", so B01's reveal still has something to reveal, and
  the headline figure is still qualified at the moment it is first stated.

**Does the intro say anything the film then has to walk back?** No. Its verb is *sort*, not
*discover* or *identify*, and it names telescopes as the thing whose time is being allocated — which
is exactly the thesis B14 lands. The summary is consistent with the conclusion rather than a
softer, hype-shaped version of it.

---

## 1. Is the main question clear?

**Yes — now stated three times, in three registers.**

B00 states it as a summary. B01 states the scale (about seven million changes a night at full
operations). B02 converts that into a human impossibility (eighty-one days of unbroken reading). A
viewer stopped at 0:30 can say "there are too many alerts for people to look at, and something has
to sort them," which is the only prerequisite for the rest of the film.

---

## 2. Can a beginner follow the process?

**Yes, provided they watch linearly.**

The load-bearing device is the **six-stage rail**, previewed in B00 and present from B03 to B14.
Past stages stay lit in cyan, the active one is amber and enlarged. At any frame after 0:30 the
viewer can answer "where am I?" without having tracked the argument.

Concepts are introduced in dependency order, and nothing is used before it is defined:

| Term | Defined at | Definition given |
|---|---|---|
| the six stages | B00 | one plain-language gloss each |
| template / reference | B04 | "a deep template, stacked from everything seen at those coordinates before" |
| difference image | B05 | shown as subtraction, not named jargon-first |
| detection (5σ) | B05 | "anything above five sigma counts" — a threshold, not recognition |
| alert | B07 | enumerated as five concrete fields |
| broker | B08 | "seven independent community brokers" + the falling-stream picture |
| light curve | B09/B10 | drawn before the phrase carries weight |
| spectrum | B13 | "splits the light into wavelengths and shows what is actually burning" |

**Weakest link:** B07→B08 is the fastest conceptual jump — one packet becomes a public stream across
a single beat boundary. B00's preview ("Package every change as an alert") gives the viewer the
word *alert* in advance, which helps, but the scale jump itself is still quick.

---

## 3. Does each beat introduce only what it needs?

**Mostly. Two beats are denser than the rest, and are known to be.**

- **B07** carries five packet fields, a 60-second requirement, a ≥98% scope note and two cutouts. It
  is the longest body beat (11.88 s) for that reason; rows stagger so the eye is led.
- **B09** carries two models, two architectures, two trigger conditions and a taxonomy count
  (11.10 s). The hard two-block split keeps it legible.
- **B00** is dense on screen (greeting, summary, six rows) but asks the viewer to *retain* nothing —
  every item is re-taught in its own beat. It is a map, not a lesson.

Everything else is one idea per beat.

---

## 4. Does the film distinguish detection, classification, and confirmation?

**This is the film's central commitment, enforced structurally.**

| | Detection | Classification | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beats | B05 (5σ threshold) | B09–B12 | B13–B14 |
| Who does it | the observatory pipeline | independent brokers | *other telescopes entirely* |
| What it produces | a record above a threshold | a probability distribution | a spectrum |
| Named on screen as | "detection" | "not a verdict" | "what it actually is" |

Three devices keep them apart: **different actors** (brokers are independent; follow-up is "a second
facility"), **different colours** (cyan detection, amber candidate, green confirmation — green does
not appear until B13), and **B06**, which breaks the naive chain ("a dot is not a discovery") before
machine learning is even mentioned. B00's preview separates CLASSIFY ("estimate what each change
might be") from FOLLOW-UP ("point a telescope to confirm") in its very first frame.

---

## 5. Does it explain what AI actually contributes?

**Yes, and it refuses the usual overstatement in four places.**

- **B00**: the contribution is framed as *sorting* from the first sentence.
- **B11**: "the answer is not a verdict; it is a set of probabilities" — and the accuracy figure is
  "a statement about the model, not about the star."
- **B12**: the headline is "Sorting is the contribution."
- **B14**: "A classification is a ranking. It is not a confirmation."

**Risk accepted:** a viewer could leave thinking ML is less useful than it is. B13 counters this by
establishing that the confirming resource is scarce *before* the limit is stated, so ranking reads as
decisive rather than menial.

---

## 6. Is uncertainty represented correctly?

**Yes, at four levels.**

1. The output is a distribution, not a label (B11).
2. Performance figures carry their conditions — "~90%" never appears without "balanced test set",
   in the spoken line as well as on screen.
3. Invented numbers are flagged *before* they appear.
4. The headline figure is conditioned ("at full survey operations"), and the intro avoids stating it
   unconditioned.

**Residual weakness:** the film shows both precision-style and recall figures in B11 but does not
define them. The labels ("Accuracy, balanced test set" / "Recall, TNS-confirmed SNe") and the
following sentence tell the viewer how to *use* both numbers correctly, so the result is
incompleteness, not a false belief.

---

## 7. Does each scene visually reinforce the narration?

**Yes. Motion carries the explanation rather than decorating it.**

| Beat | The claim | The motion that *is* the claim |
|---|---|---|
| B00 | here is the route | six stages draw down a spine as "sort millions" is spoken |
| B01 | the volume is overwhelming | a counter that keeps climbing past comprehension |
| B05 | subtraction leaves only change | two plates resolve into one; a reticle contracts onto what is left |
| B06 | three causes look identical | three stamps sit side by side and genuinely are |
| B08 | one packet becomes a stream | continuous fall into seven fixed lanes |
| B10 | shape is the evidence | curves draw over time |
| B11 | not a verdict | four bars, none reaching 100 |
| B12 | ranking is the contribution | one row rises past nine others |
| B14 | the gap is enormous | 30 dots of 1,000 light |

**Vertical composition:** every scene is authored for 9:16. B00's pipeline list and B04's stacked
before/after use the tall axis as a reading order; B05's arithmetic runs downward; B08's stream
falls; B12's queue lifts.

## 8. Misconceptions the simplification could produce

| Possible misconception | Risk | Mitigation | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Rubin reports seven million alerts tonight" | High | "at full survey operations" spoken and printed; intro says only "millions" | Low |
| "AI discovers supernovae" | High | the verb never appears; intro uses *sort*; B12/B14 name the contribution as ordering | Low |
| "Every transient is a supernova" | High | B06 is a whole beat refusing this | Very low |
| "The classifier is ~90% right about this star" | Medium | "a statement about the model, not about the star" | Low |
| "All brokers work like ALeRCE" | Medium | ALeRCE named in narration and on screen | Medium — accepted |
| "One integrated system does all of this" | Medium | brokers *independent*; follow-up at *a second facility*; B00 shows six separate stages | Low |
| "Precision and recall are both accuracy" | Medium | each number labelled with what it measures | **Medium — unresolved** |
| "The images are real observations" | Medium | every image frame carries a SYNTHETIC caption | Very low |
| "5σ means 99.99994% certain it's real" | Low–medium | framed only as a detection threshold | Medium — sigma not explained |
| "The presenter is claiming a finding" | Low | B00's source line: "summarises the film; makes no factual claim" | Very low |

---

## 9. Audience fit

- **No prior astronomy required.** Every term is glossed on first use, and B00 glosses all six
  stages before any of them is taught.
- **No prior ML required.** "Convolutional neural network" and "balanced random forest" appear as
  labels on diagrams; what they *do* is what is shown.
- **An expert is not insulted.** 5σ in positive or negative flux, the 12-month packet history, the
  ≥6-detection gate and the balanced-test-set condition are precise enough to be recognised as
  correct.

---

## 10. Verdict

**PASS** for factual integrity, uncertainty handling, and the detection / classification /
confirmation distinction.

**Known, accepted limitations**, none of which produces a false belief:

1. The scientific hook now arrives at 0:11.8 rather than 0:00 (§0) — a deliberate trade for a
   presenter introduction and an advance organiser.
2. Precision vs recall is shown but not defined (§6).
3. "Five sigma" is used but not explained (§8).
4. The B07→B08 scale jump is the fastest transition in the film (§2).
5. ALeRCE's architecture may be over-generalised by a fast viewer (§8).

**Recommended follow-up episode:** *"What 90% Accurate Actually Means"* — precision, recall, and why a
classifier tuned for one is poor at the other. It would close limitations 2 and 5.
