# PEDAGOGY — Seven Million Alerts a Night (16:9)

A teaching audit of the landscape cut. The narration, claims, on-screen wording and timing are
identical to the 9:16 cut, so the content audit there
(`../real-time-supernova-classification-9x16/PEDAGOGY.md`) applies in full and is summarised here.
This document adds what changes when the same explanation is laid out on a wide frame.

---

## 1. The presenter introduction on a landscape frame

The film opens: *"Hi, I am Dhrumil Shah, and this video is about how artificial intelligence helps
astronomers sort millions of nightly sky alerts, to find the exploding stars that are actually worth
a telescope's time."*

**It costs less here than in the vertical cut.** On a swipe feed the first seconds decide whether the
viewer stays, so a greeting ahead of the counter hook is a real trade. A landscape viewer on YouTube
has usually chosen the video from its title and thumbnail, and a named presenter with a one-sentence
promise is the expected opening, not a delay.

**It does more here.** The wide frame shows the greeting, the one-line summary and the full six-stage
route *at the same time* — who, what and how in one image — where the vertical cut has to reveal them
top to bottom. The advance organiser is complete on screen before the narration finishes its
sentence.

**It still says nothing the film has to walk back.** Its verb is *sort*; it says "millions", not
"seven million", so B01 still reveals and qualifies the headline figure; its source line states that
it makes no factual claim.

## 2. What the landscape layout does for comprehension

The cut uses one consistent grammar: **the left column says what the beat means; the stage shows the
mechanism.** A viewer who looks only at the left column gets the argument as a sequence of claims; a
viewer who looks only at the stage gets the process. That split is available only on a wide frame.

Where the layout changes the teaching, it helps:

| Beat | Landscape change | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| B04 | template **left of** new visit | before → after matches the reading direction |
| B05 | new − template → difference, left to right | reads as a written equation, which is what subtraction is |
| B11 | illustrative bars **beside** published figures | invented output and cited evidence are visibly separate objects, compared at a glance |
| B12 | queue **beside** its outcome stats | cause (the lift) and consequence (3,412 sank, #1 reached a human) seen together |
| B13 | dome **beside** spectrum | the second facility and the measurement it makes are one picture |
| B10 | plot fills the stage | the time axis is longer, so "rises in days, fades over weeks" has more room to be seen |

Where the vertical cut used the tall axis as meaning — the stream falling in B08, the queue lifting in
B12 — the landscape cut keeps those motions within the stage, which is still tall enough (1,300 px) to
carry them.

**Accepted cost:** B06's six stamps are smaller here (420 px vs 546 px), because the stage is height-
limited. The distinguishing features (trail, streak, dipole) remain clearly legible at 1080p, and the
three look-alikes still read as identical, which is the point of the beat.

## 3. Content audit (shared with the 9:16 cut)

- **Main question clear?** Yes — stated as a summary in B00, as scale in B01, as human impossibility
  in B02.
- **Can a beginner follow?** Yes. Terms are defined on first use; the six-stage rail runs across the
  top of every beat from B03 to B14 and is previewed in B00.
- **One idea per beat?** Mostly; B07 (packet) and B09 (two models) are knowingly denser. In landscape
  both benefit from the width: B07's table and requirement ring sit side by side, and B09's blocks
  each get a full-width row.
- **Detection vs classification vs confirmation?** Kept apart by actor (observatory / independent
  brokers / a second facility), by colour (cyan / amber / green, with green withheld until B13), and by
  B06 refusing "a dot is a discovery" before machine learning is introduced.
- **What AI contributes?** Named as sorting — in B00, B12 ("Sorting is the contribution") and B14
  ("A classification is a ranking. It is not a confirmation.").
- **Uncertainty?** A distribution, not a label; every published figure carries its condition;
  illustrative values flagged before they appear; the headline figure conditioned on full operations.
- **Visuals reinforce narration?** Every beat's motion is the claim — the counter, the subtraction,
  the identical stamps, the falling stream, the drawing curves, the rising row, the 3% of dots.

## 5. Verdict

**PASS** for factual integrity, uncertainty handling, and the detection / classification /
confirmation distinction — shared with the 9:16 cut, whose narration and wording this film speaks
unchanged.

**Known, accepted limitations:**

1. The scientific hook arrives at 0:11.8 because the film opens on the presenter introduction — a
   smaller cost on landscape YouTube than on a vertical feed.
2. Precision vs recall is shown but not defined.
3. "Five sigma" is used but not explained.
4. B07 → B08 is the fastest conceptual jump.
5. B06's stamps are smaller than in the vertical cut; features remain legible.

**Recommended follow-up episode:** *"What 90% Accurate Actually Means"* — precision, recall, and why a
classifier tuned for one is poor at the other.
