## What the current period produced

Six weeks, two tracks running in parallel: one Mycroft sprint and one AI STEM
explainer every week, twelve deliverables in total. Each shipped as a 4K 9:16
film with a reproducible package behind it — notebook, measured outputs, fact
check and build log — so every figure quoted below traces to an executed run
rather than an estimate.

- **Mycroft grew from a single predictive model into an auditable multi-agent
  monitoring system.** Phase 1 established the baseline on a 184,138-row,
  120-ticker OHLCV panel and reported the honest result: short-horizon price
  direction was not reliably predictable from the supplied data. Every later
  phase built on that finding rather than around it.
- **Phase 3** split the work across five specialised agents in three execution
  stages — market structure, anomaly detection, peer benchmarking, tail risk and
  prediction reliability — with 33 validation checks and a human review queue
  whose decision fields are created empty by design.
- **Phase 4** asked whether the weak result was uniform. It is not: discovered
  four market regimes and found test ROC-AUC of 0.5359 in high volatility and
  0.4909 — below chance — in `calm_bull`, the calmest and most common regime.
  Permutation importance put the regime label itself ahead of every price
  indicator.
- **Phase 5** added a 0–100 quantitative thesis-health score, contradiction
  detection, and a 3,000-path bootstrap stress test reporting VaR, CVaR and
  resilience bands.
- **Phase 6** integrated five agents behind a transparent review-priority score —
  plain integer weights, no learned model — with a written reason attached to
  every alert and an auditable JSON event log.
- **Six AI STEM explainers** covering predictive analytics, materials discovery,
  real-time astronomy, agent tool routing, causal inference and argument
  auditing. Each is a standalone 9:16 4K film with burned-in captions.

Each phase reports its own limits on screen rather than in a footnote. Phase 4
states twice that regime awareness did not make the model predictive; Phase 5 and
Phase 6 each close on a measured diagnostic explicitly labelled "not a forecast".

## Plan for the requested period

Nine weeks, same cadence: one Mycroft sprint and one AI STEM explainer per week.
Sprints 7–10 are ordered so each gates the next, and the first three close out
limits that the current period's own reports identified.

1. **Close the Phase 4 labelling gap** (1–3 Oct). Regime names are currently
   assigned from centroid statistics computed over the full period, including
   test dates. Move that to train-only statistics and re-check whether the
   regime labels — and therefore the Phase 4 finding — survive. If the labels
   change, report that.
2. **Calibration per regime and per segment** (6–10 Oct). Phases 4 and 5 report
   discrimination but never calibration. Measure reliability curves and Brier
   decomposition per regime. If a segment cannot be calibrated on the available
   sample, report that rather than smoothing it.
3. **Calibrate the Phase 6 priority score** (13–17 Oct). The weights are hand-set
   integers and the diagnostic is in-sample and non-monotonic. Fit and evaluate
   the score out-of-sample against a held-out period. "The score adds nothing
   over its strongest single component" is an acceptable outcome and will be
   published as one.
4. **Package and schedule** (20–24 Oct). Turn the monitoring pass into something
   that runs on a schedule and writes a dated report, with the reviewer fields
   still owned by a person. Document it for both a caller and a copier.
5. **Future work** (from 27 Oct). Propose and carry out the next phase, chosen
   from what sprints 1–4 actually show.

AI STEM topics for the period will be proposed one week ahead and logged in the
same folder structure. Reporting continues as it has: a dated Frictional log per
work folder, weekly hours, and a build log plus fact check in each project
repository.
