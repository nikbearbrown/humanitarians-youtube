# PROMPTS — Assisted, Not Automated.

Every prompt shown on screen, verbatim. GATE F paperwork set
(`FACTCHECK.md` · `SHOTLIST.md` · `PROMPTS.md`).

## B00 — cold open (on screen)

**Greeting:** `Kumusta, Yatra` · **runningText:** `separating replacement from reorganisation…`

> I run an agency. Everyone I compete with is using AI for SEO and content now. Show me what
> actually changed structurally — adoption, spend, what search rewards — and tell me honestly
> whether this is replacement or reorganisation.

**Result lines (the ask lands answered — COLD OPEN LAW):**

1. `agencies adopted faster than in-house teams`
2. `spend is still accelerating, not plateauing`
3. `assisted content is the norm; fully automated is rare`

The three result lines are the episode's three acts in order, so the cold open doubles as a
contents page.

## B11 — the ask micro-beat (on screen) · RESULT is B12

**Spark line:** `Some, or only?` · **runningText:** `separating 'some' from 'only'…`

> Separate two things for me, because people keep merging them: the share of web pages that
> contain SOME AI-generated content, and the share that are PURELY AI-written with no human
> editing. Then tell me which of those two actually correlates with ranking.

ASK→RESULT LAW: the actual prompt behind B12's 74.2% / 2.5% comparison. The conflation this
prompt refuses is the episode's central distinction — "some" versus "only" — so the prompt
states the thesis as a question before the data answers it.

## B22 — the handoff (on screen, READ ALOUD VERBATIM)

**Greeting:** `Your turn.` · **runningText:** `paste this into Claude…`

> Audit my last 10 pieces of content. For each one, separate what AI produced from what a
> person actually decided. Then tell me what a reader would notice if the human half
> disappeared.

**Rubric shown beneath (HANDOFF LAW — scaffolded, not "ask Claude about X"):**

1. `grade it: does it separate producing from deciding?`
2. `grade it: does it name something you'd be embarrassed to publish?`
3. `grade it: would the reader notice if the human half vanished?`

This turns the episode's finding into a test the viewer runs on their own work: the reel
argues that assistance is normal and automation is rare, and the prompt asks the viewer to
locate that line in their own output.
