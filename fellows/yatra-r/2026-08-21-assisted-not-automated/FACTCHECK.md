# FACTCHECK — Assisted, Not Automated.

## The governing rule

The human supplied seven figures as verified, with sources, and instructed: *"cite them as
shown, don't alter the numbers"* and *"don't invent any additional numbers beyond what I've
given you — use ordinal/descriptive language for anything I haven't provided data for."*

So this episode has exactly two kinds of quantitative content: **the seven supplied
figures, reproduced verbatim with citations**, and **nothing else**.

**Verification status:** these are the human's figures, presented as they were given. This
document does not independently re-verify them against the primary sources — that check was
asserted by the human, and the episode's on-screen citations pass the provenance to the
viewer rather than claiming an independent audit. Per the genre's hard rule 5
(unverified-source caution), that distinction is stated here rather than blurred.

## Structural enforcement

`SeoStat`, `SeoCompare`, `SeoDrop` and `SeoShare` each take a **required** `source` string —
a stat scene is not constructible without its citation. Values are typed as strings and
printed verbatim; they are never parsed for display, rounded, or recomputed. Bar geometry
derives a number from the string purely so two bars read as comparable, but what the viewer
reads is always the original string.

Citations render in muted ink, never in the terracotta accent: a source is provenance, not
emphasis.

## What the episode deliberately does NOT claim

- No figure for how many agencies exist, how many people were hired or let go, or what
  anything costs.
- No growth rate, no forecast, no projection beyond the supplied "plan to" intent figures.
- No causal claim that AI *caused* the price rises — the supplied source says "partly due
  to AI tooling investment", and the narration preserves that hedge with "partly".
- No claim that the seven figures were independently re-verified here.

## Dating risk

The figures are year-stamped by their own citations (2026), which is correct for a
data-driven episode. The composer chrome renders a model chip ("Fable 5") on B00/B11/B22 —
shipped component chrome, never referenced by narration.

## Register check

The Teardown move is B05 and B19: the episode interrupts its own strongest numbers to say
what they don't distinguish, and names the two observations that would overturn its
conclusion. A stats episode that only stacked figures would have failed DOUBLE-CHECK LAW.
