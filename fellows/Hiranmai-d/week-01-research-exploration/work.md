
## Week 1 — Autoimmune Disease Research and Literature Review

### Overview

This week, I focused on establishing the research foundation for ImmuneUnite. The goal was to understand existing computational approaches to autoimmune disease research and identify opportunities to investigate shared immune-related patterns across rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE).

### Work Completed

### Literature Review and Research Analysis

- Reviewed existing studies investigating similarities and differences between autoimmune diseases.
- Examined computational approaches involving transcriptomic meta-analysis, network modeling, non-negative matrix factorization (NMF), LASSO, and SVM-RFE.
- Compared the research objectives, methodologies, and limitations of existing studies.
- Investigated whether studies had identified reproducible immune-related signatures across multiple autoimmune conditions.

### Research Gap Identification

- Identified that several studies focused on individual diseases or pairwise comparisons rather than examining RA, PsA, and SLE together.
- Examined challenges related to inconsistent datasets, differences between patient populations, and limited independent validation.
- Identified the need to distinguish statistical associations from evidence of underlying biological mechanisms.

### Findings and Outcomes

The literature review helped establish a clearer research direction for ImmuneUnite.

Existing studies suggest that certain autoimmune diseases have overlapping immune-related signatures. However, similarities observed in individual datasets do not necessarily demonstrate that the same biological mechanisms are involved.

This raised an important research question about whether computational models can identify patterns that remain consistent across different diseases and datasets.
