## Learning outcome

After watching, a viewer who follows neither sport can:

1. **Identify** which sport they are watching from the floor alone (cage → MMA,
   roped square → boxing),
2. **State** what each sport permits (one weapon versus six), and
3. **Explain** why "world champion" is one name in MMA and up to four in boxing.

Measurable: hand the viewer a photo of an arena and a weight class, and they can
answer all three. This is not "understand combat sports" — there is a behaviour
attached, and it is checkable.

## Audience

Viewers new to combat sports. No assumed knowledge of cages, weight classes,
sanctioning bodies, or submissions. Consequence for the script: *octagon*,
*submission*, *weight division*, *sanctioning body*, *interim title*, and
*undisputed* are each defined on first use, in the beat where they first matter,
and never twice.

## Why this reel exists in this shape

The July 2026 version made the contrast cleanly but bought that cleanliness with
two inaccuracies: it claimed the UFC has exactly one champion per division (an
interim heavyweight champion now exists) and that one boxer in the entire sport
is undisputed (true only of men's boxing). Both were load-bearing lines.

The rebuild keeps the lesson and moves the load onto a claim that does not
expire: **MMA's top tier is one organization, boxing's is four.** The interim
title then becomes a *supporting* detail — one body managing itself — rather
than a contradiction. A viewer who learns "one org versus four" still answers
every question correctly next year, when all the names have changed.

## Known weakness

B03 is the densest beat: it introduces the UFC, weight divisions, the champion,
and the interim caveat in about thirty seconds. If a reviewer finds it heavy,
the interim caveat is the piece to split into its own beat — not the piece to
delete, since it is the falsifiability line.
