Cage, Rules, Belts — MMA vs Boxing, and Who the World Champions Actually Are

Ask who the world champion of "MMA boxing" is and you have asked a trick question. They are two different sports, with different arenas, different rules, and very different answers to who is on top. This explainer is for viewers who follow neither, and it gets you to a working understanding in three questions: what does the floor look like, what is a fighter allowed to do, and how many belts are on the line.

The short version. MMA is fought in an eight-sided cage, the octagon; boxing in a square ring bounded by ropes — the fastest tell there is. Boxing permits one thing, punches above the belt, standing. MMA permits six: punches, kicks, knees, elbows, takedowns, and submissions. And the belts are where the two sports really diverge. MMA's top tier is a single organization, the UFC, with twelve weight divisions and one champion in each. Boxing has no single league at all — four separate bodies, the WBC, WBA, IBF and WBO, each award their own world title, so one weight class can carry four champions at once. Win all four and you are called undisputed, which is rare on purpose.

Every term is defined the first time it appears. Nothing is assumed.

A note on accuracy: belt holders change every few months. The figures in this video were verified on 3 August 2026 and the video says so on screen. The structure — one organization versus four — is the part that lasts.
