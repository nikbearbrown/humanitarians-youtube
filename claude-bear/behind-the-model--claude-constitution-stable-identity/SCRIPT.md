## Deliberately not claimed

- **Not "Claude's identity never changes."** B05 is the correction to that
  overreach — the design is explicitly a trellis (growth within a stable
  shape), not a cage (no change permitted).
- **Not "the inner-experience question is settled."** B05–B06 keep that
  question honestly open and separate from the identity-stability claim —
  conflating the two would overstate what either beat actually supports.
- **The source's B01 key case and `EX` worked example are the same
  scenario, split across the anchor's plant (B01) and payoff (B06)** rather
  than told twice — repeating the full case twice would pad the reel without
  adding an angle.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud then discussed)

> "I want to understand why Claude's stable identity is treated as
> infrastructure rather than personality: what does a stable identity enable
> that a fragile one cannot? Explain what happens to Claude's values and
> behavior when its identity is destabilized, and why identity stability is
> a safety property, not just a UX design choice."

Why it's worth running: the abstract claim ("identity is infrastructure")
only becomes concrete once Claude walks through what actually breaks when
that anchor is missing — it turns a slogan into a specific list of failure
modes.
