# Is Claude's Financial Plan Its Own Judgment?

Ask Claude to build a financial plan and it's tempting to picture it forming
a judgment about what's best for you — the way an advisor who's gotten to
know your situation reaches a conclusion. That's not what's happening.
Anthropic's `financial-plan` skill only recognizes the cases its SKILL.md
names — new client onboarding, an annual review, a scenario request — and
only produces the four things the file names: retirement projections,
education funding, estate planning, cash flow analysis. Watch the anchor:
turn one dial, the retirement age, and the monthly savings target follows it
every time — the same dial a scenario check turns, again and again. A
savings target that jumps a lot for a five-year change in retirement age
isn't the skill improvising — it's the same four steps, fed a different
number. And a target that barely changes between two close retirement ages
doesn't mean the skill weighed them and judged both fine — the inputs were
just close. A financial plan from Claude is its skill running fixed steps
over the inputs you gave it, not a judgment about what's best for you.

## YOUR TURN

"Give me a retirement age, a target retirement income, and one planning
goal — say, saving for a child's education. Run the financial-plan skill to
build a plan around those inputs. Then change only the retirement age by
five years and watch how much of the plan actually moves."

That's the fastest way to see how much of a "plan" is fixed procedure rather
than judgment about your life — instead of trusting the plan's apparent
personalization.

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel redoes a published Teardown-register skill-showcase reel
(`claude-liam-financial-plan`) in the Plain register for a general audience.
The underlying facts are unchanged from the source: the skill builds or
updates a plan covering retirement projections, education funding, estate
planning, and cash flow analysis for onboarding, reviews, or scenario
requests — it does not form an independent judgment about a client's
situation, invent goals, or reason beyond what SKILL.md specifies. This
script makes no claim about any specific client, dollar figure, or
spreadsheet/PDF UI — only the general mechanism (fixed steps that turn
inputs into a plan) and its two failure directions.
