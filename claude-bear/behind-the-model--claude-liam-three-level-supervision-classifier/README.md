# Are You Supervising Claude at the Right Level?

Most people assume trusting AI "the right amount" means picking one comfort
level — a quick glance, a nod — and keeping it steady no matter what they
asked for. It doesn't work that way. Renaming a variable and asking Claude
to propose a system architecture are not the same task. Sheridan and
Verplank catalogued ten levels of automation back in 1978; applied to AI,
three matter: Level One, copy-paste use; Level Two, research-level use;
Level Three, true collaboration. Watch the anchor: Priya, on one ordinary
afternoon, gives the exact same quick glance to a variable rename (fine,
that's calibrated), a market-size citation (below the line), and a full
architecture proposal (far below it). A simple rule flags the mismatch —
short checking on high-stakes work — one flag: applying a 1978 framework to
AI chat is this reel's adaptation, not Sheridan and Verplank's own claim.
Run the flag on Priya's afternoon and it catches exactly what you'd expect —
but a flag only proves the checking didn't match the stakes, not that the
answer was wrong, and no flag doesn't prove nothing was missed. Trusting AI
the right amount isn't a fixed habit. It's calibrated to what you actually
handed it, task by task.
