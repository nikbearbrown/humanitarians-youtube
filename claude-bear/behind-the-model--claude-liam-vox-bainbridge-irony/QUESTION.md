# QUESTION

**The question:** "Why More Automation Creates More Supervision Work, Not Less"

**Source:** redo of `anthropics/youtube/behind-the-model/claude-liam-vox-bainbridge-irony`
(a Teardown-register vox-explainer slate cut of claude-agentic-ai candidate 02 —
Bainbridge's Irony: automation shifts human work upstream and into checkpoints,
it does not eliminate it).

**Why it earns a reel:** the naive picture is that a more capable AI agent means
less work for the person supervising it — capability up, workload down, one
lever pulling the other. The real picture, named by Lisanne Bainbridge in 1983,
is the opposite: the more capable the automated system, the more demanding the
supervisory role becomes, because a wider action surface means more to verify,
scope, and decide about when something unexpected happens.

**Facts preserved from the source:** the developer/agent bug-fix scenario and
its post-hoc checklist (diff, tests, edge cases, scope); Bainbridge's Irony by
name (1983); the naive-lever vs. real-lever contrast (capability up/workload
down vs. both rising together); the Priya file-reorganization anchor (twenty
files by hand vs. two hundred files with an agent — fewer hours, more
deliberate decisions, illustrative numbers); the "agent work fails at scale,
manual work fails locally" implication; the scope-upstream practice (define
what it can touch, what it must not touch, what you'll check).
