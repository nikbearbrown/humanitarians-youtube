# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **More capable agents don't reduce human work — they relocate it: upstream
> into the scope you write before delegating, and into the checkpoint you run
> after.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a more capable agent simply means less work for the person supervising
it — one lever (capability) up, the other (workload) down. Bainbridge's Irony
(1983) says the opposite happens once the agent's reach is wide: the more
capable the system, the more demanding the supervisory role, because a wider
action surface means more to verify, scope, and decide about when something
unexpected happens.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it names where the work went (upstream scope, downstream checkpoint)
rather than just asserting "there's still work," which is the distinction
that matters and the thing a flat "AI creates more work" headline misses.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not "automation never reduces work." For small, easily reversible tasks the
  naive picture holds — flagged in its own both-directions beat.
- Not a verdict on whether this is good or bad automation design (Teardown
  territory) — Plain register explains the mechanism and stops.
- Not precise numbers. The Priya file-count example is illustrative, flagged
  once, not a measured study.
