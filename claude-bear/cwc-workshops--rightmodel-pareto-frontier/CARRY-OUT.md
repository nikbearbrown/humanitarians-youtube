# CARRY-OUT

> The right model isn't the smartest one or the cheapest one — it's whichever
> one sits on the frontier for your task, and a sweep is the only way to find it.

Test: if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still true?
Yes — it compresses the key distinction (the frontier point is task-specific,
not a fixed "best" model) without overstating either extreme, and it names
the sweep as the mechanism rather than intuition.

**Wrong guess this defeats:** "Pick Opus because it's the most accurate, or
pick Haiku because it's the cheapest." The carry-out replaces that frame: on
a real eval, the extremes are usually dominated — Opus's extra accuracy can
cost far more than the accuracy is worth, and Haiku's savings can cost more
accuracy than the task can afford. The frontier model — Sonnet, in the
source's worked example — is the one where you can't improve one axis
without giving up the other, and finding it requires running the sweep, not
guessing from the model names.
