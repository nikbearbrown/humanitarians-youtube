# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Self-critique doesn't mean Claude approves of itself. It means the
> answer gets checked against a written rule instead of a gut feeling — and
> that check still can't catch what the same model was never trained to
> see.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That an AI grading its own homework is inherently worthless, because the
same model can just pass itself. That's backwards in the way that matters:
the check isn't the model's own opinion of whether an answer is good — it's
one fixed, written principle out of sixteen, applied the same way to every
answer, and the result is verified against independent human-labeled
outcomes. But it isn't fully backwards either: the same model that answers
also applies the rule, so a blind spot in what it calls "harmful" can still
slip past its own check of the same thing. That residual is real and is not
what this sentence hides.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (checked against a
written rule, not a gut feeling) without overclaiming that the check is
therefore bias-free.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not a claim that self-critique makes Claude's grading infallible — the
  same-model blind spot is a real, unresolved limit, not solved by matching
  human labels on the cases those labels happened to cover.
- Not a claim that beating human RLHF on helpfulness proves the model is
  more discerning — it may simply be refusing less broadly; that takes a
  separate check to tell apart from "correctly less cautious."
- Not a claim that this is how all AI self-improvement works — it names
  Constitutional AI's specific critique-and-revise loop, not self-critique
  in general.
