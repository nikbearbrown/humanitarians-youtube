# Claude, Demand Intake.

Someone assumes you can just tell Claude to write the demand letter. You
can't yet — first Claude needs the case. Demand-intake is a checklist,
spelled out in one file, for everything a demand letter needs before any
drafting starts: the parties, the facts, the legal basis, the leverage, the
fallback if talks fail, and anything privileged that should stay out of it.
Watch the anchor: an unpaid $40,000 invoice, sixty days late. Fill in those
six for it — the vendor, the due date, the late clause, the interest owed,
the fallback of small claims — and the letter has ground to stand on. But
only those six: a fact nobody mentions never makes it in, and this step
never writes the letter itself. It hands a finished file to a separate step
that drafts.

## YOUR TURN

"I have an unpaid invoice. Before you draft a demand letter, ask me for the
parties, the facts, the legal basis, my leverage, my fallback if they don't
pay, and anything privileged that should stay out of it. Then tell me what's
still missing."

Why it's worth running: it forces the six-question intake pass before any
letter gets written, and surfaces exactly which of the six you haven't
actually answered yet.

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel redoes an existing Teardown skill-teardown sheet as a Plain-register
hai-simple explainer. Unlike some sibling redos in this family, the source
sheet's narration was already filled in with real facts (parties, facts,
basis, leverage, BATNA, privilege filters; output to a structured file a
separate `demand-draft` step reads) — its named source file just doesn't
exist on this machine, which didn't block the rebuild. Nothing about UI, file
format, or command syntax is invented; the account stays generic to what the
source's own narration already states. Intake and drafting are kept as two
distinct steps throughout, matching the source's own two-skill split.
