# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Five careful people are not automatically a careful team. The team's
> safety lives in the fence around what they share — not in how carefully
> each person acts alone.**

(Compressed from the source's B04/B08 claim — "the gap is in the shared
space, not in any individual's practice" / "five careful individuals do not
automatically produce a careful team, the shared space needs its own fence"
— restated in Plain register. It states mechanism, not a design verdict, so
nothing needed removing.)

## The wrong guess it defeats

That five individually careful people should sum to a careful team — that
personal diligence is transitive, so a team of careful individuals is a
careful team. It isn't, whenever agents touch a shared asset. A connector one
person adds for their own convenience can be added at the account level,
which every teammate's agent then inherits — the gap opens at the boundary
between people, in the shared space nobody individually owns or watches.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (personal care vs. a
shared-space fence) without claiming every teammate needs constant
supervision or that individual carefulness doesn't matter at all.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not a claim that individual carefulness is worthless — it's a precondition,
  just not a sufficient one (source B01: "no one intended this, no one
  knew," said of five people who were each being careful).
- Not a claim that every shared thing needs a rule — a teammate quietly
  reading their own files under their own login isn't a team-level risk
  (direction A).
- Not an accusation that anyone acted maliciously or carelessly — the gap is
  structural (where a connector's grant lands), not a character flaw.
