# Content Strategy Isn't Writing Your Posts

Someone hears "content strategy" and assumes Claude will hand back finished
posts or a content calendar. It doesn't. It reads real sales data from
QuickBooks, PayPal, or Square, ranks products into top performers, slow
movers, and trending items, layers in seasonality, and returns a strategic
brief with six parts: an executive summary, what to push hard, what to hold
steady, what to reposition or pause, seasonal opportunities, and recommended
offers. No calendar, no captions, no images — those wait for the owner's
approval and a separate skill.

## YOUR TURN

"Here's what sold last month: [your top 5 sellers and slowest 5, made-up
numbers are fine]. Rank these into what to push hard, what to hold steady,
and what to reposition or pause — and stop there, don't draft any actual
posts yet."

That's the whole idea: sales numbers in, a ranked brief out — never a
caption or a calendar without your say-so.

---

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel never claims Claude can't write marketing posts — a separate
skill handles that, after this one's brief is approved. It also isn't a
claim that QuickBooks, PayPal, and Square are the only data sources a
content plan could ever use in general: that's the boundary this specific
skill states for itself, not a limit on the idea.
