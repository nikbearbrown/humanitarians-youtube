# CARRY-OUT.md — financial-services--claude-liam-model-update

**Carry-out sentence (written first, per CARRY-OUT LAW):**

> A model update doesn't make Claude smarter — it makes the model current,
> and the decision still belongs to a person.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it names the distinction (Claude changing vs. the model
changing) without naming the topic (earnings, valuation), and it survives
being repeated cold.

**The wrong guess it's built to defeat:** that a "model update," on a
Claude reel, means Anthropic shipped a new Claude. It doesn't.
`model-update` is a Skill: given new data — quarterly earnings, management
guidance, macro changes, or a revised assumption — Claude adjusts the
estimates, recalculates the valuation, and flags whatever changed enough to
matter. The model that updates is the financial model, not Claude. Whether
to act on what changed is not this skill's job and isn't in its file. That
narrowness is stated as a design fact, not judged as a strength or weakness
(Plain register, no verdict).

**Sentence it defeats, made explicit:** "a model update means Claude got
smarter" → "a model update means the numbers got current, and a person
still decides what to do about it."
