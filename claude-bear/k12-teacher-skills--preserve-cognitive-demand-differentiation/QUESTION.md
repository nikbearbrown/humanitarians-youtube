# QUESTION — k12-teacher-skills--preserve-cognitive-demand-differentiation

**The question:** What's the most important thing NOT to do when differentiating
a lesson for students at very different levels?

**Source:** `anthropics/k12-teacher-skills/youtube/preserve-cognitive-demand-differentiation/beat_sheet.json`
("The Hard Case", `nikbearbrown` brand, 8 filled beats, source chapter "Agent
Skills for K-12 Teachers (Anthropic) — k12-lesson-differentiation").

**Facts carried over, unchanged:**
- The trap: making the task easier for struggling students removes the exact
  thinking the lesson is trying to build.
- The worked case: 17 ÷ 5 across three tiers — Below (concrete array, 17 dots
  grouped by 5, 2 circled), At (3 R2, basket analogy), Above (proof: why must
  R < divisor) — all three keep the remainder as the hard case.
- The general rule: differentiation varies scaffold, representation, and
  entry point; never the intellectual demand.
- The ceiling check (UDL): does every tier arrive at the same destination? If
  not — if the below-tier task has a lower ceiling — that's tracking, not
  differentiation, even from a good scaffold.
- Cognitive load theory partition: extraneous load (waste) is what the
  scaffold should absorb; germane load (the real conceptual struggle) is the
  curriculum itself and must stay with the learner.
