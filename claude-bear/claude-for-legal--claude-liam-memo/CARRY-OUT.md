# CARRY-OUT — claude-for-legal--claude-liam-memo

**The sentence:**

> A legal memo isn't reliable because it reads confidently — it's reliable once every citation in it has been checked.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still true? Yes —
it names the actual boundary (citation verification) without naming any tool's specific
steps, so it survives being repeated out of context.

**The wrong guess it's built to defeat:** that a legal memo is a final legal opinion —
a confident, well-cited answer that's ready to act on the moment it's produced. The
reel falsifies this with a concrete case (B01): a citation that names a real case,
correctly, by name and holding, except that case was overruled — and reads exactly
like every correct citation around it. A confident memo built on a dead case is not
a reliable one.

**Why this compresses the distinction that matters, not the topic:** the topic is
"legal memo"; the distinction is *draft analysis vs. verified analysis*. Every body
beat exists to make that distinction land — the overruled-citation case (B01) is what
can go wrong inside a confident draft, and the verification boundary (B02–B03) is
what turns a correct-sounding draft into something an attorney can actually rely on.
