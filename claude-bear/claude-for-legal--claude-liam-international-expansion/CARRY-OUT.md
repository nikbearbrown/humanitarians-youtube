**The line (written first, GATE C):**

> The contract can't fix a bad classification. Work out how the role is
> classified and who the legal employer will be before you draft anything —
> the country's labor law decides that, not the label on the page.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it compresses the one distinction the reel is built to land
(classification-and-legal-employer before contract), not the topic
(international hiring generally).

**The wrong guess it defeats:** that the first move is to draft an
employment contract — the same document you'd use to hire locally, with the
country's name swapped in. A contract's label doesn't decide how a role is
classified; the country's labor law does. The anchor: a developer in Berlin,
working forty hours a week for one client, using that client's laptop and
tools — call the contract whatever you like, a German court will read that
relationship as an employee's, not a contractor's, and reclassify it
regardless of the page. Classification and the legal-employer decision have
to happen before the contract, or the contract is written for the wrong
relationship.
