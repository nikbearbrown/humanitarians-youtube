# What Comes First When You Hire Abroad?

A team about to hire its first person in a new country usually reaches for
an employment contract — the same one they'd use locally, with the
country's name swapped in. But a contract's label doesn't decide how a role
is classified; the country's labor law does. Watch the anchor: a developer
in Berlin, working forty hours a week for one client, using that client's
laptop and tools — call the contract whatever you like, a local court will
read that relationship as an employee's regardless of the page. Passing as
contractor-shaped today doesn't mean the classification is locked in, and
landing on employee-shaped doesn't mean you need your own entity — an
Employer of Record can stand in as the legal employer while you sort that
out.

under local labor law, the difference between opening our own entity there
versus using an Employer of Record, and which statutory terms — notice

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel is a reconstruction: the source reel it redoes had never had its
skill-specific facts written (its narration carried literal unfilled
template placeholders, and the SKILL.md file it pointed at doesn't exist on
this machine). Rather than invent specific product claims or a
jurisdiction's exact legal test, this script describes the generic shape of
an international-hiring implementation plan — classification, legal
employer, mandatory terms — not any single tool's documented behavior or
country's specific statute. It never claims a contract is useless, only
that it has to follow the classification and legal-employer decision, not
precede it.
