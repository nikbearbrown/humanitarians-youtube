# Data Visualization Isn't About Making It Colorful

Someone assumes Claude's data-visualization skill is about picking bright
colors. It isn't. The chart type comes first, chosen from the relationship
in the data — a ranking gets a horizontal bar, a composition over time gets
a stacked area, many variables at once get a heatmap. Some charts are
ruled out outright: pie charts, 3D charts, dual axes. Only after that does
color get a say, and it has to earn its place — one accent color carries
the finding, red and green alone are never enough (about 8% of men can't
tell them apart), and every chart has to survive in black and white via
pattern or line style.

## YOUR TURN

"Here's some made-up scores from a five-question customer survey, one to
ten: Ease of use 8, Price 4, Support 7, Reliability 9, Design 5. Tell me
what chart type this calls for, and pick colors that would still make
sense to someone who's colorblind."

That's the whole idea: chart-type reasoning comes first, and colorblind-safe
color is a real constraint, not an afterthought.

---

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel never claims color doesn't matter — the skill still applies
color, typography, and layout practice, just after the chart type is
chosen, not instead of it. It also isn't a claim that this specific
avoid-list (pie charts, 3D charts, dual axes) is a universal design law —
it's the boundary this specific skill states for itself.
