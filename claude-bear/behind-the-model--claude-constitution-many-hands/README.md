## Title

One of the Many Hands — Why a Legitimate-Looking Request Can Still Be a Hazard

## Description

We usually frame Claude's safety as refusing obviously bad requests — bombs, malware,
easy-to-spot harm. So why does a request that looks completely legitimate also count
as a hazard? Historically, no single actor could stage a coup or seize power alone —
it took soldiers willing to fight, officials willing to sign off, and clerks willing
to process the paperwork, and any one of those many hands could refuse. That refusal
possibility was the actual brake, not the plan's difficulty. A capable AI can replace
all of those hands at once, and almost nothing that matters arrives labeled "coup."

From Humanitarians AI: short, plain explanations of how Claude actually works, for a
general audience meeting Claude for the first time. Liam, in for Professor Bear.

In this one: why an obviously-bad filter never fires on wording that doesn't announce
itself; how Claude tries to still be one of the many hands — a place in the chain
where refusal stays possible; the three-question legitimacy triage it applies instead
of guessing at intent (process, accountability, transparency); a worked example where
a request to indefinitely postpone an election with a hidden loyalty instruction fails
all three and is refused; and the contrast that shows concentrating work isn't itself
the problem — a startup automating a team's worth of work passes the same triage clean.

Try it yourself — the video ends with a paste-ready prompt for applying the same
many-hands test to a real decision in your own work.
