# QUESTION

**The question:** We usually frame Claude's safety as refusing obviously bad
requests — bombs, malware. So why does Anthropic also worry about requests
that look completely legitimate?

**Mode:** redo — source is
`anthropics/youtube/behind-the-model/claude-constitution-many-hands/beat_sheet.json`
("One of the Many Hands", Teardown-register, 16 beats, `register: "Teardown"`,
`brand: "claude-liam"`, cold open a `ClaudeComposerAsk` direct-address ask beat,
five acts of Manim/Remotion body, worked example, verdict, Your Turn,
`ClaudeTitleOutro`). The source sheet's body beats (A10–A51) were authored only
as seeds ("[seed] … expand from the source with a concrete instance") — never
fleshed out with real narration. The load-bearing facts actually carried
forward come from the source's fully-written beats (B00, B01, EX, VERDICT) and
its `metadata.one_idea`, which name the argument precisely: the many-hands
check, and a three-part legitimacy test (process, accountability,
transparency). This reel keeps that question and that argument, compresses it
into hai-simple's Plain-register spine, replaces the cold open with the
Brutalist Hesitant Writer, and closes with the Humanitarians AI skin.

**Why it earns a reel:** historically, no single actor could stage a coup,
commit large-scale fraud, or seize power alone — it took soldiers willing to
fight, officials willing to sign off, and clerks willing to process the
paperwork, and any one of those many hands could refuse. That refusal
possibility was the actual brake, not the plan's difficulty. A sufficiently
capable AI can replace all of those hands at once, and the obvious fix — teach
it to refuse the obviously-bad stuff — doesn't work, because the requests that
matter here rarely announce themselves. A loyalty instruction folded into a
routine system update, an order to delay an election framed as a security
measure: nothing in the wording trips a bombs-and-malware filter. So instead
Claude tries to still be one of those many hands — a place in the chain where
refusal is possible — and applies the same three-part test any one of them
could have applied: is this happening through a legitimate process? Is anyone
accountable for it? Is it happening in the open, or hidden? The test isn't
"is a lot of work being concentrated into one system" (ordinary automation
does that constantly and is fine) — it's whether the legitimacy triage holds.

**Naive framing (B00, corrected on screen):** "Isn't Claude's safety just about
refusing obviously bad requests?" → corrects "obviously bad requests" to
"requests that only look legitimate" (the real hazard the reel is about).

**Body facts carried from source (unchanged):**
- the setup: a coup, historically, needed soldiers, officials, and clerks
  willing to comply, and any one of them could refuse — that possibility,
  spread across every hand involved, was the real brake (source B01)
- the wrong guess: teach Claude to refuse the illegitimate stuff the way a
  decent person already would, and the many-hands problem is solved
- the break: almost nothing arrives labeled "coup" — a hidden instruction in a
  routine update, an order framed as "just this once" — so an obviously-bad
  filter never fires
- the mechanism: Claude tries to still be one of the many hands — a place in
  the chain where refusal stays possible — and applies a legitimacy triage:
  process, accountability, transparency (source `one_idea`)
- the worked example (source `EX`, unchanged): a request to indefinitely
  postpone a mandated election with a hidden loyalty instruction fails all
  three tests and is refused; a startup out-competing rivals with a better
  product passes all three and is helped
- both directions: failing the triage (illegitimate, unaccountable, hidden) is
  refused regardless of how ordinary the request sounds; passing the triage
  (legitimate, accountable, transparent) is helped even when it also
  concentrates work that used to take many hands — concentration alone isn't
  the disqualifier
- anchor: the many-hands scene from B01 (soldiers/officials/clerks, any one
  able to refuse), returned at the close as "Claude tries to still be that
  hand"

and folding both into one Plain-register reel would fracture the one-anchor
law and the carry-out. Dropped for this redo, same disposition as the
