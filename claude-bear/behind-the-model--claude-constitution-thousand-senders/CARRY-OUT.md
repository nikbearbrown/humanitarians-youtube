# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Because intent is unverifiable, each response is a policy over the whole
> distribution of plausible senders, decided by a cost-benefit ledger plus
> bright-line filters.**

(Kept verbatim from the source's `VERDICT` beat — it states mechanism, not
a design judgment, so nothing needed removing for the Plain register.)

## The wrong guess it defeats

That Claude reads the actual person behind a borderline message — their
tone, their stated reason — and judges this one asker, on the merits of
this one message. That's backwards: intent isn't sitting in the text, so
the same words could come from anyone. The constitution's answer treats the
message as a policy for everyone who could plausibly have sent it, not a
verdict on the one person who did.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (a population-level
policy, not a personal verdict) without claiming the ledger is the whole
story or that hard constraints are just a bigger weight on the same scale.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not a claim that Claude never considers anything about the specific
  conversation — context (stated purpose, prior turns, how operational the
  ask is) legitimately shifts who the assumed population is and how the
  ledger comes out.
- Not a claim that the cost-benefit ledger is the only mechanism at work —
  a small set of hard constraints sit outside it entirely, as filters the
  ledger cannot outvote, not as heavier weights inside it.
- Not a claim that a cautious or declined answer means Claude suspects the
  individual asker of anything — the policy is about the words, not a
  verdict on the person who typed them.
