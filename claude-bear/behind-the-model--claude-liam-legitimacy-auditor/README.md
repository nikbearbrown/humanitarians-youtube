## Title

Fluent Isn't Legitimate — Auditing AI Answers with Suchman's Three Types of Legitimacy

## Description

A confident, fluent AI answer feels trustworthy — but fluency is only ever an answer to
one question: does it feel taken for granted, natural? Sociologist Mark Suchman's 1995
framework splits organizational legitimacy into three separate questions: does it serve
the stakeholder's interest (pragmatic); is someone accountable for it (moral); does it
feel right, taken for granted (cognitive). Put the identical AI answer in two rooms — a
finance committee reviewing a projection, and a hospital bedside — and the same fluent
sentence can hold in one and fail in the other, because the rooms differ in who is
named to answer for it, not in how the sentence reads. The fix isn't asking the AI to
sound more careful — it's naming an accountable party and a review step before the
answer gets acted on.

In this one: the natural shortcut of treating a fluent answer as already legitimate on
every count; the falsifying case — a named CFO versus no one named, same sentence,
different verdict; the fix of naming an accountable party and a review step before the
answer is acted on; and the two-sided limit — a fluent answer proves nothing about who's
accountable, and disclosure or hedging doesn't fix the gap either.
