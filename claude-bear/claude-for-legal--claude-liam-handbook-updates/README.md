# When Should You Update the Handbook?

An employee handbook usually gets written once and reviewed on a schedule —
maybe once a year. That feels responsible, but it misses the actual
trigger: a law that changes in March doesn't wait for the December
read-through. The section it touches is wrong the day the law takes
effect, and it stays wrong for however many months are left until the
scheduled review. Watch the anchor: a state raises its paid-leave minimum
in the spring — that one change touches exactly one section, the leave
policy, and gets logged with what changed, why, and when it took effect.
Being flagged doesn't mean the new language is finished — it still needs a
legal read — and a quiet quarter doesn't mean the handbook is complete,
only that no tracked law changed.
