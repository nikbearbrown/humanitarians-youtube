# CARRY-OUT — claude-for-legal--claude-liam-handbook-updates

**The line (written first, GATE C):**

> A handbook doesn't go out of date on a birthday — it goes out of date the
> day a law changes. Track updates by the law that triggers them, not a
> date on the calendar.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it compresses the one distinction the reel is built to land
(law-triggered maintenance vs. calendar-triggered maintenance), not the
topic (employee handbooks generally).

**The wrong guess it defeats:** that keeping a handbook current means
reviewing it once a year, on a schedule the company sets. A law that
changes in March doesn't wait for the December review — the section it
touches (a leave policy, a wage rule) is wrong the moment the law takes
effect, and it stays wrong for however many months are left until the
scheduled read-through. The fix isn't reviewing more often; it's tracking
updates by the actual trigger — a specific legal change — and logging
exactly what changed, why, and when, rather than silently rewriting the
page at the next annual pass.
