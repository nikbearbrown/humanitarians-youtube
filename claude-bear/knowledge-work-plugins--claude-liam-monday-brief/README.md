# The Monday Brief Isn't Memory

Someone assumes Claude already knows their numbers every Monday, like it's
memorized the business. It doesn't. It checks — reading a written file that
spells out exactly what a Monday brief means here: cash, sales, and
pipeline; the week ahead; and your top three to-dos, one page, every time.
The instructions live in a single file Claude reads before it works; ask for
anything outside that list and there's no step that covers it.

## YOUR TURN

"Pull together a one-page Monday brief for me: cash and sales, what's moving
in the pipeline, what's on deck this week, and my top three to-dos. Tell me
if anything's missing that you'd need from me first."

That's the whole idea: Claude doesn't recall your numbers — it checks them
against a fixed list, and tells you what it's missing.

---

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel never claims Claude retains your numbers between runs, or names a
data source (no CRM, no accounting tool). It also isn't a verdict on whether
five items is the right set — the boundary (only what the file specifies
gets checked) is stated as fact, not argued with.
