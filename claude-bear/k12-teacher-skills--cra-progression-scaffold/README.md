# The CRA Ladder. — Concrete, Representational, Abstract in One Lesson

A teacher's students are at three different levels with fraction addition —
does that mean three separate lessons? It doesn't. The CRA ladder —
Concrete, Representational, Abstract — runs as three entry points into the
SAME lesson, all converging on the same result: 1/3 + 1/2 = 5/6. A student
below grade level starts at the fraction circles, a student at grade level
starts at the tape diagram, a student above starts at the equation itself —
and every rung climbs toward the same abstract destination. There's a
catch: the concrete support that helps a beginner can become noise for a
student who has already moved up a rung (the expertise-reversal effect),
which is why scaffolding carries a three-part contract — contingency,
fading, transfer — and skipping the fade leaves support in place forever.
Claude doesn't build three separate lessons; it builds one shared target
with three rungs to reach it, and the test is whether every rung gets there
without skipping a step.

## Deliberately not claimed

No claim about exactly when to fade a specific real student's support — the
expertise-reversal effect and the contingency/fading/transfer contract are
stated as a test to apply, not a fixed schedule. No claim that the CRA
ladder is a one-time setting for a student; the video states the opposite —
it's a path, and the rung that helped yesterday may not help today. The
mechanism (one shared abstract target, three entry points, a fading test)
holds regardless of grade level or math topic.
