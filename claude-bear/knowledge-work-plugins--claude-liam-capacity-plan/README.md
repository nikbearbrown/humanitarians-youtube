# How Does Claude Plan Team Capacity?

Ask Claude to plan your team's capacity and it's tempting to picture it
sensing overload the way an experienced manager would — reading strain
into how the request is phrased. That's not what's happening. Anthropic's
`capacity-plan` skill runs two fixed steps on the numbers you give it:
workload analysis, which totals each person's committed hours against
their available capacity, then utilization forecasting, which projects
that load forward across the quarter. Watch the anchor: a team heading
into quarterly planning says they're slammed. The question gets asked,
matched to "team feels overallocated, need the numbers," stepped through,
and returns one answer: utilization sits at 118 percent, hire one person
or cut one project. Ask that same question twice and the same utilization
figure and recommendation come back. Ask for something outside that frame
— say, whether the team's morale can take the load — and there's nothing
tailored to reach for. Capacity-plan is a written procedure run on the
numbers you supply — not Claude's private sense of who's stretched thin.

## YOUR TURN

"Tell me my team is committed to 118% of capacity heading into next
quarter, and run the capacity-plan skill: work the workload analysis,
forecast utilization, and report the number and the recommendation. Then
ask something outside that frame — whether the team's morale can take
it."

Watching whether Claude invents a bespoke answer for an unsupported
question, or tells you plainly that it has nothing tailored for it, is the
fastest way to see that the plan comes from a written, numbers-driven
procedure rather than manager instinct — rather than just trusting that it
does.

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel redoes a published Teardown-register skill-showcase reel
(`claude-liam-capacity-plan`) in the Plain register for a general
audience. The underlying facts are unchanged from the source: the skill
plans resource capacity — workload analysis and utilization forecasting —
used when heading into quarterly planning, the team feels overallocated
and you need the numbers, deciding whether to hire or deprioritize, or
stress-testing whether upcoming projects fit the people you have. It does
not exercise private manager instinct about who's overloaded beyond what
the numbers and the file already define. This script makes no claim about
any specific company, team, or headcount figure — only the general
mechanism (a written procedure run on the numbers you supply) and its two
failure directions.
