# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Deal-tracker gives the same reliable output for exactly what's written
> in its steps — and nothing at all for what isn't.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a Claude skill named "deal tracker" understands your deals — that it
reasons about your pipeline, your counterparties, your priorities. It
doesn't. SKILL.md is a plain-language instruction set: track milestones,
deadlines, action items, and status; surface what's overdue; trigger on six
exact phrases. Ask for anything inside that — same input, same output, every
run. Ask for anything outside it, and there is no clever fallback. It's
simply not handled.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (reliable inside the
spec, silent outside it) without naming the specific trigger phrases or
steps, which could change without changing the underlying fact.

## What it deliberately does not say

- **Not a verdict on the design.** The source's B03/BVDT framed the spec as
  "what it gets right: repeatable results" against "what it bites: anything
  outside the spec" — Teardown language, weighing whether the trade-off is
  well made. Plain keeps the same underlying fact (reliable inside the
  written spec, unhandled outside it) but states it as a mechanism
  boundary, not a critique of the skill's design.
- **Not that deal-tracker is unintelligent or broken.** The reel never
  claims the skill fails at its stated job — only that its stated job is
  the whole job. Every claim inside the spec is a "works as documented"
  claim.
- **Not an exhaustive list of the six trigger phrases in the carry-out
  itself.** They're stated once, in B01, as the concrete case; the
  carry-out compresses the shape of the boundary, not its exact wording.
