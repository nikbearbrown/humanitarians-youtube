# Claude, Legal Writing.

Claude picked up a skill called legal-writing — does it grade your memo, or
does it just write a better one for you? Neither, exactly. A "skill" is a
folder holding one file, SKILL.md, that Claude reads before it starts. This
one's hard rule, in its own words: "no rewriting. Ever." Ask it to rewrite
your draft anyway and it refuses, then offers structural feedback, one
labeled example, or a drill on the underlying rule instead. First it reads
the whole draft top to bottom — say, a 1L's memo arguing negligence in a car
crash — and names what it actually is: memo, brief, paper, or exam essay.
Feedback then runs top-down: structure, then analysis depth, then clarity
and citation. On structure it's confident; on whether the rule itself is
stated correctly, it won't guess — it flags VERIFY and leaves the legal call
to you. A confident structural note proves nothing about the law inside, and
a VERIFY flag proves nothing about the structure — both can be broken in the
same paragraph.
