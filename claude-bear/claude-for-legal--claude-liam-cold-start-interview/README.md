# What's a Cold-Start Interview?

A brand-new legal matter, and Claude has no memory of the last one -- every
conversation starts from zero. The instinct is to just brief it: paste in
everything you think matters and hope that covers it. But a brief only
covers what its writer remembers to mention, and the classic gaps --
jurisdiction, conflicts, retainer scope -- are exactly what people assume
goes without saying. A cold-start interview flips it: instead of trusting
what you remember, Claude asks the same fixed questions every time. Watch
the anchor: jurisdiction, the detail everyone assumes is obvious, gets an
answer even when nobody thought to volunteer it. Answering once only
covers this matter, though -- the next one starts cold again -- and a brief
that feels thorough isn't the same as complete.

## YOUR TURN

"Before we start this matter, don't assume you know the details. Ask me a
short fixed intake: matter type, jurisdiction, any conflicts, and scope.
Ask them one at a time and wait for my answers before doing anything
else."

That's a cold start, done right -- the same fixed questions every time,
instead of trusting whatever you remembered to volunteer in a free-form
brief.
