# Should You Respond to an Infringement Letter Right Away?

A newcomer's first instinct on an infringement or cease-and-desist letter is
to respond right away — the language reads like a fire (cease-and-desist,
a threat to sue, a deadline in bold). But most incoming claims are form
letters mailed to hundreds of companies at once, with no evidence attached
and nothing specific to your product. Rushing every one of those to counsel
just delays the one that's actually different. What decides urgency is four
checks: who's asserting the claim, what specific right they're claiming,
what evidence is attached, and what deadline is demanded. Watch the anchor:
a letter claiming your packaging copies a competitor's — all exclamation
points, no attachments — until it's run through those four checks, you
can't tell whether it's noise or the one that matters. Clearing every check
doesn't prove a claim is valid, and a bare form letter isn't zero risk
forever either.

## YOUR TURN

"Pull out four things from this letter: who's asserting the claim, what
specific right they're claiming, what evidence is attached, and what
deadline is demanded."

Whichever of those four comes up empty on a real letter is what tells you
how fast it actually needs to move — the exact gap a tone-based read would
miss.
