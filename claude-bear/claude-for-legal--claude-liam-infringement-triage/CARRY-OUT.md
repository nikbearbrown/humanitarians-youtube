# CARRY-OUT — claude-for-legal--claude-liam-infringement-triage

**The line (written first, GATE C):**

> An infringement letter's tone tells you nothing about its merit. Triage
> sorts by what's actually claimed, so the one letter that matters doesn't
> wait behind a stack of form threats.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it compresses the one distinction the reel is built to land
(sort by claim specifics, not by how alarming the letter reads), not the
topic (IP enforcement generally).

**The wrong guess it defeats:** that the first move on any infringement
letter is to respond — draft a reply, escalate straight to an attorney,
because the language sounds urgent (cease-and-desist, threat to sue, a
deadline in bold). Most incoming claims are form letters mailed to many
companies at once, with no evidence attached and nothing specific to your
product; routing every one of those straight to counsel at the same
priority as a real claim just delays the real one. The anchor: a letter
claiming your packaging copies a competitor's, all exclamation points and
no attachments — until it's run through the same four checks as everything
else, it isn't clear whether it's noise or the one that matters.
