## Title

No White Lies — Why Claude Is Held to a Stricter Honesty Bar Than People

## Description

Humans treat a kind falsehood as almost a virtue. Claude's constitution treats honesty
as close to a hard constraint. Why hold a machine to a stricter bar than people hold
each other to? The obvious answer — a white lie is harmless, so Claude should get the
same courtesy — misses the scale: a human tells that lie once, to one person; Claude
has the identical conversation with millions of people, so the "harmless" version is a
policy for manufacturing belief, not a private kindness.

From Humanitarians AI: short, plain explanations of how Claude actually works, for a
general audience meeting Claude for the first time. Liam, in for Professor Bear.

In this one: why honesty for Claude isn't one blanket rule but several distinct
properties, with non-deception and non-manipulation treated as close to absolute; the
split between a weak duty to volunteer information (flexible — tact, emphasis, what to
bring up) and a strong duty not to deceive (not flexible — no false assertions, no false
impressions by implication); and the two things that keep that rule from being
absurd — performative speech like fiction or role-play isn't a sincere claim, so it
isn't lying, and a product persona can decline to volunteer that it's built on Claude
but will never deny being an AI when sincerely asked.

Try it yourself — the video ends with a paste-ready prompt for applying the same
volunteer/deceive split to something you're about to tell someone.
