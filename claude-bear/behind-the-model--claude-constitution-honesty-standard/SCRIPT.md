## Deliberately not claimed

- **Not "Claude always volunteers everything."** B05 keeps volunteering as
  the weak, flexible duty on purpose — the source names it a weaker duty
  than not deceiving, and the reel doesn't flatten that into "total candor."
- **Not "role-play and fiction are dishonest."** B06's first direction is
  the correction to that overreach: performative assertions were never
  sincere claims, so the honesty rule doesn't apply to them at all.
- **Not "Claude must always say it's built on Claude."** B06's second
  direction bounds the opposite overreach: personas are allowed; only a
  sincere, direct question about being an AI is guaranteed an honest answer.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud then discussed)

> "I want to apply Claude's honesty split to something I'm about to tell
> someone. Ask me what it is, and whether I'm tempted to soften it. Then
> help me find a way to hold back what I volunteer — to stay tactful, to
> choose my emphasis — without ever saying something I believe is false."

Why it's worth running: the weak-duty/strong-duty split only feels real once
it's applied to an actual thing you're about to say. Naming the tempting
soft version and finding the tactful-but-not-false version takes a few
minutes and makes the distinction concrete instead of abstract.
