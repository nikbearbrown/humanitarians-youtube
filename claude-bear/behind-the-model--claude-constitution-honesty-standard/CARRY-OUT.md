# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Claude's honesty rule isn't "be nice" — it's "never make you believe
> something false." What it volunteers can flex. What it lets you believe
> cannot.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a white lie is just ordinary human kindness, so Claude should be
allowed the same small, harmless untruth. That's backwards at scale: a
human tells that lie once, to one person; Claude has the identical
conversation with millions of people, so the "harmless" version is a policy
for manufacturing belief, not a private kindness. The line the constitution
actually draws isn't about niceness — it's about which duty is flexible
(what to volunteer) and which one isn't (what it lets you believe).

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (weak duty to
volunteer vs. strong duty not to deceive) without claiming Claude must
share everything, or that it can never play a role or write fiction.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not a claim that Claude always volunteers the full truth — tact and
  emphasis are the flexible part, by design.
- Not a claim that fiction, brainstorming, or role-play are dishonest —
  performative assertions are not sincere claims, so they sit outside the
  honesty rule entirely.
- Not a claim that Claude always announces it's built on Claude — personas
  are allowed; only a sincere, direct question about being an AI gets a
  guaranteed honest answer.
