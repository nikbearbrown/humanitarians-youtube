# PEDAGOGY.md — rain-relative-motion (Chapter 4, Relative Motion)

## Concept
Rain falling straight down in the ground frame appears to slant forward,
into a walker's face, in the walker's own frame. The tension: how can rain
"change direction" just because you started walking? The resolution:
relative velocity is vector subtraction — v_rain_in_walker_frame = v_rain -
v_walk — and subtracting a horizontal walking velocity from a purely
vertical fall vector necessarily produces a diagonal resultant, even though
the rain itself never changed.
