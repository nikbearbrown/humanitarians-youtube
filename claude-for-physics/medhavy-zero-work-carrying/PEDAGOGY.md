# PEDAGOGY.md — zero-work-carrying (Chapter 7, Work)

## Concept
Carrying a box horizontally at constant speed, the person's upward force on
the box is perpendicular to the box's horizontal displacement, so the work
done by that force is W = F d cos 90° = 0, even though muscles expend energy.

## Physics check (against the textbook, 7.1 Work)
- Eq. 7.1 / constant-force form: W = F·d = F d cos θ, θ the angle between force
  and displacement. Narration B02 states exactly this.
- "zero work is done when the force is perpendicular to the displacement
  (cos θ = 0)" and Figure 7.3(c): a person walking horizontally at constant
  speed holding a briefcase does zero work on it because the angle is 90°.
  The video's scenario is this figure; "steady pace" in narration matches the
  textbook's "constant speed" condition (so the person exerts no horizontal
  force on the box, and the only force considered is the upward one).
- B03's biology statement is phrased carefully and correctly: muscle fibers
  consume chemical energy internally to sustain a static contraction; none
  of it is transferred to the box (the box's kinetic energy is unchanged:
  "neither speeds up nor slows down"). No claim beyond that.
- B04: only the component of a force along the displacement does work — the
  textbook's "product of one component acting parallel to the other".
