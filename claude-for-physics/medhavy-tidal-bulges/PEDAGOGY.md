# PEDAGOGY.md — tidal-bulges (Chapter 13.6, Tidal Forces)

## Concept
The Moon's pull weakens with distance, so the near side of Earth is pulled
harder than the center, and the far side less. Earth as a whole accelerates
with its center; subtracting the center's pull leaves a residual (the tidal
force) toward the Moon on the near side and away from it on the far side,
stretching the oceans into two bulges along the Earth-Moon line.

## Physics check (against 13-gravitation/6-tidal-forces.mdx)
- "One side of Earth is closer to the Moon than the other side, by a distance equal to Earth's diameter. Hence, the gravitational force is greater on the near side than on the far side. The magnitude at the center of Earth is between these values. This is why a tidal bulge appears on both sides of Earth." -> B02.
- "The tidal force can be viewed as the difference between the force at the center of Earth and that at any other location." (Fig. 13.18) -> B03 subtracts the center pull.
- "The net force on Earth causes it to orbit about the Earth-Moon center of mass" -> narration "Earth as a whole falls toward the Moon with the acceleration of its center".
- "Relative to the Moon, the bulges stay fixed ... But Earth rotates" -> B04 land turns beneath fixed bulges.
- Arrow lengths: real near/center and far/center pull ratios are (60/59)^2 = 1.034 and (60/61)^2 = 0.968, a few percent. The diagram exaggerates the first-order (linear) difference: center = a, near = 1.4a, far = 0.6a, so the residuals are equal and opposite (+-0.4a), as in the real, nearly symmetric case. Narration says "drawn greatly exaggerated" (textbook: "this diagram is greatly exaggerated"). The Moon is drawn far closer than its real 60 Earth radii.
