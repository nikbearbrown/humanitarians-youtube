# PEDAGOGY.md — ball-and-feather (Chapter 3, Free Fall)

## Concept
A 10 kg ball and a 0.01 kg feather, released simultaneously in vacuum, hit
the ground at the same instant. The tension: gravity's force scales with
mass (F = mg), so the ball feels a much larger force — yet acceleration
(a = F/m) is identical for both, because mass cancels.

## Audit against selection.md bar
- **Single tension:** "the ball should fall faster — why doesn't it?" — one sentence, no visual needed.
- **Single resolution:** F = mg and a = F/m combine so mass cancels; a = g regardless of mass.
- **One visual object:** the falling pair (ball + feather) on parallel guide lines — the whole video orbits this one image.
- **≤6 elements per scene:** confirmed per beat (B00: 2 objects + 2 labels; B02: 2 objects + 2 arrows + 2 labels — at the edge, kept because the arrows are the beat's entire point; B03: equation + 2 derivations, objects hidden).
- **Self-contained:** understandable without the chapter — force, mass, and acceleration are assumed-known prerequisites only.

## Exclusions honored
No air resistance / terminal velocity, no derivation of g from universal
gravitation, no numerical kinematics problems. (Per storyboard.)
