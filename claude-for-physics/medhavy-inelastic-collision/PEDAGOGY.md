# PEDAGOGY.md — inelastic-collision (Chapter 9, Types of Collisions)

## Concept
In a perfectly inelastic collision (objects stick), total momentum is
conserved but kinetic energy is not; the loss of kinetic energy is the
maximum possible, and it goes into deformation, heat and sound.

## Physics check (against the textbook, 9.4) — every number recomputed
- m₁ = m₂ = 2 kg, v₁ = 4 m/s, v₂ = 0. Momentum: p = 2·4 = 8 kg·m/s.
- v_f = p / (m₁ + m₂) = 8 / 4 = 2 m/s. After: 4·2 = 8 kg·m/s (conserved).
- K before = ½·2·4² = 16 J. K after = ½·4·2² = 8 J. Half is lost.
- **Storyboard error corrected:** the storyboard showed KE after = 6 J; the
  correct value is 8 J. Bars, counter and narration all use 8 J.
- Textbook: "Any collision where the objects stick together will result in
  the maximum loss of kinetic energy"; "perfectly inelastic"; energy goes to
  "deformation ... and some heat and sound" (Example 9.x). B03/B04 match.
- B04's "free of external forces" is the textbook's closed-system condition
  for momentum conservation.
