### B01 — Stakes: The Decorative Approval
**Move**: 1 Stakes First  
**Visual**: Manim visual of an automated validation report generated instantly with an authoritative layout and a decorative digital signature line: "System Validated: 100% Passed. Ready for Deployment." Floating weightlessly in mid-air.  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> Ask a language model to validate an AI deployment, and it produces an immaculate document. It cites internal benchmarks, declares guardrails operational, and appends a formal approval. Management archives the document. But when the system crashes in production or fails a vulnerable user, who signed? The artifact has the visual shape of accountability without a single gram of substance.

---

### B02 — The Wrong Guess: Better Templates Fix It
**Move**: 2 Wrong Guess & Falsification  
**Visual**: Manim illustration showing an engineer adding detailed compliance templates and prompting the model with longer checklists. Struck out in terracotta: prompt templates cannot bear liability.  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> The standard reaction is to blame the prompt. Teams assume the model simply needs stricter compliance templates or more exhaustive safety questions. But prompt engineering cannot manufacture accountability. If the model is wrong, the prompt cannot be demoted, sued, or reprimanded. A model doesn't lose its job.

---

### B03 — Mechanism: The Nature of a Signature
**Move**: 3 Epistemic Mechanism (Part 1: The Attestation)  
**Visual**: Manim comparative diagram: Decorative Sign-Off (asserts completion, claims perfection, zero stakes) contrasted with an Honest Attestation (documents edges, declares limits, answerable human).  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> A signature is not decorative formatting; it is a personal commitment by an agent with stakes. When an organization substitutes an automated sign-off for human review, it confuses generating text with accepting liability. The honest artifact is not an automated sign-off that asserts validation, but an attestation that documents its own boundaries.

---

### B04 — Anchor Planted: The First Two Blocks
**Move**: 4 Anchor Planted (`morph` Part 1)  
**Visual**: The floating, hollow sign-off box begins to morph. Two heavy foundation blocks rise beneath it: Block 1 ("WHAT WAS TESTED") and Block 2 ("WHAT WAS NOT TESTED").  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> An honest attestation begins with what was tested: specific checks, exact acceptance thresholds, and verified results. But its load-bearing core is the second section: what was NOT tested. The untested distributions, the missing stress tests, the failure modes known to exist but unprobed.

---

### B05 — Anchor Move: The Unwritten Gap
**Move**: 3 Epistemic Mechanism (Part 2: Why AI Cannot Write Negation)  
**Visual**: Manim graphic zooming into Block 2 ("WHAT WAS NOT TESTED") highlighted in terracotta. A boundary demonstrates that an AI can only summarize its own prompts; it cannot know what it never encountered.  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> This is the section an AI can never write. A model can summarize the tests it was instructed to execute, but it cannot know what it did not do. When a report lists only passed tests, it reads like a clean bill of health. And a clean bill of health is where catastrophic blind spots hide.

---

### B06 — Anchor Payoff: The Complete Structure
**Move**: 4 Anchor Paid Off (`morph` Part 2)  
**Visual**: The morph completes. Two final structural blocks snap into place beneath the foundation: Block 3 ("NAMED HUMAN PER GATE") and Block 4 ("CALIBRATED VERBS"). The signature line locks securely into the foundation with substantial weight.  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> Two final blocks complete the structure. Third: a named human per decision. Not "the machine learning team," but an actual person who can be asked, and if necessary, answer for the call. Fourth: calibrated verbs. "Passed" means passed a documented stress test; "believe safe" admits an unverified hypothesis.

---

### B07 — One Flag: The Organizational Friction
**Move**: One Inference Flag  
**Visual**: Manim diagram illustrating the trade-off: Velocity vs Legible Answerability. Friction is highlighted as the core protective mechanism.  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> Here is the inference to watch. Writing what was not tested introduces friction that organizations actively resist. Declaring unverified edge cases exposes uncomfortable vulnerabilities before deployment. But that friction is precisely the point: it forces teams to acknowledge the boundary between verified performance and blind hope.

---

### B08 — Limits & Both Directions
**Move**: 5 Both Directions  
**Visual**: Split card: Direction A ("Attestation Alone: Documents limits, but does not fix untested failures") vs Direction B ("Clean Sign-Off: Masks limits, guaranteeing blind operational failure").  
**Narration (Liam)**:
> Both directions matter. An honest attestation does not make an AI system safe; documenting that you skipped edge cases does not prevent those cases from failing. But an automated sign-off that omits what was not tested guarantees disaster, transforming inevitable uncertainty into dangerous organizational illusion.
