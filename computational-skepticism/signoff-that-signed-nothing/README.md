# The Sign-Off That Signed Nothing

Ask an AI to write your validation sign-off and you get a perfect one in one second: a name, a review, an approval — and every word of it is empty.

An AI can generate the shape of accountability, but not the substance. A signature is not text formatting; it is a commitment by someone with stakes. A model doesn't lose its job, face disciplinary review, or answer to users when a system fails.

In this episode of *Computational Skepticism for AI*, Liam (in for Professor Bear) unpacks the structural difference between decorative sign-offs and honest attestations. The true load-bearing section of any validation artifact isn't what passed — it is what was never tested. And that is the one section a model can never write for you, because it cannot know what it didn't do.

---

### Key Takeaways & Carry-Out
- **The Shape of Accountability vs. The Substance**: LLMs produce fluent approval memos, but fluent syntax is not accountability. Real sign-offs require someone who has something to lose.
- **The Four-Block Attestation Architecture**:
  1. *Tested*: Concrete scope, specific environments, explicit metrics.
  2. *NOT Tested*: The critical negative space — edge cases, unexamined sub-populations, distribution shifts.
  3. *Named Human*: The specific engineer answerable for deployment, not a model or team handle.
  4. *Calibrated Verbs*: Precise claims ("tested against test suite B") instead of expansive declarations ("certified safe").
- **The Negative Space Principle**: What was NOT tested is where silent deployment failures originate.
- **Carry-Out Law**: *"The load-bearing section of an attestation is what was not tested."*
- **Direction A (Fluent Approval ⇏ Rigorous Testing)**: A flawless validation summary generated by an AI guarantees nothing about underlying system safety.
- **Direction B (Model Fault ⇏ Model Liability)**: When an AI-signed system crashes in production, liability does not adhere to the model; answerability belongs entirely to the human who accepted the empty signature.
